Register-Rooted Unate Synthesis
for DOMINO and CMOS

Bounded functional decomposition and shared-cost optimization at sequential boundaries

1Motivation and Contribution

A DOMINO gate [1] evaluates a function that is monotone in its supplied rails through a series-parallel pull-down network; a DOMINO latch additionally holds the value as the cycle's state. Absorbing a register's next-state logic into one or two such stages creates an opportunity that ordinary combinational covering does not explicitly target. Output-phase assignment and DOMINO mapping [2, 3], and earlier unate decomposition [12, 13], provide the foundations. Here the synthesis objective is the complete mixed static/DOMINO implementation of each next-state cone, including the logic it shares with other consumers.

The method combines three decisions: where to place an endpoint's functional boundary, how to realize its exact function under DOMINO constraints, and which shared static logic must remain. Exact bounded truth tables let the search move beyond the XAG's current factoring; divisor-image care sets expose alternative endpoint functions; coordinated endpoint selection captures gains unavailable to independent choices. Residual optimization feeds changed interfaces back into selection. Literal-network extraction further explores using stable controls to simplify the function evaluated in the dynamic phase (Section 6). These contributions concern the organization and target objective of the search; cut enumeration, unate covers and resubstitution build on established methods.

The current realization, pass.usyn, evaluates this logical selection by expanding it into static CMOS and technology-mapping it. Physical DOMINO cells and characterization remain future work; transistor cost and stage count therefore express logical objectives, not measured DOMINO area or timing. Fig. 1 and Table I contrast the method with the LiveHD ABC flow. Shared infrastructure is described in synthesis.html (cited as [S §n]); implementation options are documented in the USYN reference.

2Regions and the Logical Network

The shared region construction [S §2, §3] uses a flop-to-flop profile to keep each register's next-state cone together. Logical translation preserves every original register bit and pipeline stage independently of library cell selection. Reset, clock and initialization semantics are protected; memories, clock gates, latches and opaque instances remain native barriers. Each region supplies an XAG, a structurally hashed AND/XOR graph with complemented edges.

The XAG supplies dependencies and reusable signals; a selected boundary supplies the variables of an exact Boolean function. Moving that boundary can hide a complex subfunction behind one input, expose unateness or change which logic the endpoint absorbs. Inside an admitted window, synthesis uses the truth table rather than preserving the original AND/XOR factoring. Functional resubstitution can also use divisor sets that are not structural cuts, provided they determine the endpoint exactly. Structural bias thus remains in bounded window and divisor discovery, but does not constrain the subsequent realization to the original subgraph.

Definition 1 (Endpoint). An endpoint is a bit of a positive-edge register in a known clock domain whose next-state function Fq is computed inside the region. Its cone is the XAG transitive fan-in of Fq up to region inputs and register outputs. Negative-edge and unknown-edge registers are not endpoints; their cones stay static.

3Register-Rooted Endpoint Selection

Definition 2 (Cells). A DominoLatch computes Fq and stores it as the endpoint's state; a Domino cell is a phase-1 stage that feeds a DominoLatch; a Static cell is ordinary CMOS. A DOMINO formula is legal when it reads at most 8 logical inputs (x and ¬x count once), has a series stack of at most 4 and at most 10 factored parallel branches. The logical cell model supplies both output rails; complementary static inputs require a charged inverter unless already available. A function binate in a logical variable can therefore use both rails in a formula monotone in its rail inputs, without first making the entire network unate by output-phase assignment. This is a Boolean rail model; physical monotonicity and clocking require a characterized implementation.

Selection minimizes a transistor proxy, used consistently by every search and acceptance test:

cost = 2·#AND + 4·#XOR + #NOT (static)  +  Σcells ( |formula| + 5 [Domino] + 8 [DominoLatch] )(1)

where |formula| is the transistor count of the pull-down network and static logic and inverters shared between cells are counted once. Each region starts from an identity baseline: one DominoLatch per endpoint fed by its unchanged static cone, which is always legal. Every endpoint then searches, under its own work slice, for a cheaper realization (Algorithm 1), and keeps the baseline if the search fails or runs out of work.

Exactness boundary. For a fully specified unate function, normalizing its input polarities gives a positive monotone function whose minimal true points determine the unique irredundant monotone SOP cover. This cover is exact; minimizing the transistors of a factored series-parallel network is a separate problem. Factoring, signed covers for binate functions, boundary discovery and decomposition encoding are bounded searches. Every retained formula is checked exactly, but failure to find one does not establish infeasibility or optimality.

Algorithm 1 SELECTENDPOINT(q) — tiers in order, each bounded
  1. incumbent ← identity cell; admit a window: the whole cone or a reconvergence-driven window of ≤ 16 inputs
  2. one cell: the whole window in one DominoLatch; unate ⇒ exact minimal cover, else a bounded signed cover, series-parallel factored
  3. decompose F(B, R) = H(G0(B) … Gk(B), R): new phase-1 divisors Gi as Domino cells
  4. boundaries: enumerate cut frontiers (≤ 32) with contraction and growth moves; existing-divisor two-cell covers; wide-boundary round
  5. two-phase general tier; residual removal with care completion over ≤ 16 phases; witness-guided local divisors
  6. validate the best candidate exactly; commit iff its total cost (1) is lower

Care-aware divisor search. For divisors D(x) on one admitted independent-input basis, a top function exists exactly when D(x) = D(y) implies F(x) = F(y). Its care set is the image of D; unreachable combinations are free to complete. For a chosen input polarity, upward closure of the required true points yields the least monotone completion, or proves that polarity impossible if it reaches a required zero. The search tries bounded polarity and completion alternatives and checks their composition against F. A dependence conflict provides two assignments that a further divisor must separate, guiding search beyond structural cuts. These are local combinational don't-cares, not assumptions about reachable sequential states.

New functional divisors follow Ashenhurst–Curtis decomposition [4]. All functions and compositions are checked over truth tables of up to 16 inputs. Candidate cost includes the endpoint, any first-phase cells, the remaining static cones and demanded inverse rails. An unchanged outside consumer protects its cone from removal credit: absorbing a shared subfunction into one endpoint cannot make its still-needed static implementation disappear.

Coordinated endpoints. If two endpoints share a static cone, changing either alone may add DOMINO cost while leaving that cone live; changing both can remove it and become profitable. Pair and joint reselection therefore compare complete regional cost, including interface alternatives that lose individually. Shared gates reaching exactly two endpoints in one clock domain seed pair trials; joint search can also use shared source inputs. Bounded private fanout-free windows preserve outside readers, while common functional divisors and joint recoding explore shared first-phase implementations. Every affected endpoint must remain legal and exactly equivalent; only a strict total-cost gain commits. The search is procedural and bounded, without a global optimality claim.

4Residual Optimization

Logic not absorbed into cells is a shared multiple-output network: its boundaries include primary outputs, unselected next states, selected-cell inputs and protected control ports. Bounded rewriting [5], resubstitution [6] and balancing preserve these functions and use nonzero AND/XOR costs with depth guards. These are established combinational operators; their role here is to improve the residual network under the same regional objective as endpoint selection.

The region is replaced only if its total cost (1) does not rise. One feedback round then re-selects endpoints whose static inputs changed, because a better residual implementation can expose a different absorption boundary or alter sharing costs. Endpoint and residual synthesis are thus coupled through their interfaces before freezing, rather than treating selected cells as permanently fixed barriers. Native search uses exact bounded Boolean checks without a SAT or SMT solver; independent whole-design LEC remains a separate validation step.

5Freeze, CMOS Expansion and Cleanup

The selection is frozen into an endpoint netlist of Static, Domino and DominoLatch cells with their functions, formulas, rails, phases and source-state correspondence; identical phase-1 cells in one clock domain are shared, and the whole artifact is validated (functions, legality, shared cost, phase order, clock domains) or nothing is published. It is then expanded into behavioral CMOS: one native register per original bit and stage, each cell's formula as static logic, no phase-1 storage. Expansion is structural and checked exactly against every register's reference next state.

The expansion is cleaned on a private copy with the residual optimizer of Section 4 over all outputs and next states, now across the former cell boundaries. With multi_rep (on in the second trial, Section 7), windows of ≤ 8 inputs also receive alternative representations — positive and negative SOP, a reduced ordered BDD [7], a bounded disjoint-support decomposition and NPN4 templates — kept as structural choices [8] and extracted for area and for depth, in the spirit of mixed-representation synthesis [9, 10]. The result is exported as an Lnet and reconnected to packed ports and native memory, ICG, instance and loop boundaries.

6Literal-Network Extraction (experimental)

In a DOMINO pipeline a register's output is stable during the first half of the cycle, while DOMINO gates evaluate in the second. Logic controlled only by registered signals can therefore settle in the otherwise idle first half and choose the literals a DOMINO gate reads, as precomputation does for power [11]. This is a step between expansion and the final cleanup (pass.usyn.literal_extract=n, n ∈ {1, 2, 3}, default 0).

Definition 3 (Literal network). Let a selected cell compute F(c, r) with n controls c driven by register outputs or primary inputs. Shannon expansion gives F = ∨v [c = v] · Fv(r). A template T(s1…sm) realizes all cofactors if for every v there is a substitution λv mapping each slot to a literal of r, its complement or a constant with Fv = T ∘ λv. The literal network realizes each slot as a balanced tree of 2:1 multiplexers over c (depth n) selecting λv(sj).

The cell then implements T instead of F; any template costs at least maxv cost(Fv), which bounds the gain. For every cell with ≤ 8 inputs, every control subset of size n is tried; the template is a cofactor's own gate, and an exact bounded search with ternary-simulation pruning finds the substitutions. Every accepted rewrite is re-verified over all input assignments. In the DOMINO flow the DOMINO cells and their literal networks are kept and only the remaining logic is re-optimized; in the CMOS flow (implemented today) the rewritten next states are passed with everything else to cleanup and tmap. Only the cell function is rewritten (mode a, the default). Cofactoring upstream cones (mode b) is reserved for time-critical cones once placement feedback exists, and admitting previous-phase DOMINO outputs as controls is a place-and-route recovery option; both add DOMINO stages or area.

7Technology Mapping and Trials

CMOS expansion is followed by mapping only, so the backend does not replace native selection with another Boolean synthesis recipe. Proposed DOMINO boundaries impose no barriers on static mapping, while high-fanout static cones receive separate partitions to preserve sharing. The current ABC provider uses the mapping, sizing and boundary environment of [S §3.4, §4.2–4.4]; its Boolean-synthesis and architecture trials are disabled.

The static cost (1) approximates mapped area. Two bounded whole-design trials therefore compare the base selection with a choice-rich variant using light preselection cleanup and multiple representations. The winner has fewer mapped gates (weighted by instance count), then smaller area, then lower maximum region delay. This measures the consequence of native structural choices through a common mapping backend; it does not establish a characterized DOMINO benefit.

8Budgets, Reuse and Determinism

Structural work (preparation, baseline, validation and publication) and optional search have separate deterministic ledgers, each bounded by the configured work allowance. Search exhaustion preserves a complete legal incumbent; structural or process-resource refusal publishes no result. This distinguishes bounded exploration from evidence that an endpoint cannot be realized.

Logical decisions are reused independently of Liberty and mapping settings. Reuse also preserves a cold run's budget-dependent decisions: a search independent of its exact initial credits can replay at or above its recorded floor; a credit-bound search requires exactly the original credits. Hits replay recorded charges so later regions receive the same allowance. Mapping reuse follows [S §3.5]. These conditions support reproducible bounded experiments; a warm cache is not an additional search heuristic.

Scope of the evidence. Native function and composition checks certify local replacements; independent LEC tests the emitted design. CMOS gate-count results remain behind the ABC flow on several control-heavy designs, including arbiters and comparators. Physical DOMINO timing, wire-aware cost and placement-driven retiming remain open evaluation questions.

9Positioning Relative to Prior Work

Output-phase assignment [2] and DOMINO technology mapping [3] address polarity and covering; unate decomposition [12] and its application to dynamic CMOS [13] already establish that binate functions can be realized through unate blocks. Our distinction is the mandatory register-rooted endpoint objective, with mixed static/DOMINO cost and preserved source state. Exact unate covers and decomposition supply established foundations for this objective.

Priority cuts [14] bound structural boundary exploration. Here a retained boundary is judged by constrained functional realization and surviving shared logic, rather than support size alone. Care-aware decomposition has also been studied for LUT mapping [17]; the endpoint search applies divisor-image completions to static and first-phase interfaces under DOMINO legality and rail cost.

AnySyn [15] motivates evaluating transformations with the target cost rather than graph size. Multiple-node Boolean optimization [16] demonstrates the value of coordinated changes to shared logic. Our specialization couples these ideas to sequential endpoints: outside consumers protect live cones, interface alternatives are priced jointly, and common divisors must respect the endpoint clock domain and phase order. Residual rewriting [5, 6] and structural choices [8] remain conventional machinery. The proposed combination, and the literal-selection opportunity of Section 6, are the focus of the method.
