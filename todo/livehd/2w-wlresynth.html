2w-wlresynth Wirelength-aware cone resynthesis around virtual buffers and long nets

The experimental pass is available in lhd synth with --set synth.place.wl_resynth=true. The default is off. The placement work directory's wl_resynth/report.json contains the cone measurements, timed trials and selected edits. wl_resynth/history.jsonl records each decision across routability restarts.

On HighTide ASAP7 at 8296f7ffa, comparing the enabled and disabled flow with one binary, litedram used 132 µm less detailed-route wire and gained 0.90 ps of extracted setup slack. liteeth used 499 µm less and gained 9.17 ps. Both pairs routed with zero DRC errors. Their independent pre-CTS setup checks fell by 0.11 ps and 7.33 ps respectively. On NVDLA/partition_a the pass rejected all candidates, reproduced the baseline DEF, and increased cold synthesis time from about three minutes to more than six.

Repeating litedram on the integrated master 405a55af4 reversed the small gain. Routed wire increased from 98,680 to 98,814 µm and extracted setup slack fell from +69.98 to +64.00 ps. Both routes had zero DRC errors and the two placements passed equivalence over all 4,422 registers. In a single-thread Sky130HD sha3 run, two driver moves saved 1,854 µm of final pre-CTS HPWL but cost 249 ps of independently measured setup margin. These measurements are why the pass remains opt-in.

After a few timing-driven global-placement iterations, some nets show that the logic's fanout structure conflicts with the placement: one driver and many loads spread over the die (a virtual buffer root), or a long net with few pins (a repeater candidate). Today the answer is always buffers. This task extracts the cone of logic around such a net and lets USYN restructure it using the placed positions, so that copies of the driver logic sit near their load clusters. A buffer tree or a repeater stays as one option among the others, chosen where it wins.

Why

  • Routed wirelength is the remaining gap to OpenROAD. On HighTide asap7, LiveHD routes to 0 DRC with 1.12 to 1.34 times OpenROAD's routed wirelength on macro-free designs (2026-11-18, after virtual buffer trees). More wire means more detailed-route iterations and route time.
  • Existing resynthesis is timing only. synth.place.io_resynth remaps a whole color only when its placed slack is within 5% of the period and its boundary got tighter than synthesis assumed. It has no wirelength objective and skips stages that already meet timing.
  • Remapping for area does not help. Area-oriented mapping cut HPWL only 2 to 6% and cost timing (sha3 +209 to −86 ps). Cell and pin counts are already within about 7% of ORFS.
  • The excess wire sits in buffered nets. On NVDLA/partition_a the netlist-side excess is in 5 to 8 pin nets and nets over 64 pins driven by timing-repair buffers (BUFx16f and BUFx24 cells driving 65 to 138 spread loads). 3-pin nets driven by flops are 3 times longer than with OpenROAD's placer on the same netlist.

Decisions (2026-11-19)

#TopicDecision
W1Unit of workThe cone of logic around one target net, not the whole color.
W2TargetsVirtual buffer roots (high fanout) and long low-fanout nets (repeater candidates).
W3EngineUSYN's native XAG, not ABC logic optimization. ABC stays only as the technology-mapping provider for the rewritten cone, as it is for USYN today.
W4Cone extentThe forward and backward cone of the target net up to the flops, flops included, restricted to the part where there is a chance to optimize. Beyond that cone, look only a few extra combinational gate levels. No fixed gate-count bound.
W5ColorsA cone may cross colors.
W6FlopsFlops may be duplicated. All copies share a common name derived from the original register so semdiff collapses them. LEC either collapses the copies or structurally undoes the duplication before proving.
W7AreaThe per-cone growth limit is soft. The real constraint is the overall area of the die, so candidates from all cones compete for the free area, ranked by gain per area.
W8Growth budgetThe fixed 5% growth budget is a soft guide against search explosion. It may be replaced by a better method to decide buffer insertion, such as the ranking of W7.
W9Phase 1 gateNo stop gate. The measured upper bound only orders the search (which cones to try first).
W10OrderFix the rank-based timing-driven placement weights first (step 0), so cones are scored on a placement that has not stretched nets for no reason.

Steps

Step 0: slack-based placement weights

Today about half of all nets get a placement weight from 1 to 2 by slack percentile, even on a design that meets timing (NVDLA/partition_a: about 29k of 58.6k nets, design finishes at +354 ps). With timing-driven placement off, liteeth HPWL drops 11% with better WNS and NVDLA/partition_a drops 7% at equal WNS, but minimax, sha3 and litepci lose timing. Weight a net by its slack relative to the clock period instead: weight 1 for comfortable slack, rising smoothly as slack approaches zero, saturating for negative slack. Code: data_timing_weights in pass/place/timing_place.cpp and the post-synthesis seed in lhd/lhd_kernel_synth.cpp.

Step 1: measure

  • For each target net on litepci, NVDLA/partition_a, sha3 and minimax: fan-in cone size, load count, load spread, loads per geometric cluster.
  • Estimate the upper bound: HPWL if each load cluster had its own copy of the driver, against the buffer tree or repeaters built today.
  • Use the bound to order the search (W9).

Step 2: cone extraction and placement feedback

  • Build the cone as a small graph with placed positions on its boundary pins: inputs with arrival times, loads with required times and coordinates.
  • Cluster the loads by position, reusing the spatial clustering of the buffer-tree planner (plan_buffer_choices in pass/place/repair.cpp).

Step 3: restructuring options, scored per cone

  • Duplicate the driver. One copy of the root gate, flop or small fan-in cone per load cluster, each fed by the original inputs. One long high-fanout net becomes several short ones, at the cost of more load on the cone's inputs.
  • Re-decompose. When the root is a wide AND, OR, XOR or mux, split it so partial terms are computed near the inputs they depend on and only the final stage sits near each load cluster.
  • Push through. When all loads apply the same next-stage function with different side inputs, move that function before the fanout point.
  • Move the driver. For a long low-fanout net, move or duplicate the driver gate toward its loads.
  • Buffer tree or repeaters. Today's expansion, kept as the baseline option.

Step 4: cost model and accept rule

  • Score each option on values measured on the placed design: HPWL of the affected nets, worst slack through the cone, slew and capacitance limits, and area.
  • Rank all candidates of all cones by gain per area and fill the die's free area in that order (W7, W8). No setup slack may be lost. Ties go to fewer cells.

Step 5: integration

  • Run after the first timing-driven global-placement iterations and before expand_virtual_buffers. Targets the pass resolves never reach buffer expansion.
  • Place new cells at their cluster centroids, then ECO-legalize, as buffer expansion does.
  • Prove each rewritten cone equivalent to the original and reject it on failure.
  • Only the private physical copy changes: logical definitions and mapper cache keys stay as they are. The pass is deterministic and part of the placement cache key, so warm runs stay byte-identical.
  • Clock and reset cones and design-instantiated cells (attrs::user_cell) are never touched.

Step 6: verification

  • Unit tests per option on hand-built cones, and an unbounded LEC of a whole design before and after, including duplicated flops (W6).
  • HighTide pre-CTS and post-route on minimax, sha3, litedram, liteeth, litepci and NVDLA/partition_a, as same-binary on/off pairs. Targets: routed wirelength down without setup loss, 0 DRC kept, fewer inserted buffers.

Risks

  • Input loading. Duplicating a driver adds load on its inputs, which can move the high-fanout problem one level back. The cost model must include the fan-in nets.
  • Small upper bound. Cell and pin counts are already close to ORFS, so step 1 has to show where restructuring can beat good buffering.
  • Runtime. Thousands of cones per design. W4 bounds each cone by structure, and the ranking of W7 bounds how many are applied, but the number tried also needs a limit.
  • Hold. Duplicated flops and shorter paths change hold margins after CTS; check the ORFS hold-buffer counts in the post-route runs.
