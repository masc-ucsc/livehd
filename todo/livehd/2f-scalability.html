2f-scalability Placement that fits one chunk at a time

A chunk budget applies to the downstream worker. The color floorplanner, the current flat exporter and a whole-design timing or routing run have different memory footprints. See the working flow and replay commands and the clean-run checks.

Eyeriss worker experiments

HighTide ASAP7 Eyeriss, top TOP, has 275,073 standard cells and 1,040 SRAM banks in this mapping. The first export with a strict 1,907 MiB budget made two homes. We compared four homes following the repeated cluster groups, automatic hierarchy and connectivity cuts, alternative rectangles, a central controller home, and tighter worker budgets, covering three through seven homes. All placement after export ran on individual homes. Three- and seven-home exports peaked at 2.53 and 2.49 GB for the producer process tree. The 2 GB worker budget therefore needs a separate producer budget today. A deliberately understated estimate at a 128 MiB test limit reached 184 MB before the supervisor stopped the worker. A retry at the normal budget reproduced its original handoff. Use a host-enforced limit or measured admission headroom for a hard RAM ceiling.

  1. Partition whole repeated groups first, then refine. At 120 macro and 300 final iterations, keeping the four groups together cut 1,890 signal nets including die IO, versus 2,249 for hierarchy packing and 4,365 for greedy connectivity packing. Assembled HPWL was 5.874, 5.912 and 11.006 million µm respectively. Score boundary capacity, physical pin spans, and shape along with memory. Screen candidate macro moves independently and score their exact assembled coordinates before promotion. After per-home macro, density and iteration trials, the two finalists reached 4.759 and 4.778 million µm HPWL. Both passed placement legality and routed all nets with zero global overflow. The second used 3,671 µm less routed wire and improved global-route-RC WNS by 1.68 ns.
  2. Dispatch versioned packets to available workers. Four final jobs copied into separate directories reproduced the serial handoff checksums using three worker slots. The two serial runs took 35.7 and 36.5 seconds, the parallel runs 18.4 seconds each. The three worker process trees together peaked at 0.70 GB in a separate simultaneous sample. Six smaller chunks finished the same 300-step placement wave in 13.3 seconds with three workers but increased assembled HPWL by 17% relative to the four intact groups. Include both wall time and assembled quality when choosing a partition. Extend the project coordinator with a short dispatch transaction and a short result transaction. Run the solver outside the project write lock. Identify a result by design revision, packet and engine digests, placement seed, and incident interface revisions. Accept disjoint results independently, reject obsolete results, and retry a failed job from the same packet. Track transfer bytes, per-process peak RSS, queue time, and the slowest job.
  3. Let the coordinator own boundary slots. In one four-group frame, 586 two-home signal nets shared the same proposed point on a 43.146 µm edge. One 0.24 µm lane per signal would need 140.64 µm. Even when both workers favored sliding a contact, re-placement could increase whole-design HPWL. Ordering 1,744 contacts into shared slots and re-solving for 1,500 iterations improved HPWL by 48,056 µm and placement-RC WNS by 1.26 ns relative to an equally advanced frozen-contact run. Have workers return preferred intervals, demand by layer and timing sensitivity. Assign compatible slots once per interface epoch, send exactly those slots to every incident home, and compare the round with an equally advanced frozen-contact control. Include multi-home trunks and fixed die ports.
  4. Route the color and macro terminals before legal placement. An offline directional pattern-route experiment used 128 lower-layer track pitches per floorplan tile and 32 per macro tile. It included SRAM layer obstructions and reserved capacity using local cell density. Across three four-home partitions, correlation with independent FastRoute crossing-net wirelength maps was 0.64–0.73 at the floorplan and 0.82–0.93 after macro placement. The existing contact-span map gave 0.15–0.54. Expose directional demand, available tracks, internal reservation, and overflow separately. In the all-net reference, floorplan density alone correlated only 0.10 with internal wire demand. Under a tighter track budget, the resulting floorplan overflow flags covered less than 1% of the reported third-iteration marker weight. Adding per-net internal demand from 96 and 120 local macro iterations brought those figures to 46% and 51% in the highest-ranked tenth of the map. The four-home placement waves took about 2.6 seconds each. Workers can return sparse directional tile demand and incident terminals. The coordinator can then update affected tiles and crossing routes without reading full worker placements. Test false negatives on a held-out design before using the map to reject a floorplan.

Keep independent timing measurements for finalists. The live WNS estimate favored automatic hierarchy packing over four intact groups (−32.85 versus −40.80 ns). OpenSTA with placement RC and the same 2.5 ns clock favored the intact groups (−19.03 versus −21.86 ns for hierarchy packing). Exchange arrival and load at cut pins, track the critical path family, and test candidate deltas against this reference. On a second four-group geometry, coordinating just five address signals across all four homes improved placement-RC WNS by 4.45 ns against a matched 850-iteration control. The live WNS estimate returned the same value for both. Coarse and full-grid routing are validation stages with their own memory budgets.

Independent routing workers retain complete interior nets and the coordinator verifies the assembled DEF. The resident route agent reports failed pin coordinates. On P, moving six implicated SRAMs and reopening their incident nets repaired all six named signals while preserving the rest of the partial route. Each worker still loads a full DEF. Geometry-limited worker inputs and general multi-pin crossing contracts remain open. The route-first measurements give the matched full-chip results.

The floorplan editor now exchanges two equal, site- and row-snapped tabs across a shared edge. Both workers receive complementary row intervals and the same boundary segments. Remaining work

  1. Export without a flat physical design. Read cells, pins, obstructions and boundary summaries by home from mapped storage. Today a selected worker can load only its packet, but the producer still builds the flat view. Measure producer and worker RSS separately on a design too large to place flat; a large synthetic color graph tests the planner only. Profile the remaining graph, net and name copies before choosing the next memory optimization.
  2. Reuse and stitch physical implementations. An interface match helps choose how pins face; it does not prove interchangeable netlists, rows, rails or SRAM pin access. Carry logical instance and pin identity through inlining and extract arbitrary colored chunks without requiring separately supplied RTL. The worker frame now stores macro orientation and stitches each owned physical instance once. Check substitutability of independently produced implementations across equivalent logical instances, including masters, fixed obstacles, row phase and required pins. Acceptance: reproduce a representative in another instance from saved collateral; check the stitched DEF in OpenROAD, then compare crossing-net wirelength and routed DRC against independent placements.
  3. Bound timing and negotiate edges. Exchange arrival/required time, exterior load and RC at cut pins. Compare local deltas with a flat reference, including multi-clock and reconvergent paths. Shared-slot contact moves and stale-result checks now work for incident homes. Next, test moving cells between neighbors and permitting crossing routes to use an intermediate home. Charge overlap capacity once, preserve unique cell ownership and stop or roll back a round that oscillates. Measure convergence on a pair and a ring under simultaneous IO proposals. A host-level physical memory controller remains to be tested across multiple Linux machines.
  4. Calibrate on unfamiliar designs. Challenge the worker RSS envelope at 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 GB across technologies, sparse and macro-heavy homes, and detailed placement. Separate a completed peak from a killed worker’s lower bound. Check simultaneous producer and worker memory on a constrained machine; polling an individual RSS is not a hard machine limit.
  5. Incremental replacement and precision. Using the verified stitching path, remap one edited color and show which home and neighbor results really need invalidation. Compare with a clean run. For the proposed FP32 solver, benchmark local normalized coordinates first; retain exact integer legal poses and verify timing, wirelength and memory before changing default precision.
