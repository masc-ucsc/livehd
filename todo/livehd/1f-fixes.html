Relation to 2t-timeclosure

2t-timeclosure is the opt-in redesign of the GP timing loop (synth.place.timeclosure; CNN and litepci from OpenROAD's preCTS netlist, no resynthesis): a cheap in-memory late-mode proxy, incremental wire and timing updates, a stability-gated phase controller and phase-7 buffering/sizing. Its premise — no external timer in the loop, speed from incremental work and a cheap timer — is exactly what the measurements below show the default flow lacks. Division of work: 1f fixes the default path every lhd synth run takes today (legacy checkpoint / repair / rounds loop, Liberty rescans, convergence veto), reusing 2t's landed foundations where they fit; 2t keeps building the replacement loop and must not inherit the behaviors measured here. Problems to address

  • A — Hundreds of full-design STA evaluations where OpenROAD does two.
    • Observed: 446 evaluations on minimax (319 inside GP, 94 in the rounds, 28 in resize/repair), 76 on lfsr. Each evaluation on minimax costs 14 s; OpenSTA scores the whole placed design from files in 6.3 s.
    • Where: final-GP checkpoint every synth.place.macro.timing_interval=50 iterations from final.timing_start=200 plus a forced final one (pass/place/place.cpp:6503-6622). Each checkpoint runs the STA once, then repair_gp_buffers (repair.cpp:1815) runs up to resize.gp_trials=4 trial batches per phase for two phases (insert, remove), each scored by a full evaluate (repair.cpp:1995): about 6 evaluations per checkpoint, 52 checkpoints. The rounds (timing_place.cpp:149-205) add GP checkpoints plus one evaluation per candidate; the legacy resize and the repair pass evaluate every attempt (repair.cpp:2382, 2556; synth.place.resize.engine=legacy is the default, lhd/lhd_kernel_synth.cpp:644).
    • What has to change: checkpoint on density milestones (OpenROAD evaluates at overflow ≈ 0.6 and ≈ 0.2 and at the end), never on an iteration clock; score trial edits with the existing what-if timer (Physical_session::Proxy, physical_session.cpp:877; the new resize engine already requires it, resize_engine.cpp:2308-2330) instead of a full re-evaluation; one evaluation per outer stage. Budget: ≤ 8 full evaluations for the whole flow on any design. Reuse the cheap-sampling / decision-checkpoint split of 2t H.
    • Acceptance: pass.opentimer.incremental count ≤ 8 on lfsr and minimax with WNS within 5 ps of today's B result.
  • B — An “incremental” evaluation re-times the whole design and re-fits every driver.
    • Observed: 14 s per evaluation on 18 k cells; every evaluation logs OpenTimer's rebase spef resistance; 74 % of the repair pass is RcDriver::crossing / RcRamp::crossing / rc_fit_ramp / expm1.
    • Where: Pass_opentimer::apply_physical_rc (pass/opentimer/physical_rc.cpp:75-179) compares each net's RC tree with the previous evaluation and re-attaches every net that differs. During GP every cell moves, so every net differs and timer.read_parasitics puts every net root on the frontier (OpenTimer ot/timer/spef.cpp:40-50) → full forward/backward propagation. Each propagated net arc then goes through the fork's iterative effective-capacitance waveform fit (ot/timer RC driver, the 27c3982 “crossing bracket” work) — the dominant per-net cost. Around it, every evaluation also rebuilds string-keyed maps over all nodes (validate_physical_rc, physical_rc.cpp:11-50; Impl::update, physical_session.cpp:150-272) and fills Feedback.pins, a std::map<std::string, Pin> with seven optionals per pin for every pin (opentimer.cpp:2194-2700), which timing_weights then looks up by string per net (timing_place.cpp:14-58).
    • What has to change: (1) only nets whose RC changed beyond a tolerance are re-attached, and the GP checkpoint should move only the critical fraction's trees (or evaluate on a coarse star/Elmore model and keep the waveform fit for the final STA); (2) a closed-form driver model (Elmore + Dartu-style ramp, as OpenSTA uses) for in-loop evaluations, with the iterative fit reserved for sign-off; (3) index-based pin/net identity between the placer and the timer — no string maps on the per-evaluation path. Measure with the existing Physical_session::Statistics buckets (pass_opentimer.hpp:246-254), which already split validation / reconcile / propagation / feedback time but are not reported in result.json. The native proxy of 2t B and the rc_cache of 2t C are the landed building blocks.
    • Acceptance: a GP-checkpoint evaluation on minimax ≤ 1 s; the final evaluation ≤ 3 s; total STA time ≤ 10 % of the placer on every design.
  • C — The 45 MB Liberty text is re-read and re-scanned at every checkpoint.
    • Observed: lfsr spends 41 % of 90 s in read_floorplan_library and 8 % in prects_library; 34 s of kernel time is file reading; 15 % is liberty::read_files.
    • Where: read_floorplan_library (repair.cpp:1533-1539) is called from every repair_gp_buffers (repair.cpp:1824) and from the resize/repair entry points (repair.cpp:1639, 1643); it calls read_lef, inventory → scan_comb_cells, scan_special_pins (five scans: dff, async dff, latch, icg, memory) and scan_output_drives. Every liberty::scan_* re-reads the files (pass/liberty/liberty_dff.cpp:144) and re-scans the whole text (scan_cells, liberty_dff.cpp:1056). refresh_timing_weights re-parses the LEFs and prects_library (six more scans) on every weight refresh (timing_place.cpp:60-67); resize_engine.cpp:74-76 and repair.cpp:2214-2215 do it again per pass; environment_key re-hashes the Liberty text (place.cpp:354-372). None of this depends on the placement.
    • What has to change: parse Liberty and LEF once per run into one shared technology object (cells, drives, special pins, control roles, truth tables) owned by the placement session and passed to repair, resize, weights and io-timing. Scans of a 45 MB file must not be on any per-checkpoint or per-attempt path.
    • Acceptance: lfsr placer ≤ 5 s CPU; liberty::scan_* and read_lef appear at most once each in a call-graph profile.
  • D — The bare global placement is far from OpenROAD's wirelength and timing.
    • Observed: pure GP on LiveHD's own floorplan: HPWL +32 % vs A and WNS −232 ps where OpenROAD on the same LiveHD netlist (D) gets −9 % HPWL and +82 ps. On OpenROAD's floorplan (flow C input, identical netlist, IO and taps) the pure GP gives 3.3× OpenROAD's HPWL. OpenROAD's repair_design on the ablation-1 placement finds only 4 slew violations and does not recover timing: the loss is the placement itself, not missing buffers.
    • Where to look first: (1) initial positions are uniform random over the core (DREAMPlace dreamplace/native/placement.cpp:1631-1632): initial HPWL 652 k µm (ablation 1) and 868 k µm (ablation 2) versus OpenROAD's conjugate-gradient initial placement at 17 k µm; Nesterov then has to contract wires against the density force and settles in a worse basin. (2) Density ramp: overflow is still 0.475 at iteration 500 and reaches 0.1 at 1 060, OpenROAD reaches 0.19 at 428 (initial_density_weight=0.01 and the multiplier schedule). (3) Bins are fixed at 64×64 regardless of die and cell size (synth.place.bins=64, dynamic_bins=false; place.cpp:5441, lhd_kernel_synth.cpp:456-457): on lfsr a bin is 0.09 µm against a 0.27 µm row, the overflow floor is 0.28–0.40 and stop_overflow=0.1 is unreachable; OpenROAD derives 8×8 (lfsr) and 64×64 (minimax) from average cell area over target density. The bins=8 ablation made lfsr's main GP converge. (4) On the imported floorplan: IO terminals, the 4 µm core margin and the density target 0.7 below the 0.71 utilization (OpenROAD raised its target to 0.72) must be checked one at a time; fixed-cell repulsion is already excluded (ablation 2b).
    • What has to change: a quadratic/center initial placement; bins from cell area and target density; a density schedule that reaches the target in a few hundred iterations; a per-iteration HPWL/overflow trace next to OpenROAD's for the same netlist and floorplan (3_3_place_gp.log is the reference) until the two curves agree within 10 % at equal overflow.
    • Acceptance: pure GP + legalization on the flow-C input within +10 % of OpenROAD's 45 743 µm GP wirelength; on LiveHD's own floorplan within +10 % of flow D's 43 333 µm; both in ≤ 30 s.
  • E — Global placement is not allowed to converge.
    • Observed: every GP run in B and C stopped at its iteration cap (WARNING: native DREAMPlace GP stopped at 1500/500 iterations … objective settled false), 9 times on minimax, 4 on lfsr; with the timing machinery off the same GP converges at iteration 1 060.
    • Where: with post_density_factor=4 (default, lhd_kernel_synth.cpp:339) the objective counts as converged only if convergence.timing_satisfied, i.e. the last checkpoint reported timing_violation == 0 (placement.cpp:3531-3536, 4033-4040); the checkpoint sets it from the GP-time WNS (place.cpp:6627-6629). On an unlegalized, half-spread placement with Steiner RC the WNS is negative (“last timing satisfied false” on every minimax run), so convergence is vetoed until the cap. The continuation deadline is 5× the iteration at which density first settled (placement.cpp:3944-3950) — with density settling near 300 that is exactly the 1 500 cap. Every weight refresh (smoothing 0.5, up to 5× on 20 % of nets, every 50 iterations) and every accepted GP buffer insertion replaces the design and restarts Nesterov momentum (placement.hpp:196, 372), so the objective never settles either.
    • What has to change: convergence = overflow ≤ target (plus routability if enabled); timing may re-weight nets but must never veto convergence; weight refreshes without momentum restart and only at density milestones; continuation bounded to a small fraction (≤ 25 %) of the density-settling iteration; the iteration cap warning should be an exceptional outcome, not the normal one. 2t H states the same rule for the new controller.
    • Acceptance: no cap warning on any benchmark design; GP iterations ≤ 600 on minimax.
  • F — The timing-driven rounds cold-restart placement and are accepted on timing alone.
    • Observed: minimax HPWL 94 954 µm entering the rounds → 107 230 µm after; the three round GPs stop at 500 iterations with overflow 0.41–0.46, and legalizing a placement 4× over the density target is what inflates the wirelength. lfsr: 33 s (38 % of the run) for one accepted round that improved the internal path delay by 0.5 %.
    • Where: timing_place (pass/place/timing_place.cpp:149-205) erases every non-FIXED component from the floorplan (line 172) and runs a complete place() again with iterations / rounds = 500 (line 168, 192) — a cold GP that cannot converge in that budget — then keeps the candidate if timing_improves (line 196), which is retain(): “no timing or electrical metric got worse” (repair.cpp:1271); HPWL, area and power are never consulted.
    • What has to change: rounds off by default when the first STA shows positive WNS; when on, warm start from the converged placement with the density weight preserved, a short refinement budget, and an acceptance test that bounds HPWL and area growth. OpenROAD's model — re-weight inside the same Nesterov run with a virtual repair, twice — is the reference, and the same-generation, density-compatible acceptance of 2t D is the model.
    • Acceptance: HPWL after the physical stage ≤ +3 % of the pure-GP HPWL on lfsr and minimax.
  • G — The internal timing target is inconsistent with the SDC and with OpenSTA.
    • Observed: ABC targets 0.4 × the SDC period (synth-target-unreachable: timing target 360 ps … achieved 803 ps on minimax, 280 vs 665 ps on lfsr); io_resynth reports color slacks of −581 ps and −199 ps while OpenSTA measures +88 ps on the same final placement; GP checkpoints report violations that E turns into non-convergence. Flow D proves the netlist meets the clock with margin in OpenROAD. In ablation 1b io_resynth reports two accepted remaps and a restarted stage, but the final netlist and placement are byte-identical to the run with io_resynth=0 — the accepted result is either dropped or never applied when GP checkpoints are off.
    • What has to change: one timing target for the physical stage, equal to the SDC period, used by weights, convergence, resynthesis triggers and repair; the 0.4× budget stays a synthesis-internal hint and must not leak into placement decisions; GP-time timing feedback only once the placement is near-legal (overflow ≤ 0.2); io_resynth must be verified to apply what it accepts, and a restart of the whole final stage (minimax: +1 500 iterations and +131 evaluations = 1 786 s) needs a measured benefit before it is automatic. See 2t D (one period objective) and 2t K (restart interaction of the legacy io_resynth policy).
    • Acceptance: internal WNS within 10 % of score.tcl on the final placement at every checkpoint that feeds a decision; no “unreachable target” for a design that meets its SDC in OpenROAD.
  • H — Physical repair adds cells and area without a budget.
    • Observed: B has 18 283 cells against 17 115 for the same LiveHD netlist repaired by OpenROAD (D): +1 168 cells, +100 µm² (+4.9 %), power 7.43e-3 vs 6.09e-3 mW. OpenROAD's repair_design on minimax inserts one buffer and the GP-time virtual repair nets −0.7 % area; LiveHD's GP buffer repair, resize and repair run on a negative internal slack (G) and so keep inserting.
    • Where: repair_gp_buffers (repair.cpp:1815-2080) at every checkpoint; resize_placed / repair pass after the rounds (resize_engine.cpp, repair.cpp:2148-2560).
    • What has to change: electrical repair (slew, cap, fanout) once after GP; setup repair only against the SDC target and only on legal geometry; an explicit area/cell growth budget reported in placement.json. 2t J: no sizing or buffering through a legacy hook before phase 7.
    • Acceptance: cells ≤ +3 % and area ≤ +3 % over the synthesized netlist on minimax, as in flow D.
  • I — Scaling to the 230–650 k-cell designs (issue 6).
    • Observed: one evaluation grew 440× (32 ms → 14 s) for 137× the cells between lfsr and minimax; at cnn/gemmini size a single evaluation would take minutes and the 3 h budget is gone before the first GP ends. GP iteration cost is fine (≈ 9 ms per iteration at 15 k cells, 9.5 s total) but 4 500 iterations at 645 k cells is still hours. Peak RSS is 1.8 GB at 18 k cells (OpenROAD: 458 MB in GP), i.e. ≈ 100 kB per cell — the 64 GB cap is hit near 600 k cells. Per-checkpoint Design snapshots (placement.cpp:3436), per-checkpoint JSON traces and gp_buffer_repair_<iter>.json files, and the string-keyed maps of B all scale with the design.
    • What has to change: everything on the per-checkpoint path must be O(changed), not O(design) (A, B, C); memory per cell measured and bounded; the iteration budget follows from convergence (E). Re-run cnn, gemmini and partition_c with run_design.py … --flows SB once A–E land.
    • Acceptance: each finishes in < 1 h and < 16 GB; CPU ≤ OpenROAD's for the same stage.
  • J — Hard failures that block the measurement of macro designs (issue 5, out of this plan's speed scope).
    • lhd synth: eyeriss front-end (undeclared identifier 'psum_acc_times', elaborates in yosys-slang); NyuziProcessor and bp_uno unmapped physical object: flop after ABC.
    • physical_repair: floorplan requires contiguous equal-width N/FS rows for every design whose rows OpenROAD cut around macros — the flow-C comparison is impossible on cnn, partition_c, NyuziProcessor, bp_uno, eyeriss until imported row fragments are supported.   5. B, G, H: evaluation cost and repair budget — needed to keep WNS at OpenROAD's level once the loops are gone.
  6. I: run the three big designs; fix J in parallel to add the macro designs.

2t-timeclosure stays opt-in and does not gate any of this. When its controller becomes the default loop it supersedes A, E, F and H on that path; B(2), C, D and J are needed either way, and every change here is measured against the same control artifacts 2t A records.
