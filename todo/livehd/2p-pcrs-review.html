2p-pcrs-review PCRS clock tree: review, limits and plan

Review of 2026-11-22 of the native clock tree and power grid (2p-pcrs). Five fixes and one option were applied in the work tree and tested; the rest is pending work, listed in 2p-pcrs. The Track A measurements cover subsequent builder changes.

The short version: the plumbing works end to end and the tree is small and cheap, but it is not a balanced tree and it is under-driven, and the way results are measured cannot yet tell a good tree from a lucky run. The pending work fixes the measurement first, then the wire model, then balance and drive.

What changed in this pass

  • Abort on retained pins. The leaf fanout option was also applied to pins the existing clock source keeps (discrete gate forks). A source with more of them than leaf_fanout made the build fail with "no legal clock driver". The option now bounds planned stages only (pass/place/pcrs/clock_tree.cpp, test RetainedSourcePinsDoNotCountAgainstTheLeafFanout).
  • Abort on a third build in one workdir. A fresh buffer name is a hash of its members and a retained name stays with the branch that kept most of its old members, so after a regrouping the two can be equal. The build stopped with "clock instance name already in use". It now takes the next free name (pass/place/clock_network.cpp, assign_clock_names). No test yet: it needs three generations.
  • Stale placement after a new RC fit. The content of synth.place.pcrs.clock_rc_fit was not an input of the physical cache, only its path (lhd/lhd_kernel_synth.cpp, both lists).
  • Small ones. The help text of skew_hold_margin_ps said 4 ps where the code uses 29 ps. tools/clock_rc_fit ended on a bare map::at for a tree without trunk stages.
  • New option synth.place.pcrs.max_slew_ps (default 0, no change): a bound on the transition a planned stage delivers. It exists to measure limit L3 below, and it is a strict no-op when it does not bind (minimax at 80 ps is identical to the default run).
  • Clock view in the GUI and on the agent socket. See the last section.

Tests: bazel test --config=backend //pass/place/... //tools/... //pass/timing/... //lhd:lhd_options_test //lhd/tests:lhd_placement_pcrs_smoke. The first pass of this review saw four timeouts in //pass/place/tests/regularity (mesh_4x4, mesh_6x6, ring_32, hierarchy_4x4) at a machine load of 60 to 80. Run again with the machine quiet they pass in 60 to 250 s of the 300 s limit (ring_32 251 s), so the timeouts were load, and that test is close to the limit.

Limits of the current PCRS

Ordered by how much they limit the result. "Measured here" means a short run of this pass, with the frozen binary and scripts in /lscratch/renau/domplace_runs/pcrs_gui.

The tree changes smoothly. The flow result moves by 110 ps with no trend. On the identical placement (the DEF right after the tree is built, timed with tools/clock_timing_report) the worst slack of the six trees differs by about 11 ps. So the swing comes from what runs after the tree: the repair loop starts from −387 to −819 ps and ends anywhere between −28 and −135 ps. The timing-driven placement round after the tree runs 100 steps, stops unconverged and is rejected in every run. A lead of 3.7 ps on minimax is far inside this spread, and the routed repeats carry no spread at all because they repeat the same inputs.

L2 — The tree is not balanced

With the default options nothing compares arrivals. Measured here on liteeth (sys_clock, 2,976 pins): every leaf is five stages deep, but one stage costs between 19 and 209 ps, and the estimated arrivals run from 451 to 796 ps. Chunk homes and gated domains are collapsed on their own and meet at different depths; a macro pin is a leaf of one. balance=true pads early arms, but it is off by default, it does not run at a source fork, it is skipped for a design with an inverted clock at a register (NVDLA), and on coralnpu its guards rejected every candidate. There is no gate cloning, which is what the coralnpu loss needs.

L3 — Stages are under-driven and the wire model is not the router's

A trunk stage at the limit costs 150 to 230 ps where a healthy one costs 30 to 60 ps. The low clock area in the results table is the other side of this. Measured here on liteeth with max_slew_ps=80: trunk transition drops to 65 / 75 / 79 ps and estimated skew from 345 to 280 ps, but the planner meets the target with chains of the smallest buffer, 9 to 13 stages deep, and the insertion stays at 467 to 747 ps. The reason is the wire: the planner uses one platform clock RC, and at that resistance a long arm is slow whatever drives it. The router puts the trunk on upper layers with a wider rule, so the estimate is far off: 219 ps mean error on coralnpu, and 3,493 ps estimated against 1,418 ps extracted on 25 single-load stages of NVDLA. A fit made on one design made another worse. Even on minimax, at the same placement, the planner's arrivals are 10 to 16 ps early against the timer, which is more than the 11 ps of skew it reports.

L4 — Topology: bisection by count, long paths, half-full leaves

  • Groups are halved by count until one fits, so leaves end near half the limit when the count falls just above a power of two. Leaves built against leaves needed at 24 per leaf: minimax 128 / 95, liteeth 178 / 142, coralnpu 643 / 419, litepci 1,068 / 544, NVDLA/partition_c 3,708 / 2,231 (455 of them under 6 pins).
  • Each driver sits at the middle of the box of its loads, without looking at blockages or macros. On the latest liteeth path the four trunk arms add up to 646 µm.
  • On NVDLA/partition_c 1,579 of 2,138 trunk buffers have one child: repeater chains.
  • An electrical failure of a full group is cut at the widest gap in one direction, which gives uneven leaves; and one discrete gate fork anywhere on the net switches the outlier rule off for the whole net.

L5 — Hold

The native tree starts with worse hold on sha3 and needs twice the hold buffers. NVDLA/partition_c ends with 65,073 hold buffers and litepci with 28,289. Inside lhd the liteeth run above ends at −644 ps of hold, left to the downstream repair. The native hold repair has three acceptance problems found by reading (not measured): an edit is rejected when an untouched endpoint inside the 29 ps reserve moves by a fraction of a picosecond (clock_schedule.cpp:1101, clock_bounds.cpp:224); a net is given up when one of its failing loads has no setup room (clock_hold.cpp:177); and the rejected list is cleared after every accepted buffer, so the same candidates are timed again (clock_schedule.cpp:1178).

L6 — One bad domain or input stops the whole run

  • An infeasible domain throws out of the planner with no catch, also for a net where PCRS would insert nothing (clock_network.cpp:1099, clock_tree.cpp:367). NVDLA/partition_c has 556 clock nets.
  • The final tree build runs outside the try block and log redirect of the first one (lhd_kernel_synth.cpp:5167).
  • With the tree on, the power grid turns on by itself; a floorplan DEF that already has a grid fails in final legalization with "the optimized netlist is too dense for the fixed core" (lhd_kernel_synth.cpp:2948, :5193).
  • Clock pins on a net that was demoted to data are in no report (prects_networks.cpp:340).
  • An unsupported SDC command switches every gate search off and the report shows empty lists without a reason (clock_network.cpp:1163, :1218, :1410).

L7 — Timing engine

  • A register behind a clock inverter is checked against the full period: optimistic by half a period. It is reported as unsupported, not fixed (pass/timing/engine.cpp:910). The fixture that expects 510 ps (pass/timing/tests/clock_phase) is in no BUILD file.
  • After a clock edit in a trial, the enable check of an integrated gate keeps the old clock arrival (engine.cpp:864). This affects exactly the skew and gate trials.
  • A divider behind a divider without -master_clock throws under a propagated clock (engine.cpp:791, sdc.cpp:650).
  • Hold ignores set_clock_uncertainty (engine.cpp:1962). No cohort SDC uses it.
  • Median error against OpenSTA on the same wires: 45 ps on coralnpu, 33 ps on NVDLA, 26 ps on litepci.

L8 — Cost

31 pins take 3.2 s in pass.pcrs.clock. NVDLA plans in 95 s and one balance attempt takes 677 s. The litepci schedule search ran 77 trials in 2,165 s. A skew round has no trial budget (clock_schedule.cpp:733), every trial copies the floorplan and rebuilds name-keyed maps of every pin, and a journal trial pays two full hold evaluations (engine.cpp:2488). The grouping cache is dropped when fields that do not affect grouping change (clock_tree.cpp:141).

L9 — Placement around the tree

The largest move in the downstream legalization is 60 µm on liteeth and 91 µm on coralnpu with the native tree, against about 2 µm with OpenROAD's; the hand-off keeps the estimates of the planned positions and does not record how far a buffer moved. Tree buffers ignore synth.place.resize.dont_use (clock_network.cpp:422). The placement anchors stay off by default: they made NVDLA and coralnpu worse.

L10 — Power grid

By reading, not run. A via at a crossing where the strap or the rail ends inside the other's width is sized for a full crossing and can overhang (power_grid_export.cpp:277); it can happen at the core edge, at a macro halo and with a small JSON offset. JSON overrides are checked only for sign and pitch, not against the manufacturing grid or the layer's width and spacing, and the remaining errors appear in final legalization (power_grid_profiles.cpp:94). The routing-capacity model takes nothing off for via landings that run across a layer (legroute_grid.cpp:432). The grid is rebuilt and copied on every routing-plan call (place.cpp:13036; 312,336 vias on liteeth). The first clock legalization budgets the reference grid, not the tuned one (place.cpp:13074).

L11 — Clock gates

By reading: a gate trial lists the gate's test pin as a control that must have a timing check, and a pin tied to a constant has none, so merge, leaf, raise and clone would all be rejected as "missing_gate_checks" on a gate cell with a tied scan enable (clock_schedule.cpp:281, :970). This matters now that the asap7 integrated gates are allowed. It needs a fixture before a fix. An enable flop with an asynchronous reset is not recognized as part of a gate (clock_gate.cpp:42). No test runs lhd lec on a merged or cloned gate.

  • Sizing alone is most of the setup gain. Fifty-six cells recover 315 ps of latency in G, 318 ps of setup WNS and 269 ps of hold WNS. In the placement-RC sweeps upsizing only the leaves did nothing, and upsizing every trunk buffer by nine steps was worse than by five: a larger buffer loads its driver.
  • Delay alone fixes hold between domains and nothing else. It cannot help the setup of G to the ports, which is set by G's absolute latency, and it moves the hold problem to the asynchronous recovery and removal checks (−26 ps to −445 ps). Delay placed before the gate instead of after it broke the gating hold by −1,000 ps in the placement-RC runs, so chains go after the gate.
  • Both together take hold TNS from −803k to −34k (−96%), failing hold endpoints from 4,237 to 575 and setup WNS from −803 to −434 ps. The remaining failures are the 327 dm-to-port setup paths (the domains now arrive at 0.8 ns against zero-latency ports) and the asynchronous checks, which a delay on a flop domain makes worse. The three domains line up to 4 ps in the last row.
  • Measurement caution. Incremental global routing after an edit (global_route -start_incremental) is not usable here: swapping 645 buffers to another master and back, which changes nothing, moved the latest arrival from 1,197 to 386 ps. Each row above is a full do-5_1_grt of the edited 4_cts.odb.
  • Placement RC overstates all of it. The same sweeps with the platform's clock wire RC give a G latency of 1,617 ps and a sizing gain of only 190 ps (L3), which is why the planner's wire model was changed first.

What this decided: trunk drive by default (A6), a clock wire RC from the routed layers (A2), and balancing by arrival band with a skew bound (A4). The numbers of the code that followed are in the next section.

Plan

The work that answers these limits is in 2p-pcrs, rewritten on 2026-11-22 into three tracks: the tree builder (A1 to A8: one wire model, a delay interval per subtree, a merge at the balance point, propagated transition and sizing, leaf fill), per-chunk subtrees joined at the top (H1 to H5), and a common centroid helper that replaces the anchors (G1 to G5). The measurement step P0 comes first, and the robustness, timing-engine and gate fixes (P4, P5, P7) do not wait.

The comparison runs

On 2026-11-22 at 08:21, 24 OpenROAD processes of the comparison were running. Several detailed routes had not converged after more than a day: the unmodified coralnpu control (iteration 29, about 31,000 violations), the two gate- routes of s6/coral-grt-overflow (1.3 and 1.7 million violations after 6 hours), and two NVDLA global routes still in timing repair after 30 hours. This pass did not stop any of them. By 09:27 none was running: they ended at about 09:00, when the session that owned them restarted. The rows of 2p-pcrs that waited for them (the unmodified coralnpu control, the newer-build control, the matched S2 arm) are therefore still open and need a new launch, preferably after P0.

The disk reached 100% at about 08:30. Results older than three days were pruned to their small files (1.37 TB freed); the frozen placement campaign the comparison reads was not touched.

Looking at the tree

Under lhd synth --gui with synth.place.pcrs.clock=true, every scene after the tree build carries the tree by node (Scene::clock_nets, filled in gui_scene, with the hand-off's estimates). The code is pass/place/gui/gui_clock.{hpp,cpp} with gui_clock_test.cpp.

  • Page: a clock tree layer with four views: stage depth, sink arrival, leaf slew and a grid of local skew.
  • Agent: clock (summary, nets, sinks, pair, map) and screenshot layers=...,clock.
  • Decisions: clock_edit stages leaf drive steps, wanted arrivals and the scalar options, and op=apply restarts the flow with them. Checked on lfsr: staging leaf_fanout=8 and step 2 on one leaf gave four leaves of eight and a larger buffer on that leaf after the restart.

Not done: there is no browser on the test machine, so the page layer was checked by a syntax pass and by running its drawing code against the live replies with a stub canvas, not by eye. The numbers shown are the planner's estimates, which L3 says are 10 to 16 ps off on minimax and far more on larger designs; the next step is to send the timer's per-pin arrivals with the scene. A decision costs a full restart; a gate right after the tree build would let an agent try one without re-placing. Routed clock wires are not drawn.
