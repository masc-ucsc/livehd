Review findings that determine the experiment

  • The existing SAT density is movable cell area divided by usable row area. It does not measure route congestion. Existing pair selection requires both movable single-height CORE endpoints to fit inside one region; fixed SRAM endpoints and cross-region pairs are not primary alignment targets.
  • The completed nine-leg 40 µm study preserves its input identities. Attempted regions contain, on average, 1,246.875 CNN, 3,480.5 Eyeriss and 6,375.875 Coral cells. Both SAT legs on every design reach zero solver trials because candidate construction fails. Wider CNN movement reaches 32 bounded-candidate UNSAT trials in five regions, with no accepted changes. This does not prove that the physical regions have no legal solution. The first new experiment must expose candidate coverage and provide bounded legal row-gap choices within each full source region; merely changing region ordering cannot fix an empty menu.
  • The new estimator exposes H/V utilization, macro-pin utilization, blocked fraction and aggregate overflow/unreachable counters. It does not export per-net routed segments. Enhanced RSMT uses tree lengths with free-space redistribution inside reachable net boxes/channels; it is a routing proxy. Its internal tree edges can also represent bounding rectangles, and nets above the degree cap fall back to weighted RUDY. A long-block experiment requires explicit proposal construction, not an assumption that the map already stores legal detailed routes.
  • Map freshness includes physical pin positions, orientations/offsets, masters, topology, weights, masks, core/grid, capacities and model options. A GP continuation signature alone is insufficient. The current rudy_metrics snapshot diagnostic substitutes unit weights, so it cannot transport the timing-weighted placement map unchanged.

  • F — Optional satlegalize: incremental pin-access alignment at the end of final GP.

Equal average pin-center coordinates alone are insufficient. Evaluate separation limits in rows/sites as well as µm.

Include affected neighboring cells and the impact on incident nets with endpoints outside the window. Budget by movable cell count and candidate positions, density and solver effort; the discussed 100 × 100 µm window is illustrative, not a chosen constant. Either model complete legal placement of all window cells or validate completion with the existing legalizer. Total whitespace alone does not guarantee that the remaining cells fit around fixed aligned pairs.
    • Incremental search: keep one solver per window and guard proposed pair alignments with activation literals/assumptions. Both encoding/backend alternatives must solve incrementally, reusing constraints and learned information across trials. Preserve accepted alignment relationships where possible without immediately freezing absolute coordinates, so aligned cells can still move together. Try the next critical pair until the budget is exhausted; on conflict, evaluate skipping the pair and continuing versus stopping that window. Treat a timeout/unknown result as undecided, not proof of infeasibility.

Bound this search. Heuristic legalizer success need not be monotonic, so backoff does not prove the largest feasible prefix. A single parity equation does not encode coordinate equality. Do not assume native XOR outperforms compact CNF; measure the complete incremental workload, including model extraction and repeated rollback/assumption changes.

Verify a clean compile and full lhd link with cvc5 and ABC present, for both solver choices. Preserve cvc5's private dependency isolation and existing formal behavior; an isolated solver-only build is insufficient validation.
    • CNN evaluation to evolve the algorithm: replay the same saved final-GP inputs and representative CNN windows across both incremental encodings/backends.
