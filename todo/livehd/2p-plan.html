Subtasks — pending

  • A — Local TIELO/TIEHI drivers for each constant use.
    • Observed: the live CNN design inspected on 2026-10-20 has one __place_tielo driving net_46575 with 34 top-level output pins and 181 internal input pins, and one __place_tiehi driving net_46574 with 9 top-level output pins and 994 internal input pins. The TIELO net's terminal bounding box spans approximately 1.95 × 1.86 mm. These are unrouted connections displayed as flywires, not measured routed wirelength.
    • Cause: pass/place/place.cpp materializes one physical driver per constant value and merges the corresponding sinks into its net. The same global tie identities are reused in later placement and repair rounds.
    • Before GP: replicate the tie for each physical sink pin/bit, including macro inputs and constant top-level output pins. Give each tie output a single load so its edge pulls it toward that user. Include all tie-cell area in density and capacity accounting from the start; initial tie positions are provisional.
    • During legalization: identify ties from characterized constant-output semantics rather than instance-name prefixes. Collect them for final processing while legalizing their functional users first.
    • Final placement: after the last stage that can move the users, including detailed placement and later ECO/repair legalization, revisit the ties. Move a single-load tie to a nearby legal row site using its user's actual pin location and orientation. Split a remaining shared tie into local copies, one per load. Prefer free sites; permit bounded local cell displacement when necessary, respecting fixed cells, locked macros, row compatibility and core bounds.
    • Do not lose the fix: preserve stable per-sink tie identities across incremental and repair rounds. Replace assumptions about one global tie per polarity so refresh/cache paths cannot merge local drivers again. Update physical nets and graph connectivity together, including bus aliases, memory pins and top-level outputs, so DEF, Verilog, timing and saved graphs agree. Final snapshots must include the repaired tie placement.
    • Acceptance: every constant use has the correct driver; each used tie output drives exactly one load; all ties are legally placed without overlap and close to their users. Add small repository-owned checks for both polarities, constant IO outputs, multiple constant bits on one user, imported/shared ties and repeated placement/repair/cache flows. Reinspect the CNN placement to confirm the global constant flywires are replaced by local connections.
  • B — Check IO movement and clock-pin placement; fix if needed.
    • Observed: in the inspected pre-CTS CNN placement, CLK sits at the bottom-left corner and connects to 181 cell pins spread across the design. The current run has fixed IO pins, but was intended to allow pin movement. Establish whether that fixed placement was explicitly requested or introduced unintentionally by the flow.
    • Controlled rerun: repeat the placement with IO pins movable and IO placement enabled, keeping the design and other settings comparable. Record effective configuration, explicit pin constraints and pin coordinates before and after FP, macro placement and final placement. Compare against the fixed-pin run; do not infer that IO movement worked merely because an option was accepted.
    • Check the handoff: when IO placement is enabled, fp_plan and macro_plan should perform the intended pin adjustment unless the user explicitly hardcoded a pin position. Trace the request through stage controls, imported/generated pin status, cached placements and stage-to-stage freezing. Distinguish an explicit fixed-position constraint from an initial placement or a DEF export status; neither initialization nor an unintended early freeze should silently prevent requested IO movement.
    • Clock sanity check: for a broadly distributed clock load, expect a movable clock input near the midpoint of a longer core side: top/bottom for a wider core, left/right for a taller core. Treat a corner or another strongly offset location as suspicious unless the actual DFF/clock-sink concentration supports it. Use the sink distribution to assess the choice rather than imposing a midpoint regardless of the design. Explicitly fixed clock pins must remain fixed.
    • Fix and acceptance: if the rerun confirms unintended immobility or a poor clock-location decision, correct the responsible option/constraint handoff or placement policy. Add small repository-owned checks for movable IOs in both FP and macro stages, preservation of explicitly fixed pins, broadly distributed clock sinks and a deliberately concentrated sink distribution. Verify legal pin placement, unchanged connectivity and applicable bus-order/spacing constraints, then reinspect the movable-pin CNN result in OpenROAD.
  • C — Recover redundant buffers and buffer chains after final legalization.
    • Example to reproduce: running CNN and inspecting its final pre-CTS placement in OpenROAD can show adjacent, single-fanout buffer chains. In the 2026-10-20 run, the chain ending at __place_rebuffer_sub_20.inst_ram_w16_l32768_id1_1.u_ram_w16_l32768_id1_1_mem_10 has sibling buffers …mem_12 → …mem_11 → …mem_10, with pin-to-pin Manhattan distances of 0.596 µm and 0.970 µm. Each of those two nets has exactly one driver and one load, with no IO branches. …mem_10 then drives …mem_9 about 519.8 µm away. The middle buffer is a bypass candidate; the long outgoing connection means the final driver may still be needed. The inspected run's settings are in the local CNN evaluation plan. Exact names and distances are observations of that run, not requirements for future runs.
    • Current gap: post-placement optimization already runs after initial legalization/detail, and buffer rebuilding receives ECO legalization and fresh timing checks. However, its final recovery sweep only resizes cells. With full physical timing feedback, individual buffer-bypass trials are skipped in pass/place/repair.cpp. Thus nearby buffers can remain after rebuilding and legalization; a final sizing sweep does not remove them.
    • Fix: extend final recovery to trial identity-buffer bypass and chain collapse using the final legalized coordinates. Cover DFF → BUF → CELL and the general CELL → BUF → CELL, including buffers at either endpoint. For this check, both connecting nets must have exactly one load; exclude extra fanout and top-level port loads from the simple-chain case.
    • Acceptance of edits: closeness identifies candidates, not proof that a buffer is unnecessary. Trial changes transactionally, recompute physical RC and STA, and keep them only when the existing timing/electrical acceptance policy permits the area/topology recovery. Check the changed driver load, capacitance, slew and fanout, preserve protected networks and fixed cells, and retain buffers required to drive long wires or meet electrical limits. Locally legalize any resulting sizing/placement changes and recheck timing.
    • Placement and persistence: run cleanup after the last relevant legalization/detail or buffer-repair edit, with bounded repetition until no beneficial removals remain. Keep graph aliases, physical nets, saved graphs, DEF/Verilog, RC/STA and final reports consistent. Repeated placement, ECO and cache paths must not skip the check or silently restore removed buffers.
    • Validation: add small repository-owned positive checks for an adjacent removable buffer and a collapsible buffer chain, plus negative checks where bypass violates timing/electrical constraints or a long-wire driver must remain. Verify single-load eligibility, connectivity, legality and repeated-run behavior. Use CNN as an optional visual reproduction and compare final buffer counts, area and timing; keep default tests independent of sibling benchmark repositories.
  • D — Share reduced timing pull across inserted buffers and refresh critical nets periodically.
    • Observed: placement weights are keyed by driver pin. Post-GP buffer repair carries the original driver's weight forward, while a newly inserted buffer output defaults to weight 1. In the inspected CNN, net_191017 retains weight approximately 3.064 on a 0.386 µm driver-to-buffer segment, while the inserted buffer's output net_16096 defaults to 1. FP/macro timing checkpoints refresh weights, but this run's final cell GP has no timing checkpoints, and fresh post-GP repair STA does not refresh placement weights. Existing smoothing also leaves residual extra pull after a net leaves the critical set.
    • At buffer insertion: reduce the original driver's extra pull above the baseline weight of 1, then assign that same reduced weight to the new buffer-output edge. Do not retain the full old priority only on the driver-to-buffer segment or leave the downstream segment unweighted. Choose and validate the reduction factor as part of this task; apply the policy consistently when inserting a buffer chain or tree. These inherited weights are provisional until fresh timing is available.
    • Next OpenTimer sizing/timing run: capture the changed topology, loads and physical RC, and recompute critical-net membership and target weights for all current nets, including newly inserted buffer outputs. Feed the result back into placement's applied weights; updating timing reports or sizing decisions alone is insufficient. Replace the provisional split weights with the fresh timing-derived priorities.
    • Periodic refresh: run timing from time to time during FP, macro GP, final cell GP and subsequent repair/local placement, with a bounded cadence and a refresh after accepted sizing or topology changes before further placement uses stale priorities. Re-rank the worst-slack 20% of eligible timed non-clock nets, add newly critical nets, and remove nets that leave that set. Restore non-critical nets to baseline weight 1 rather than leaving historical extra pull to decay indefinitely. Remove obsolete driver identities after topology changes.
    • Validation and inspection: add small repository-owned checks for equal reduced weights on both sides of an inserted buffer, chains/trees, new critical nets entering and former critical nets returning to weight 1 on the next timing refresh. Verify final cell GP and repair consume the refreshed weights. Export current membership, applied weight, driver identity and timing checkpoint/stage so OpenROAD queries can distinguish current criticality from inherited priority. Reinspect CNN and compare placement, timing and electrical QoR.
  • E — Timing-aware legalization after final cell GP.
    • Current gap: the native legalizer uses uniform cell displacement weights for greedy packing and Abacus; GP's timing priorities do not protect critical cells during final legalization. Abacus can move cells that were already legalized, so priority ordering alone does not preserve their positions.
    • Proposed approach: run fresh OpenTimer analysis using the final GP coordinates and physical RC, then derive cell priorities from current pin/path timing rather than historical smoothed net weights. Give critical cells first choice of nearby legal rows/sites and a larger displacement penalty. Preserve the spatial ordering required inside the row solver, and carry timing protection through Abacus and subsequent detailed placement.
    • Alternatives to evaluate: compare continuous criticality-based ordering and displacement costs with a two-phase pass that legalizes/protects critical cells first, optionally settles the remaining movable cells, then legalizes the rest. Prefer strong movement penalties or bounded protection over unconditional permanent locks; allow controlled relaxation when necessary to avoid fragmented row space or infeasible packing. Do not assume one extra GP iteration is sufficient. Subtask F develops an optional solver-based alternative.
    • Timing feedback: after full legalization, recompute RC and STA, refresh criticality as in subtask D, and permit bounded local correction when movement worsens timing. Check newly critical paths as well as the original critical set. Preserve explicit fixed constraints, row/site compatibility, connectivity and placement legality.
    • Evaluate and adjust constants with Eyeriss and CNN: sweep criticality-to-priority/displacement scaling, protection thresholds, relaxation limits and any settling/local-correction iteration budgets. Compare against the existing final legalization on both designs, using comparable final GP inputs. Record post-legalization and post-detail WNS/TNS, timing/electrical violations, critical-cell and overall displacement, HPWL, legality and runtime. Choose constants from measured tradeoffs across both designs, not one favorable case; inspect the resulting placements in OpenROAD.
    • Validation: add small repository-owned checks for critical/non-critical site competition, preservation of timing priority through Abacus/detail, dense-row relaxation and changing critical paths. Keep Eyeriss and CNN as separate evaluation runs; default tests must not depend on sibling benchmark repositories.
  • F — Optional satlegalize: incremental pin-access alignment at the end of final GP.
    • Scope and activation: develop an opt-in satlegalize option at the end of final cell GP, before its legalization/detail handoff. Optimize only standard cells in small local windows; preserve macros, explicit fixed cells, IO constraints and objects outside the window. Retain the existing legalization flow as the default and fallback. This is an elaborated alternative to subtask E, not a replacement of FP or macro placement.
    • Alignment targets: use fresh OpenTimer criticality to try driver-sink pairs in descending timing priority, starting with short connections and then nearby critical connections. Align usable pin access points on compatible horizontal or vertical routing tracks, using actual pin shapes, legal access locations, layers, cell orientations and routing obstructions. Equal average pin-center coordinates alone are insufficient. Evaluate separation limits in rows/sites as well as µm.
    • Window model: constrain legal row/site placement, allowed orientations, non-overlap, fixed obstacles, window boundaries and bounded displacement. Include affected neighboring cells and the impact on incident nets with endpoints outside the window. Budget by movable cell count and candidate positions, density and solver effort; the discussed 100 × 100 µm window is illustrative, not a chosen constant. Either model complete legal placement of all window cells or validate completion with the existing legalizer. Total whitespace alone does not guarantee that the remaining cells fit around fixed aligned pairs.
    • Incremental search: keep one solver per window and guard proposed pair alignments with activation literals/assumptions. Both encoding/backend alternatives must solve incrementally, reusing constraints and learned information across trials. Preserve accepted alignment relationships where possible without immediately freezing absolute coordinates, so aligned cells can still move together. Try the next critical pair until the budget is exhausted; on conflict, evaluate skipping the pair and continuing versus stopping that window. Treat a timeout/unknown result as undecided, not proof of infeasibility.
    • Checkpoint and rollback: save accepted constraint prefixes and placement snapshots. If 100 accepted pairs prevent the remaining legalization from completing, try a shorter prefix such as 50; after success, optionally try 75, keeping the best placement that actually legalizes. Bound this search. Heuristic legalizer success need not be monotonic, so backoff does not prove the largest feasible prefix. At zero accepted alignments, restore the untouched final-GP snapshot and run the original legalizer. Later legalization/detail must preserve accepted alignments or explicitly relax and revalidate them.
    • Encodings and backends: evaluate CryptoMiniSat with native XOR/XNOR constraints and ordinary CNF, and CaDiCaL with ordinary CNF. Compare candidate-position encodings against bounded binary coordinates where appropriate. Bitwise equality may benefit from XNOR, but pin offsets require arithmetic and legality requires comparisons, cardinality and overlap constraints. A single parity equation does not encode coordinate equality. Do not assume native XOR outperforms compact CNF; measure the complete incremental workload, including model extraction and repeated rollback/assumption changes.
    • Clean solver build and linkage: both CryptoMiniSat and CaDiCaL already exist inside the cvc5 build, with private solver symbols; the existing LEC profiles are not a placement API. Provide a clean, model-producing incremental interface, reusing the pinned builds where practical. If a separate build is needed, check compile-option/ABI compatibility, duplicate symbols and global-state isolation rather than assuming another copy can link safely. Verify a clean compile and full lhd link with cvc5 and ABC present, for both solver choices. Preserve cvc5's private dependency isolation and existing formal behavior; an isolated solver-only build is insufficient validation.
    • CNN evaluation to evolve the algorithm: replay the same saved final-GP inputs and representative CNN windows across both incremental encodings/backends. Sweep window/candidate limits, movement bounds, criticality order, search budgets, skip policy and rollback granularity. Record accepted alignments, completed legalization, fallback frequency, solver time/memory and runtime tails, displacement, HPWL, routing-access quality and post-legalization/detail WNS/TNS and electrical violations. Recompute physical RC/STA before accepting a timing result; use routing checks to validate predicted alignment benefits. Inspect CNN in OpenROAD and use the measurements to choose CNF versus XOR/XNOR and adjust the algorithm. Cross-check generality with Eyeriss as in subtask E.
    • Focused validation: add small repository-owned checks for legal pin-access alignment with offsets/orientation, competing pairs, fragmented free space, boundary nets, assumption retraction, timeout handling, model extraction and complete rollback to zero. Exercise repeated solver instances alongside cvc5 to catch linkage/state conflicts. Keep large CNN/Eyeriss evaluations outside default tests.
    • Related work: Vertical M1 Routing-Aware Detailed Placement (DAC 2017) models local legal placement and pin alignment with MILP; TransPlace (ASP-DAC 2024 presentation) uses incremental SAT in detailed-placement windows at transistor level. These motivate the approach, not a demonstrated timing or runtime gain for LiveHD standard-cell placement.
