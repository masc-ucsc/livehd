Confirmed target restrictions: DOMINO synthesis rejects negative-edge clocks and transparent latches except those belonging to recognized integrated clock-gating (ICG) structures. Emit an error at the offending source line; do not silently preserve the offending state as CMOS. These restrictions do not apply to the generic-CMOS USYN target.

Confirmed register eligibility: all positive-edge registers outside memories, ICGs and opaque state are eligible for DominoLatch conversion. A future register keep attribute, settable from Pyrope and defaulting to false, may opt out individual registers. Confirmed rail model: every DOMINO cell, including DominoLatch, has two outputs Q and !Q. A gate may consume both polarities of the same signal, and either output of a DOMINO producer is available for free. Otherwise, insert a NOT ahead of the consuming DOMINO when residual logic is needed, with its cost and dependency recorded. With no residual logic, the preceding DOMINO already supplies both rails: resolve NOT(Q) to its !Q output. This is rail reuse, not a reason to reject the candidate or add a NOT.

Confirmed technology constraints: configurable maximum logical inputs (default 8), maximum series stack (default 4), and maximum parallel discharge branches (default 10). These are frequency/technology dependent. There is no maximum-transistor legality constraint; transistor count remains an optimization cost. Parallel branches mean factored network width: a literal has width 1, parallel composition sums widths, and series composition takes their maximum. Thus (a OR b) AND (c OR d) has width 2, not four enumerated paths. Cut/window analysis uses a separate, larger support limit.

Confirmed phase configuration: DOMINO construction uses a configurable one or two clock phases. All leftover CMOS logic supplies inputs before the first phase. In two-phase mode, phase 1 feeds phase 2. Each phase may contain any number of parallel cells, and a phase-1 cell may feed any number of phase-2 cells. The endpoint register is always replaced by the final-phase DominoLatch in DOMINO output; leftover logic never justifies keeping that register. There is no cone-wide cell-count or cross-phase fanout limit. Routing and fanout timing are separate physical problems, outside this implementation.

Confirmed LEC boundary: LEC is a separate problem and flow. USYN does not invoke a prover, gate publication on a verdict, or add LEC-specific analysis. Preserve original register/DominoLatch names and source-state correspondence as much as possible to make independent LEC easier and faster.

Optimization order:

  1. Apply cheap structural hashing, constant folding and dead-logic cleanup.
  2. Try the admitted whole cone in one legal dual-rail cell. Accept a competitive result after checking shared cost; otherwise retain the function and failure information for the next attempt.
  3. When two clock phases are enabled, try exactly two cells: one first-phase producer feeding the endpoint DominoLatch. If that does not yield a competitive result, try wider parallel two-phase decompositions within the same bounded tier. Reuse the admitted function. Stop on a competitive full-cone result; further cost improvement uses a separate budget. Two cells is a fast path, not a topology restriction.
  4. For unresolved or expensive endpoints, search what to move into upstream CMOS. Preserve shared-input dependencies inside the selected function where profitable; compare bounded coordinated removals, asymmetric cuts and reconvergence-driven growth using the same evaluator. Removing a subfunction means exposing its computed value at the boundary, not deleting its behavior. Keep alternatives that expose a repeated input across multiple branches; neither fewer boundary inputs nor greater depth alone identifies the best cut.
  5. Perform target-cost local and two-endpoint optimization where needed, then derive the residual network from all live consumers.
  6. Optimize that shared residual once: cleanup → four-input rewriting → bounded resubstitution → cleanup. Start resubstitution with up to eight window inputs, approximately 32 divisors and at most two inserted nodes per replacement. Require positive estimated area gain with shared-node accounting and a depth guard. Reconsider affected endpoint boundaries and shared costs once, retain the better legal result, refresh the residual live set and clean up, then freeze. Do this before expanding selected functions for CMOS output, so that expansion does not trigger another generic optimization round over absorbed logic.

Whole-cone search keeps repeated-input dependencies visible to functional simplification. Residual optimization improves what selection leaves outside, and its feedback can expose better inputs for the selected functions. Here “non-DOMINO” includes unate logic left outside the selected cells. It does not mean only binate functions. See E for boundary selection, M for residual optimization, and search scheduling for budgets and measurements.

Dedicated residual refactoring and balancing remain later measured extensions. Boundary search complements network optimization; V1 does not add a separate ABC fx-style algebraic extraction pass.

Choosing the function inputs: optimize the boundary, rather than committing to an order in which primary inputs are absorbed. Start with the admitted cone and ask which subfunctions to leave outside. Keep several alternatives because removing one branch may hide a cancellation or a shared factor in another. Use coordinated growth to recover such dependencies when starting from a bounded window. For example, absorbing both a | b and a | c beneath an AND exposes a | (b & c). The final choice includes the cost of computing anything left outside and the cost of shared logic that other consumers still require. Full absorption avoids the boundary-choice problem within that cone; it does not prove minimum cost for the shared network.

Coverage-guided fast path: the reported observations—over 10% of cones fitting one function and around 50% fitting two DOMINO—motivate trying full absorption before broad cut enumeration. They are a scheduling hypothesis to measure, not guaranteed coverage or a reason to enlarge search without bounds. Reuse the admitted function between attempts; spend boundary search effort on the remaining or expensive cones. Distinguish exactly two cells from the configurable two-phase network, which may contain more cells. The one-cell attempt precedes the bounded exactly-two-cell attempt; wider two-phase covers remain supported after that fast path. The default strategy is therefore whole-cone analysis first, selective removal second, and generic optimization of the surviving residual third. Cut enumeration remains a bounded source of alternative boundaries within that strategy.

Confirmed decisions

The following decisions incorporate the user's clarifications and supersede conflicting wording in the proposal and older task pages. Proposed file/option names, analysis budgets and provisional cost coefficients remain implementation choices to measure; they are not additional technology constraints.

  1. First delivery boundary — confirmed by the user. Fully independent USYN with zero ABC dependencies. Retain ABC in LiveHD for comparisons and the alternative flow; only an explicitly requested tmap step uses ABC, mapping logical DOMINO gates and residual CMOS to existing cells. Future per-gate Liberty descriptions are consumed by an external real-cell generation/characterization tool. Physical DOMINO cells and their characterization are deferred.
  2. State realization and permanent CMOS target — confirmed by the user. Creating a physical DominoLatch replaces the original register because the cell contains the latch. In the first implementation, ABC/TMAP maps all selected logic to plain CMOS, no physical DOMINO gate is used, and the original register remains. This CMOS flow stays permanently for ordinary-library targets and benchmarking, including after the future cell-characterization flow exists. Retaining a register in this target is normal behavior, not a failed DOMINO synthesis fallback.
  3. CMOS tmap granularity — confirmed by the user. ABC reads the whole selected network; do not split proposed DOMINO from non-DOMINO logic for this target. In the future DOMINO target, USYN has already mapped the DOMINO cells, so those cells do not pass through tmap; only residual CMOS does. Both targets retain the independent USYN synthesis result.
  4. State eligibility and clock/latch restrictions — confirmed by the user. DOMINO mode cannot handle negative-edge clocks. Source transparent latches are allowed only for recognized ICG structures. Violations are errors pointing to the offending source line, not silent exclusions or CMOS fallback. Generic-CMOS USYN does not impose these restrictions. The generated DominoLatch's integrated storage is distinct from an unsupported source latch. All positive-edge registers outside memories, ICGs and opaque state are candidates. A future register keep attribute, settable from Pyrope and defaulting false, may exclude individual registers; defer its implementation and assume all current test/benchmark registers are not kept. Memories and ICGs are special non-DOMINO structures, not ordinary registers awaiting a keep flag. Preserve enable, reset and initialization semantics; their physical realization remains part of the future cell contract. Physical cell realization is deferred; it must preserve the selected state behavior while converting eligible ordinary registers into DominoLatch cells.
  5. Dual rails and per-gate polarity — confirmed by the user. Both polarities of one signal may feed one DOMINO gate. Every DOMINO cell, including DominoLatch, has Q and !Q outputs for free; otherwise a NOT may be inserted before the consuming gate when residual logic is available. It cannot be inserted in the one-DOMINO case with no extra leftover logic because the previous DOMINO already supplies the needed !Q: reuse that rail rather than create NOT(Q). The user's follow-up clarifies that this case is not a candidate rejection; any missing opposite rail is an implementation/modeling error. Explicit residual NOTs remain relevant for ordinary producers without an already available opposite phase. This supersedes the older domino plan's charged-twin model for DOMINO producers.
  6. Technology limits and branch metric — confirmed by the user. Configure logical inputs (default 8), maximum series stack (default 4), and maximum parallel discharge branches (default 10) for the chosen technology and frequency. Do not constrain maximum transistors. Optimization cuts are separately configurable and larger than gate support (proposed initial default 16). Constrain factored parallel width: a literal has width 1, parallel composition sums widths, and series composition takes their maximum. Thus (a OR b) AND (c OR d) counts as 2, matching the existing SP formula metric, not 4 discharge paths. Expose dual-output Domino/DominoLatch overhead as provisional cost-model coefficients and document their values during implementation; they do not add a technology-legality constraint or imply characterized cell area.
  7. LEC separation — confirmed by the user. LEC is a totally separate problem. USYN's accommodation is preserving register/DominoLatch names as much as possible to simplify and accelerate independent LEC. Do not integrate LEC, proof gates, solver budgets or verdict caches. Exact truth-table dependence, completion and function construction remain Boolean synthesis operations, not a separate equivalence flow.
  8. Clock-phase configuration — confirmed by the user. Select one or two clock phases. In the two-phase configuration, first-phase cells feed second-phase cells. Both phases permit arbitrarily many parallel cells, and each first-phase cell can feed arbitrarily many cells in the second phase. Do not limit total cells in a cone or impose a cross-phase fanout cap. Routing/fanout timing is a separate problem.
  9. Residual logic and mandatory replacement — confirmed by the user. Whatever logic remains outside the selected one/two-phase DOMINO structure supplies its inputs before the first clock phase. The original endpoint register is always replaced in DOMINO output. No leftover-logic case changes that rule; keeping original registers belongs to the separate permanent CMOS target.
  10. Whole-cone-first selection and selective removal — confirmed by the user. Attempt a legal dual-rail whole-cone implementation in one cell, then two phases when enabled, under strict admission/work bounds. For remaining cases, search which subfunctions to compute in upstream CMOS while retaining useful shared dependencies inside the endpoint. Use bounded windows when the whole cone is too large, with priority cuts and reconvergence-driven growth as alternative seeds. Reuse failed-attempt information and retain legal incumbents. Neither shrinking nor growing a boundary guarantees monotonic improvement or globally optimal inputs; evaluate exact candidate functions and total affected cost.
  11. Residual optimization — confirmed by the user. Implement native XAG cleanup, four-input rewriting and bounded resubstitution after initial endpoint selection, followed by one affected-endpoint reconsideration round. Preserve shared residual outputs and state boundaries. Use nonzero AND/XOR cost weights and a depth guard. Mockturtle is algorithm inspiration only, not a dependency. Additional refactoring/balancing are later measured extensions.

Search scheduling and coverage evidence

The user reports that over 10% of cones fit one function with Q/!Q available, and around 50% fit with two DOMINO. This motivates cheap whole-cone attempts before broad boundary exploration, with exactly-two-cell candidates tried before wider two-phase networks. Do not interpret the 50% figure as an additional 50 percentage points without measured denominators. When collecting results, report cell count and phase count separately, with explicit denominators. Report one-cell fits, exactly-two-cell fits, wider two-phase fits, and partial-cone results separately; also report cumulative full-cone coverage after the one-cell and exactly-two-cell attempts over the same eligible population. This lets the measurements test the motivating observation without conflating two cells with two phases. The implementation permits parallel cells within the configured phase limit. These observations are not measurements of this implementation or acceptance thresholds. Configured one-phase mode never attempts a second phase.

Record whether a reported fit means Boolean representability with the available Q/!Q rails or a legal implementation under the configured logical-input, stack and parallel-branch limits. Only the latter is an accepted fast-path result. Keep those limits identical in scheduling comparisons; the larger analysis support budget must not silently enlarge the gate limits.

Use the observed coverage to order work, not to assign fixed fractions of the runtime budget. A one-cell attempt can be cheap enough to try on every admitted cone; two-phase decomposition needs its own tighter candidate/work budget because failed attempts may be expensive. Measure additional full-cone successes per unit of work for each tier before increasing its budget. Fully absorbed cones skip residual optimization only when no other live consumer still needs their internal logic.

In particular, the coverage observations justify trying full absorption early; they do not show that two-phase search should receive half the runtime. Measure successful and unsuccessful attempts separately, including the cost of discovering support and constructing tables. Compare schedules under the same total work cap. Keep the initial schedule deterministic and tune its defaults from these measurements; runtime learning or an adaptive scheduler is not required for V1.

The resulting V1 scheduling policy is an early-exit search with an incumbent: spend little on the one-cell fast path, give admitted two-phase decomposition a bounded opportunity, and reserve broader boundary exploration for unresolved or expensive endpoints. Prioritize reconvergence and the violated gate constraint within that final group. Do not infer that every remaining cone needs a large search: retain a legal minimal endpoint and cap unsuccessful work. A higher coverage percentage is useful only together with total affected cost and search effort; it does not override sharing or technology legality.

Validate this scheduling choice against a bounded cut-first baseline using the same technology limits, cost model and total work cap. Include cones that fit one cell, cones requiring exactly two cells, wider two-phase covers, and cones needing upstream residual logic. Record both fast-path coverage and final mixed-network cost so early acceptance cannot conceal a systematic quality loss. Keep this as an algorithm comparison inside native USYN; ABC remains a separate flow for later mapped-result benchmarking.

The reported coverage supports this order of attempts, but does not imply that full-cone analysis or two-cell decomposition is cheap. Charge cone discovery, truth-table construction and failed decomposition work as well as successful candidates. Keep a bounded-window route and a reserved selective-removal budget even when the observed absorption rate is high.

Collect an admitted cone and its exact function once, then reuse that analysis across the one-cell, two-phase and removal tiers while the graph and boundary remain unchanged. Keep three decisions separate: whether analysis fits its resource budget, whether a candidate satisfies the technology constraints, and whether selecting it improves the shared implementation. A larger analysis window can simplify to a legal smaller-support gate; free Q/!Q availability alone does not establish input, stack or branch legality. Rank removal moves by the constraint or cost that actually blocked the preceding attempt, not simply by the presence of a binate operator.
