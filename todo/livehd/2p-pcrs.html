2p-pcrs PCRS clock: a placement-aware clock network owned by LiveHD

Plan of 2026-11-22, from a question-and-answer session. It replaces the plan of 2026-11-19, whose subtasks S1 to S13 were worked through. The first implementation is being reviewed and committed separately; every clock option is off by default. Names of options, commands and record kinds below are proposals.

LiveHD builds the clock network itself and OpenROAD only routes it. The first implementation proved the hand-off, but its tree is not balanced and is under-driven, and it treats the design as one flat region. This plan has three tracks:

  • A — the tree builder. A tree built to arrivals and properly driven, measured on a frozen placement.
  • B — chunks. Each chunk builds its own subtree in its own worker, in parallel; a top level joins them.
  • C — centroids. One placement helper that groups cells, flops and memories around a point. The GUI, agents and the clock tree all use it.

A leads, C runs in parallel, B comes second and reuses A's builder (K19).

How options are exposed

This is the rule for every choice below (K17).

  • A choice that is good for most designs is on by default.
  • A choice that helps some places and hurts others is not a global flag. It gets a neutral or weak default and a local handle: one centroid, one leaf group, one gate, one chunk. The GUI and the agent socket show it and change it there.
  • A local change is collateral: a JSON record the next run reads, the same file for the GUI, an agent and a batch run. Macro placement already works this way (move, align, export).
  • What PCRS decides by itself is written as generated state, apart from what a person or an agent authored, so a rerun does not turn its own guesses into intent.

A — The tree builder

What the coralnpu tree swap showed (2p-pcrs-review): with the data path held fixed, changing only the tree moved setup and hold from −320 / −83 ps to −811 / −660 ps. On one data path the native launch clock arrived at 339 ps and the capture clock at 1,174 ps, against 877 and 724 ps with OpenROAD, whose early branch carries 29 delay buffers (490 ps). The native tree has no delay on that branch, passes a single latest arrival from one gated domain to the next, and sizes every stage at one reference input transition.

The builder is rewritten around four mechanisms. They are the parts of iCTS that fit; iCTS itself has no clock gates, no macro delays and no useful skew, so those stay PCRS's own.

  • A delay interval everywhere. Every subtree reports the earliest and the latest arrival at its sinks, the sink behind each, its input load and the transition it needs. A gated domain passes that interval up to the domain that feeds it, not one maximum. A macro pin and a wanted arrival (K5) enter as an offset of the interval.
  • Merge at the balance point. Two subtrees are joined where their intervals line up, not at the middle of their bounding box. The point is closed-form on the segment between them under the planner's wire model. What the wire cannot absorb becomes a delay stage on the early side, and a skew bound per clock says when to stop. This replaces the midpoint fit and the padding pass.
  • Several sites before "infeasible". A fork tries a few legal sites along the merge segment and toward its source before it is given up. This is the remote gate clone of the coralnpu tree swap, where only the midpoint was tried at 498 µm.
  • Propagated transition, then resize. Build once on estimates, time the tree with the transition that actually reaches each buffer, then size: first the buffers of the late branch, and only then delay on the early branch. A transition target bounds every planned stage.

Leaves are filled to the limit: the number of groups is pins / fanout rounded up, and pins are assigned under that capacity, instead of halving by count. The builder holds no global state and works on ids, so one instance per chunk can run at the same time.

B — Chunks

  • One subtree per chunk and clock. A chunk worker builds the tree for the clock sinks in its home, from a clock entry pin, with the builder of track A. It runs with the chunk's own placement (lhd synth chunk final), so chunks need no order among them.
  • The summary. A finished subtree is known to the top by its entry position, input load, needed transition, and the interval of arrivals at its sinks. It also says how much delay it could add or remove inside at low cost. That last range is what a later budget round would use.
  • The top. The same builder, with chunk entries as its sinks. It equalizes chunks by sizing and delay above the entries, and it takes useful-skew offsets between chunks from the boundary timing of the chunk flow.
  • A budget round, only if measured. If the top has to add more delay than a chunk could absorb inside for less, the top hands that chunk a target and the chunk rebuilds. Chunks still run in parallel; this is one extra pass over the chunks that got a target.
  • A change. Editing a chunk rebuilds that chunk's subtree and the top. The other chunks keep their trees and names.
  • Gates across chunks. A gate and the flops it drives in another chunk are joined at the top level, with the gate's interval; a clone of the gate inside the far chunk is a top-level decision.

C — The common centroid helper

A centroid is a point with members that are pulled toward it during placement. Today agents can move and align memories, PCRS pulls flops with its own anchors, and the floorplan stage has its own centroid groups. This becomes one helper.

  • Members. Cells, flops, memories, macros, and other centroids. A centroid inside a centroid gives recursive coordination: a bank of memories with its flop groups, inside a block.
  • Position. Free (it follows its members), at given coordinates, or relative to another centroid or to its chunk.
  • Layout of the members. Floating, a row, a column, or an array. It is a target before the final legalization, which may snap members to rows and sites.
  • Strength. How hard the members are pulled. It can be raised or lowered per centroid.
  • Stages and scope (K21). The same record is read at the plan, macro and final placement stages and by PCRS. At the plan stage the members are colors and macros of the whole design, and a centroid may span chunks. In a chunk's own macro and final stages the members are that chunk's cells, flops and memories, and a centroid does not leave the chunk. Clock leaf groups are of the second kind.
  • Collateral. A centroid is a record of the placement hints (livehd.placement.hints), next to the existing position, align, matrix and cluster kinds, with the same templates for repeated hierarchy. Groups PCRS forms by itself are generated state.
  • Clock fields (K20). A centroid whose members are clock sinks also carries the clock decisions for them: the drive of its leaf driver and its wanted arrival. A field set on a centroid that contains other centroids applies to all of them, so a whole block can be asked to arrive late in one record. Setting a field on a group PCRS formed turns that group into an authored one, with its members.
  • GUI and socket. Create a centroid from the selection, add or remove members, move a member to another centroid, set position, layout and strength, and export. The page draws each centroid with its members.

How the clock uses it. PCRS forms one centroid per leaf group once placement has spread, and again at each timing round, and pulls at a small default strength (K18). The hard leaf groups at the end are the same groups. A gate that drives a group is a member, so it sits with its flops. An agent that sees a group with a long leaf, a bad local skew or a critical data path among its members changes that one group. The pairwise anchors and their option are removed.

Independent chunk routing on ASAP7

On November 23, 2026, OpenROAD detailed-routed CNN chunk 0:190 (1,953 nets) and Eyeriss chunk 0:1916 (7,873 nets) in separate processes. Both checks reported full connectivity and zero routing violations after SRAM track alignment and cell legalization. A CNN reassembly check imported 1,952 fixed nets and routed the remaining net. The resulting DEF preserved the wire geometry of all 1,952 nets and passed both checks.

A PCRS buffer can straddle a chunk boundary even when its origin is inside the chunk. Its incident nets need top-level ownership when a pin would lie outside the worker's routing area. Whole-design timing requires extraction on the assembled routing.

Open points

  • Whether the top needs a budget round (H4 answers it).
  • What the clock fields mean on a centroid of retained pins: they have no leaf driver of their own, so a drive or a wanted arrival there has to act on the source or on a stage added for them.
  • What a layout means for members of different size (flops in an array next to a memory), and how far legalization may move a member from its target before the centroid reports it.
  • A balance point computed on wire delay moves when buffers are sized. How many build-and-time passes A4 and A6 need together.
  • Clock muxes and several clocks reaching one sink.
  • The reset tree of PCRS under useful skew: its flops are clock sinks like any other.

Risks

  • Model against routed clock. Until A2, every balance is computed on wires that are not the routed ones.
  • Balanced chunks, unbalanced whole. Each chunk can be well balanced and the chunks far apart; the top then pays in delay cells. H4 measures it.
  • Useful skew and hold. A schedule that is too aggressive moves the hold-buffer problem instead of removing it.
  • Pin density in flop groups. A pull raises pin density where the router already struggled; the weak default (K18) is the guard, and a centroid's layout must leave pin access.
  • Passes that edit the clock cone. The cone is still found separately in several passes.
  • Two arms with different cell lists. With integrated gates allowed for LiveHD only, every comparison has to say so.
