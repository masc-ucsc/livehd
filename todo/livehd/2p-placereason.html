Target flow for large designs: chunk-first, agent-driven

Principle: synth --gui must not synthesize and time the whole design flat before the agent sees a floorplan. That works only when the chunk memory budget is set to the machine's memory (the whole design is then one chunk). Today it is flat: on the real mixed_soc, ABC, a full-design STA and the flat physical view come first, and the fp gate arrives after about 30 minutes and 51 GB (9 minutes when the abc cache is warm).

  1. Compile and color. Colors (synthesis partitions) and the color graph, with no mapping of the whole design.
  2. fp_plan on the color graph: chunks, the macros as independent objects, boundary contacts. Today: the planner and the GUI/agent edits (move, split, fuse, density, interface copies) exist; they run after full synthesis.
  3. The agent works the plan. It may trigger macro_plan for one chunk, not the whole design. That synthesizes only that chunk's regions (incremental: the abc cache, so unchanged regions are not mapped again) and skips costly steps: no timing feedback and no resynthesis, unless it reuses a previous run that already had resynthesis incorporated. Today: lhd synth chunk init --budget 1GiB saves the source recipe and runs fp_plan. update replays synthesis and tracks changed packets, membership and shared IO. After accepting fp_plan, lhd synth chunk macro and final run selected exported homes from the CLI or chunk GUI. The live FP agent socket still lacks an in-gate worker for previews.
  4. Chunks and most macros decided. Macros that float between chunks are left for the final stage. Today: not seen as an option.
  5. Chunk IOs decided (one fixed contact per crossing net). Today: fp_plan supplies initial contacts. The chunk GUI and move-io can accept a macro measurement and invalidate incident homes.
  6. Final placement per chunk. Cells do not leave their chunk. Today: matches (no cross-chunk migration). Wanted: a porous flag that lets cells cross chunk edges, with the chunk IOs moving and adjusting as cells move.

Rules for the agent

  • Incremental. A chunk is synthesized once per content; moving or reshaping a chunk replaces it instead of re-synthesizing or re-placing it. A stored local placement is keyed by chunk content and kept relative to the chunk (synth.place.chunk.seed_cache, in progress).
  • Relative coordinates. Macro alignment and pins are relative to themselves or to their chunk. Absolute coordinates are chunk-absolute; die-absolute is the rare case. Today: frame:"chunk" for move and macro_scan and per-macro chunk coordinates exist. Pins and saved hints are still die DBU.

November 23 implementation and macro trials

The opt-in repeated-scope planner found four Eyeriss siblings with 493 members each and 29 glue members from the saved occurrence inventories. The same native check grouped two BlackParrot slices and 16 NVDLA banks. CNN and CoralNPU stayed in single homes with the downstream budget disabled.

Physical geometry edits can assign disjoint connected tile unions and reserve the remaining sites. Workers consume the exclusion rectangles, boundary segments and unchanged placement handoffs. The floorplan and chunk views show the physical SRAM footprint. A GUI or socket trial can exchange two equal-footprint SRAMs in one live worker. IO timing reports expose each measured region occurrence alongside the shared worst-case mapping environment.

Exchanging 20 SRAM assignments inside one Eyeriss cluster reduced whole-chip pin HPWL from 5.996970 to 5.991720 m. A fresh full global route used 7.254824 m versus 7.261395 m, both at zero overflow. Setup changed from −10.6493 to −10.6443 ns and hold from −0.4795 to −0.4883 ns. The SRAM worker connected all 69,938 nets and retained exact wire geometry for all 69,086 frozen nets. Its independent geometry check found 241 markers, down from 683 in the original worker.

The full-chip routed path to SRAM bank 6 also crosses the neighboring ClusterGroup 1_0. Moving six standard cells within that group improved global-route setup slack to −10.1403 ns and the clock estimate to 79.11 MHz, compared with 76.05 MHz for the original central strip. Hold was −0.4884 ns. Global overflow stayed zero, with 7.257384 m of wire and 5.994042 m of pin HPWL. A larger five-cell move dropped the clock estimate to 72.28 MHz. All four trials kept the same four equal worker boundaries, central glue, and PCRS clock tree. Full-chip detailed routing, extracted timing, and equivalence-checked private color variants remain open.
