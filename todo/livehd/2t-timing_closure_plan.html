2t-timing_closure_plan Timing forces in global placement: what remains of the timing-closure plan

Written 2026-11-15 from timing_closure_plan.md (design, 2026-10-24) and the closed 2t-timeclosure page (paused 2026-10-25). Nothing below is started. Option and step names are proposals.

The 2026-10-24 plan describes a single-threaded timing loop inside global placement (GP). It has a cheap timer, timing forces on the placer, early virtual buffers, a latched phase controller, and buffering and sizing during GP. Part of it was built as an experiment. Today the native GP timing loop runs only from tools/timeclosure_gp_probe; lhd cannot turn it on. The timer part has moved to 2q-fasttimer. This page keeps what is still pending and worth doing. It also lists what is dropped, and why.

What the experiments showed

The experiments ran on HighTide asap7 CNN and litepci, from OpenROAD's preCTS netlist, without resynthesis. The numbers come from the closed 2t page. Its evidence directory, repros/timeclosure_20261024/, is no longer in the tree.

  • Mixed, unconverged results. CNN, 20 GP steps with weights: OpenSTA setup WNS improved by 690 ps, but HPWL rose 18.6%. Over 40 steps WNS regressed by 135 ps. A run bounded by density overflow improved WNS by 319 ps, with HPWL up 48%. No run reached GP convergence, and no routed win was shown.
  • Legalization ate the gain. On the 40-step pair, legalization lost 1.85–2.26 ns of modeled WNS.
  • Timer coverage blocked most runs. Litepci aborted on effective-capacitance driver-fit failures, and strict legal references failed coverage on both designs. 2q's total-load model removes this failure class.

Steps

  • T1 — Move the timing forces to the 2q engine. Pin_weight_bank, Period_guard and Gp_timing_session take late::Result. After 2q step E, switch them to the engine's numeric pins, criticality and worst predecessors, and drop the late::Proxy and sdc_subset dependencies so 2q's cleanup can delete them. Exit: the same weights on the existing fixtures within tolerance; CNN and litepci run with no unsupported coverage.
  • T2 — One opt-in option in lhd. For example synth.place.timing_forces=true, with typed settings under it. Off leaves today's flow unchanged. Reject explicit settings that conflict with the legacy checkpoint and repair policies, so two timing loops cannot run at once. Move the probe's needed setup into the flow, then delete tools/timeclosure_gp_probe.
  • T3 — Keep the gain, bound the cost. Cap the driver-to-sink coefficient and the pin weights by their effect on HPWL outside the window. Turn on timing priorities in legalization under the option. Measure WNS before and after legalization on every run. Exit (proposal): the final legal WNS keeps at least half of the GP-modeled gain, and HPWL grows at most 5%.
  • T4 — Live gate controller. Feed timeclosure::Controller from GP samples and let it decide when timing forces start, replacing the fixed start and interval. Replay saved traces first, then confirm on live runs. Phases: initial, memory growth and RUDY, timing, end of GP. Phases 4–7 of the old plan are gone.
  • T5 — Early buffered delay for long nets. In the engine's wire model, time a net longer than the maximum unbuffered length L with the linear delay of an optimally buffered line (Otten–Brayton) instead of the quadratic RC delay. Fit the per-unit delay to what the resize engine actually inserts. Without this, the forces pull hard on long nets that buffering fixes later anyway. Exit: buffered nets rank the same before and after the resize engine runs.
  • T6 — Gap slots for gap-crossing nets (only if T5 shows these nets matter). For window nets with fanout ≤ 3 that cross a macro gap, choose the gap with buffering/graph_search. Reserve the buffer area in the density bins over that gap, and price contested gaps with PathFinder-style history costs on Site_reservations. Exit: fewer buffers legalized out of their gap, and no HPWL growth on nets outside the window.
  • T7 — Acceptance. Run end to end against the default flow (DREAMPlace timing-driven weights, the 1f loop, the resize engine) on HighTide asap7 CNN, litepci and minimax. Compare final legal WNS and TNS, routed WNS, HPWL and wall time, using the 2q R9 protocol. Flip the default only on a win.
  • T8 — Cleanup. Delete what is not adopted: the experimental gp_timing paths that T1–T4 do not keep, the diagnostic buffering/gap_forces carrier if T6 is skipped, and tools/timeclosure_replay if T4 is not adopted.

Dropped from the old plan

  • Buffers and LR sizing inside GP (old phase 7): a masked buffer pool, transactional materialization, and Lagrangian sizing (Chen–Chu–Wong, Flach et al., Sharma–Chu). After GP, the resize engine (synth.place.resize.engine=new) already does path-based sizing and buffering, with legalization, and 2q step G moves it to the new engine. Revisit only if T7 shows a gap. Vt swap already exists as synth.place.resize.vt_swap.
  • BEN for fanout ≥ 4, and full Hanan-graph negotiation: no design has shown a need.
  • Resynthesis during GP (old phases 4–5): owned by 2is-incsynth.
  • Post-route hold ECO and useful skew: left to the OpenROAD flow now that 3x-reroute is dropped. Hold before routing is a best-effort guard in 2q step J.
  • Timer, incremental updates, calibration and path-style factors: owned by 2q-fasttimer.
