2a-aegraph Acyclic e-graph cprop alternative

Build a second constant-propagation and peephole engine for LGraph. It is a Cranelift-style acyclic e-graph ("aegraph"): hash-consing, eager rewrites at insertion, union nodes, no rebuild, cost-based extraction, and elaboration back into the graph. It sits next to pass.cprop behind one knob, compile.cprop.engine, so the two engines run on the same compile schedule and can be compared on compile time, memory, node counts, synthesis area and delay, simulation speed, and LEC equivalence. A second goal carries the same weight: adding an optimization should take one short declarative rule (plus a fixture when the rule needs one). From that rule the build generates the matcher, its knob and counters, a differential fuzz test, a "fires on its example" test and a bounded proof obligation. Classic cprop stays the default and is not deleted. The work is split into Phase 1 (milestones A–I, which end at a decision point with an A/B table) and Phase 2 (J–O, planned only if Phase 1 passes gate G3).

Depends on

    • EPRP labels and the --set plumbing (merge_sets :818-833, check_known_set_passes :967, kSetPasses lhd/lhd_kernel_internal.hpp:313-339);
    • //pass/lec:lec_query (prove_equal);
    • the prplec equiv corpus (inou/prp/tests/equiv/);

Fix P7 (classic splits ? literals) in both pass/cprop and pass/bitwidth; it is a hard prerequisite for J. Rulings

Decided (authoritative)

  1. Width-agnostic algebra. LNAST and LGraph operations work over unbounded integers. Width matters only through explicit operations: Get_mask, Set_mask, Sext, and the frozen Concat width pairs. Every rule is written and checked over unbounded integers and needs no width information. Widths affect only the cost model and the elaborator's realization stamps.
  2. ? is a don't-care that the optimizer resolves, one literal at a time.
    • x*? may become 0 or x, so x - (x*?) may become 0.
    • With c=? and then a*c and b-c, the value of c is picked once and is the same at both uses.
    • c=? and d=? are independent literals.
    • Once a value is fixed (for example a = x*? resolves to x), every later use (a+c) sees that choice. Nothing picks again unless the value is still kept as ?.
    • No pass may split one literal into several, because that adds freedom. Merging two literals is a legal refinement, but it is an extraction decision and never a rule.
    • LEC's formal.lec.gold_x=ignore smears unknowns through every non-mux op (pass/lec/encode.cpp:3436-3448), and the value plane masks ? bits to 0 on both sides (encode.cpp:1199-1228). So LEC cannot detect an inconsistent choice. The engine writes a choice certificate (literal → chosen value or expression). LEC substitutes it into the reference for a strict check, and every literal becomes one shared symbolic variable (J, K).
  3. Cost depends on the target. In hardware, x << k is free wiring and always beats x*2^k; in software that is not obviously true. The same holds for a mux chain vs. a Hotmux vs. a collapsed mux. Extraction uses selectable cost profiles, at least synth and sim. Rules never encode a preference.
  4. Architecture.
    • An aegraph on LGraph, as an alternative path to pass/cprop, selected by a knob, so the two engines can be compared on speed and QoR.
    • Rule tags:
      • simplify: always better; it subsumes the original.
      • alt: the result is unioned with the original, and extraction decides.
      • choice: an alt tagged with the ? assumption it relies on; extraction enforces consistency.
    • These side passes stay outside the e-graph:
      • false-cycle cutting (a pre-pass);
      • muxctx path facts;
      • vector-mux window narrowing;
      • flop-enable folding and enableopt;
      • loop_hoist;
      • bitwidth's cyclic fixed point.
    • An optional simplify-only pre-pass on upass/LNAST is a later follow-up that needs its own measurement first. It is not part of this plan's milestones.
  5. Clean rule API. Adding a transformation must be simple, declarative and self-testing (see "Rule API").

Decided by the user, 2026-10-15 (were open questions Q1–Q5)

  1. Q1 — An out-of-range or disconnected Mux selection is ?. ? gives the most freedom, and every concrete value is a valid choice for it.
    • For Mux(s, p1..pn), a constant s ≥ n, or a selection that names a disconnected arm, yields a ? owned by that Mux node: one implicit literal per Mux, not shared with any other literal.
    • This removes the conflict between classic's two rules. The equal-arms collapse (cprop.cpp:911-922) is valid, because ? may be that arm. The fold to 0 (:961-983, :1323-1345) is also valid, but only as one refinement. The aegraph writes the out-of-range case as a choice ("any arm, or 0"), and extraction decides.
  2. Q2 — Div and Rem are for runtime verification only, unless the divisor is a constant.
    • a/b and a%b with a non-constant b are not synthesizable. They are legal for simulation and runtime checks. The engine does not try to optimize them, except where the division disappears:
      • b is a power of two, as a constant or as 1<<k;
      • b/b → 1. Dlop already makes x/0 all-unknown (../hlop/hlop/dlop.cpp div_op, "Division by zero is X"), so 0/0 is a ? that may be 1.
    • Division and remainder by a constant are synthesizable, and lowering them to simpler logic is an aegraph goal (rule group rules/div.rules, milestone G):
      • a/2^k becomes a shift and a%2^k a mask. These hold over unbounded integers only for a ≥ 0: Dlop rem_op truncates (the sign follows the dividend), so a negative dividend needs the rounding correction or a range guard.
      • A general constant divisor uses multiply-and-shift by a magic constant. That needs a bound on a, so it is an alt guarded by the range analysis. It is width-dependent only through that guard.
    • Div and Rem are pure, so they are hash-consed and CSE'd like any other op. is_computed_comb_op (cprop.cpp:266-268) and opshare (cprop_opshare.cpp:68-73) must agree on that.
    • Dlop makes both x/0 and x%0 all-unknown (../hlop/hlop/dlop.cpp div_op/rem_op, hlop b0a6142), as Verilog does, so both are a ? the engine may resolve. In LGraph an invalid/nil value is also treated as all-unknown. Only LNAST constant propagation differs: there, any use of nil is a compile error, because it is likely a source bug.
  3. Q3 — No type distinctions in LGraph. Every constant is a Dlop, and rules use Dlop semantics.
    • In LNAST a string is a valid type, but all inputs of one Mux must then be strings. Mixing them is a compile error before LGraph.
    • In LGraph, strings, integers and booleans are all Dlop constants that operate on each other (../hlop/hlop/dlop.hpp). Rules never special-case a type. A test such as "is this arm zero" calls Dlop::is_known_zero.
    • Consequence: classic's false_path_zero = v.is_known_zero() || v.is_string() (cprop.cpp:1732) is wrong. It rewrites Mux(s,"str",s) (that is, s ? s : "str") to s. This is P10.
  4. Q4 — A ? may resolve to a signal. Mux(s, a, ?) → a is legal because ? may be a, and Mux(s, a, a) == a. Expression choices (?q := a) therefore ship with constant choices in J, under three constraints:
    • one value or expression per literal, at every use;
    • the expression's cone does not contain the literal, so the result stays acyclic (checked at extraction);
    • for a partial literal (0ub1?), the range/known-bits analysis must prove the expression is within the literal's value set, or the arm is rejected.
  5. Q5 — A literal is one Dlop constant node. In LGraph it is a const node. In LNAST it is a const foo tree entry.
    • Identity is therefore the const node in its graph. Whatever produces const nodes defines the literals. Today tolg interns every ? with the same spelling and width in a graph into one node (upass/tolg/upass_tolg.cpp:1200-1217, pool ../hhds/hhds/graph.cpp:3033-3066). Same-spelling ?s in one graph are thus one literal. That is a legal merge.
    • Making one const node into several (P7, or any per-consumer create_const) is a split, and it is forbidden.
    • Plan assumption: a per-instance copy made by inlining, specialization or loop_hoist (inline_sub.cpp:382.., loop_hoist.cpp:212) becomes that instance's own literal, because each instance is separate hardware. Within one instance a literal never splits. The certificate is keyed by (graph, const node).
    • Optional, measured in J: exempt ?-bearing Dlops from interning in tolg, so two LNAST const entries stay independent and extraction gets more freedom. This is not required for correctness.

Pre-existing cprop issues found

landed 2026-10-15 (uncommitted) P1–P10 are fixed in the worktree, each with a test (P1/P3 documented and pinned under Q1). Extra bugs found and fixed on the way: Ror(0ub????) folded to 0, the sim runtime drew one ? literal twice at two widths (inou/cgen/sim_tune_rt.hpp), bitwidth sized a gapped Mux selector by edge count, and both sim backends gave b for a Div/Rem with only b connected. Still open: cgen Verilog emits a Get_mask of a ? constant as a separate literal (inou/cgen/cgen_verilog.cpp ~2361).

These defects are in classic cprop and exist whether or not the aegraph is built. Milestone A fixes them in pass/cprop (P7 also in pass/bitwidth), each with its own gtest or equiv fixture. Each fix also cleans up the A/B reference. All citations were re-checked against the current tree.

  • P1 — Out-of-range Mux selections fold to 0 (legal, but suboptimal under Q1).
    • try_collapse_forward collapses Mux(s, a, a, a) to a for any s (cprop.cpp:911-922).
    • It compares only the connected arms, so a disconnected arm (read as 0) is ignored too.
    • The constant-selector fold maps an out-of-range or disconnected selection to 0 (:961-983).
    • Under Q1 an out-of-range or disconnected selection is ?, so both rules are valid. The collapse is a correct choice, and 0 is one refinement. Classic is not wrong, only less free.
    • Fix: document the Q1 semantics at both sites. The constant-selector fold may pick any connected arm instead of creating a fresh 0. The aegraph expresses it as a choice.
  • P2 — Arity checks count edges, not port ids.
    • scalar_mux tests inp_edges_ordered.size() != 3 (:1529). The same-driver collapse right after it and the Set_mask lane factoring inside it (factor_set_mask_pair, :1541ff) read [1] and [2] as the two arms.
    • An n-ary Mux with pins 0, 2 and 3 (arm 1 unconnected) passes as binary and is rewritten as s ? p3 : p2. Selector 1 must give p2, but it gets p3. Q1 does not excuse this, because the selection is in range and connected. (The pins 0, 1, 3 case is legal under Q1, since selector 1 names the disconnected arm, which is ?.)
    • Fix: use is_two_arm_mux.
  • P3 — The Sext(x,1) selector bypass also applies to n-ary Muxes.
    • scalar_sext rewires every Mux port-0 consumer from Sext(x,1) to a bool01 x (:1767-1776).
    • That keeps truthiness, which is enough for a two-arm Mux. For an n-ary Mux, selector -1 (out of range) becomes 1 (arm 2).
    • Under Q1 this is legal (out of range is ?, and arm 2 is a valid pick), so P3 is downgraded to a note. Keep a test that pins the Q1 reading.
  • P4 — A single-input Div is forwarded.
    • try_collapse_forward forwards the lone driver of a one-input Div (:884-895), whichever pin it sits on. A Div with only b connected becomes b.
    • Fix: drop Div from that list, or forward only when the lone pin is a and a missing b has a defined meaning.
  • P5 — muxctx overwrites the proven code, so the CSE key changes between the two cprop slots.
    • Path pruning calls gu::set_proven(c.node, gu::kFormalOnehot) on a Hotmux (cprop_muxctx.cpp:247-250).
    • It only reaches Hotmuxes that are already exclusive (:141), so no obligation is discharged. But it overwrites any existing code (for example kFormalAssumeUnchecked), and it stamps structurally exclusive Hotmuxes that were unproven.
    • The Hotmux CSE key includes has_proven/proven_of (cprop.cpp:3301). cse_pass runs before muxctx::prune in one do_trans (:3553, :3561), so the second compile slot keys identical Hotmuxes differently.
    • Fix: set the code only when none is present, and keep existing codes.
  • P6 — Wrong strength-reduction formulas in pass/cprop/TODO.
    • a*(2^p+2^q) → (a+a<<q)>>(p-q) and the 2^p-2^q twin (TODO:77-84) shift right where they must shift left.
    • The correct forms are ((a<<(p-q)) ± a) << q.
    • Fix the text before anyone ports it.
  • P7 — Folds split ? literals (cprop and bitwidth).
    • For any fold result, including one with unknown bits, replace_node and replace_logic_node (cprop.cpp:1503-1530) make a new constant. Examples:
      • Get_mask(0ub????,[0,2)) → 0ub?? (:1441-1448);
      • the SHL, Ror, Rxor/Popcount, Set_mask, Sum, Or, And and Concat folds in replace_all_inputs_const (:1203-1499).
    • Bitwidth does the same:
      • bitwidth_rewrite.cpp:79 builds create_const(get_mask_op_opt(0,bits)) on a ? constant, one new spelling per consumer window;
      • bitwidth.cpp:290-299 can do it through a min==max range.
    • Sim draws the new spelling independently of its source. One literal then reads two unrelated values, for example an output and its own low slice.
    • Fix: fold only when the result is fully known, or when its unknown bits are exactly one whole source literal; otherwise keep the op. Picking a value for the source literal at every use is also legal (decision 2), but it is a choice, and it belongs to the aegraph's J, not to classic.
  • P8 — Missing cheap rules (also aegraph parity targets):
    • Not(Not x) → x. The only Not fold is the all-constant case (:1376-1380). The Not handling at :3075 is a mask rewrite, not a double-negation fold.
    • EQ(x,x) → 1, LT(x,x) → 0 and GT(x,x) → 0 for a non-constant x without ? bits. scalar_eq (:1833ff) only handles constant comparands, and the EQ arm in the collapse path is still a FIXME (:1004-1005).
    • A constant fold for Sext. replace_all_inputs_const has no Sext case, and scalar_sext (:1750) never folds a constant a.
  • P10 — scalar_mux treats a string arm as zero (Q3).
    • false_path_zero = v.is_known_zero() || v.is_string() (cprop.cpp:1732) lets Mux(s, "str", s), that is s ? s : "str", collapse to s. When s is 0 the result must be "str".
    • Fix: drop the is_string() term and use Dlop semantics only. Any other type special-casing in cprop is suspect for the same reason.
  • P9 — A stale comment. simplify_after_inline in lhd/lhd_kernel_formal.cpp (around :5182-5188) mentions a "normal cprop entry assertion" that no longer exists.

Order change: classic runs muxctx before mux_op_share, mux_share and the bit-reduction vectorizer (cprop.cpp:3560-3564). Here muxctx runs after extraction, so after the union producers of L. H and L measure the effect.

Core data structures (pass/aegraph/aegraph.{hpp,cpp})

  • E-node.
    • Contents: op, arity, inline operand class ids (up to 3; n-ary banks stored out of line), a 32-bit hash, a union link, the creating rule's id, and an optional assumption set of (literal, value) pairs.
    • Target size: 32–48 B per e-node. LGraph spends about 64–100 B per binary node (a 32 B NodeEntry plus about one 32 B PinEntry, ../hhds/hhds/graph.hpp:1531-1708).
    • The e-graph is freed after each graph.
  • Normalization before the hash lookup. Bank operands are sorted by class id, so commutativity costs nothing. Multiplicity is preserved: Sum(a,a)=2a and Xor(a,a)=0 keep both operands (cprop.cpp:3219-3222). Known constants are merged per op:
    OpConstant merge at insertion
    Sum, LT, GTadd as constants, subtract bs constants; one net constant on one side. A lone bs operand stays a negation and is never forwarded (cprop.cpp:886-893).
    Multproduct
    And / Or / Xorbitwise. And/Or idempotence is a rule, not normalization.
    EQ ("all operands equal")distinct constants fold the node to 0; equal constants dedup to one
    Rorany nonzero constant folds the node to 1; zeros drop
    Concatwidth pins are frozen comptime widths; no rule or analysis rewrites them (graph/cell.hpp:59-81)
    A constant with ? bits is never merged; it stays a separate literal operand. A node whose operands are all constant is folded by the constant analysis only when the result is fully known (the P7 contract).
  • Hash key.
    • The key is op + operands + color + synth_policy + proven (for Hotmux).
    • synth_policy is new and stricter than classic, whose CSE ignores it (cprop.cpp:3229-3330).
    • proven has to be in the key because muxctx::exclusive reads proven_of == kFormalOnehot (cprop_muxctx.cpp:41-50). Merging a proven Hotmux with an unproven twin would lose or forge an obligation.
    • LUT nodes, runtime_check nodes and non-exclusive Hotmuxes are not keyed. They are skeleton leaves (below), as classic excludes them from CSE (:3256-3258).
    • Names are not in the key, so rules still match through named nodes. The naming policy is applied at elaboration: keep the named twin, and when two named pins land in one class, keep both nodes (classic's skip, :3318-3323).
  • Literal identity.
    • A ? literal is one Dlop const node (Q5). Because the pool is value-interned, that node's identity is (graph, spelling, width).
    • Its class is a leaf that is never merged by value.
    • The engine never calls create_const on a ?-bearing Dlop. Interning would silently alias a new spelling with an unrelated literal, which is a merge nobody chose and makes certificate keys collide. This is enforced by a rulegen lint and by a runtime I() in the elaborator.
  • No rebuild. The engine rewrites in one forward pass; once a node is inserted it is never re-canonicalized. Cranelift's limits bound the rewriting, and each is a knob:
    • rewrite_depth=5
    • matches_limit=5
    • eclass_limit=5
    • extractor_fuel=500
    • a per-graph budget proportional to the number of input edges, which keeps cprop's O(V+E) contract (cprop.cpp:3545-3552).
  • Hotmux obligations.
    • A Hotmux that is non-exclusive (muxctx::exclusive false) or has runtime_check is a skeleton node.
    • The original node is kept: never merged, never deleted, its operands rewired to the extracted representatives. Consumers see it as a leaf.
    • It needs no new op. Two active controls give nil (../hlop/hlop/dlop.hpp:760-770), so the node is not a pure value.
    • This keeps the observable obligation (UnusedOverlapSurvivesForFormal).

Tagged unions (choice) — soundness rules

  • A tagged member contributes no facts to its class's analyses. Facts are computed only from untagged members.
  • A simplify rule may never subsume a class through a tagged member.
  • An e-node built by a rule whose match went through tagged members carries the union of their assumption sets. A member whose set is inconsistent (one literal, two values) is dropped when it is created.
  • Worked example: x - (x*?q) becomes 0. This is sum_cancel matched through the x [q:=1] member of Mult(x,?q), so the resulting 0 carries {q:=1}.
  • The union-invariant fuzz checks tagged members for equality only under their assumption set.

Extraction and elaboration

  • Cost profiles live in pass/aegraph/cost/{synth,sim}.cost.
    • Width W comes from the driver stamp when one is present, else from the range analysis, else from a profile default.
    • Cost is sharing-aware: a class with several parents is counted once. Without this, extraction would undo low_lane, mux_share and opshare, which add nodes on purpose to narrow bits.
  • ? consistency.
    • Each literal gets exactly one value across the whole extracted DAG. Candidate values come from the tags (usually three or fewer); extraction picks the assignment with the lowest total cost, then extracts with it fixed.
    • A literal with no chosen value stays its single ? pin.
    • Once literal L := v is chosen, the elaborator replaces every sink of L's pin with v. That includes comb sinks and skeleton sinks (Flop/Latch initial, Memory, Sub, IO, AttrSet). No use of a chosen literal may remain ?.
  • Root constraints.
    • A Latch din keeps its hold Mux (cprop.cpp:1689-1700, latch contract).
    • Hold regions that enableopt can see stay intact.
    • Concat is preferred where the pre-pass cut a false cycle.
  • Determinism.
    • Class ids follow forward-walk order. Ties break by cost, then by the creating rule's priority, then by class id.
    • No decision that affects the output may iterate an unordered or absl container. This covers extraction order, union order, rule firing, elaboration order and certificate order; absl seeds its hashing per process.
    • LEC structural identity and every cache hash the output.
  • Diff-elaboration, not a fresh body.
    • Rebuilding the body with GraphLibrary::replace_body_from (graph.hpp:3543-3560) would renumber every nid and attribute.
    • Reusing the original nodes that match keeps nids, names and srcids. Specialize's inherit_colors (pass/specialize/specialize.cpp:298-300) and the caches depend on that.
    • It also limits create_node calls. Each one bumps a library-wide atomic (graph.hpp:3818-3820, :4340-4352).
    • The elaborator uses the hhds idiom: snapshot out_edges(), call connect_sink for each sink, then del_node (cprop.cpp:820-856). It always creates a fresh operand pid and never shares a sink.
    • Before connecting a consumer sink to a representative, it checks closes_loop_self_edge (cprop.cpp:824-828). On a hit it keeps the original node for that sink, because forwarding there would create a false compact-loop carry.
  • Width stamps.
    • Class members are equal as unbounded integers. So when a new representative replaces an original class root, the root's bits/pin_signed stamp is a valid realization, and the elaborator copies it.
    • An interior node the elaborator creates is stamped from the range analysis when that bounds it. Otherwise it is left unstamped, as classic make_node leaves it, which is legal only where bitwidth follows.
    • This matters because an unstamped non-Mux output is read as one bit by the LEC encoder and cgen (cprop_opshare.cpp:468-473).
    • The elaborator never changes the stamps on skeleton pins (IO and register widths are declared, not derived).
    • In preflight mode (G) any unstamped interior node is refused.

Knobs and which callers follow them

  • Selector: compile.cprop.engine=classic|aegraph|aegraph+classic|none (default classic).
    • aegraph+classic runs the aegraph then classic in each slot, to measure what classic still removes.
    • none skips the engine. It isolates the input for timing and gives the failure baseline.
    • It is registered as a pass.cprop label (pass/cprop/pass_cprop.cpp:17-30), so lhd describe and unknown-flag checking work.
    • An eager value check sits next to the satopt stages check (lhd_kernel_common.cpp:978-984).
    • compile_graph_passes swaps the method in both slots (:1575-1586).
    • A second flag, compile.cprop.engine_slots=both|first|second, swaps only one slot, which separates the pre-legalize run from the post-legalize run.
    • Pass_cprop::optimize refuses any non-classic value that reaches it, because the kernel does the dispatch.
  • Method pass.aegraph in pass/aegraph/.
    • Add a kSetPasses row {"compile.aegraph","pass.aegraph",List::all}.
    • The driver (lhd/lhd_kernel_compile.cpp:1802) merges the compile.cprop set and then the compile.aegraph set.
    • The pre-legalize loop_hoist=false gate (:1808) becomes method == "pass.cprop" || method == "pass.aegraph".
    • pass.aegraph registers low_lanes, loop_hoist and engine with INTERNAL help, so they are hidden from lhd list options. check_known_set_passes rejects compile.aegraph.{low_lanes,loop_hoist,engine} with "use compile.cprop.X". Each flag has one spelling.

Rule API — the clean way to add a transformation

Layers

  1. Runtime (pass/aegraph/runtime/match.hpp):
    • combinators in the style of LLVM PatternMatch (m_Sum, m_as, m_k, m_rest, ...);
    • Ctx;
    • Results.subsume/add/choice;
    • fuel-limited iteration over class members.
    Generated code calls this layer. Native rules call it directly.
  2. Rule files (pass/aegraph/rules/*.rules, S-expressions). These are the normal way to add a rule. The rule group is the file name.
  3. rulegen, a C++ host cc_binary that links //graph:cell and hlop.
    • It checks op and pin names against the Ntype tables and folds constant expressions with Dlop.
    • The Bazel macro aeg_rules() in tools/aegraph.bzl turns the rule files into C++ (with #line back to the .rules file), a registry, tests and proof obligations.
    • It is C++, not Python, so the build path stays off the Python/PATH traps.

Syntax

(simplify NAME [PRIO] LHS [:let (v EXPR)...] [:when GUARD...] => RHS [OPTS])
(alt      NAME [PRIO] LHS [:let ...] [:when ...] => RHS [OPTS])
(choice   NAME [PRIO] LHS [:let ...] [:when ...] => RHS1 [?q := C1] | RHS2 [?q := C2] [OPTS])
OPTS := :example TERM | :expect (PROFILE lhs|rhs)... | :fuel N | :proof bv|int|lean|none | :doc "..."

Patterns

PatternMeaning
xBinds an e-class; a repeated x must be the same class.
_Wildcard.
0, -1Literal constants.
(k c)Binds a fully known constant.
?qBinds a ? literal leaf, with its identity.
(Op p...)Positional, with exact arity, so (Mux s f t) never matches an n-ary Mux.
(Op lo: p)By pin name.
(as p.. ..r), (bs ..)Bank multiset; ..r binds the rest. A bank not written in the pattern must be empty, so (Sum (as x)) does not match Sum(as x)(bs y).
{attr}On a right-hand-side constructor, sets a registered contract attribute.

Guard and constant-expression builtins: + - * min max log2 pow2? popcount mask_of < <= = and or not. Each builtin has three implementations in one rulegen table (runtime Dlop, SMT, Lean), so a guard means the same thing everywhere.

Examples

;; rules/arith.rules
(simplify sum_drop_zero (Sum (as 0 ..r) (bs ..s)) => (Sum (as ..r) (bs ..s)))
(simplify sum_cancel    (Sum (as x ..r) (bs x ..s)) => (Sum (as ..r) (bs ..s)))  ; a - a + c -> c
(simplify not_not       (Not (Not x)) => x :example (Not (Not $x)))             ; P8
(alt mult_pow2_shl (Mult (as x (k c))) :when (pow2? c) => (SHL x (k (log2 c)))
     :expect (synth rhs) :example (Mult $x 8))

;; rules/mask.rules   Get_mask(a,lo,hi) = bits [lo,hi) of a, unsigned
(simplify getmask_getmask
  (Get_mask (Get_mask x (k l1) (k h1)) (k l2) (k h2))
  :let (lo (+ l1 l2)) (hi (min h1 (+ l1 h2))) :when (< lo hi)
  => (Get_mask x (k lo) (k hi)))
(simplify getmask_getmask_empty
  (Get_mask (Get_mask _ (k l1) (k h1)) (k l2) (k h2))
  :when (>= (+ l1 l2) (min h1 (+ l1 h2))) => 0)

;; rules/mux.rules    Mux s p1 p2 = s ? p2 : p1
(alt mux_chain_hotmux (Mux s (Mux t c b) a) :when (bool01 s) (bool01 t)
  => (Hotmux{proven} s a (And (as (Not s) t)) b c) :expect (synth rhs))

;; rules/choice.rules ('?' literal = optimizer's choice; implicit (admits ?q C))
(choice mult_unknown    (Mult (as x ?q)) => 0 [?q := 0] | x [?q := 1])
(choice mux_unknown_sel (Mux ?q f t)     => f [?q := 0] | t [?q := NONZERO])
(choice mux_unknown_arm (Mux s a ?q)     => a [?q := a])        ; Q4: Mux(s,a,a) == a

;; rules/div.rules    Q2: only constant divisors are worth optimizing
(simplify div_self      (Div x x) => 1)                          ; 0/0 is ?, so 1 is legal
(simplify div_pow2      (Div x (k c)) :when (pow2? c) (nonneg x) => (SRA x (k (log2 c))))
(simplify rem_pow2      (Rem x (k c)) :when (pow2? c) (nonneg x) => (Get_mask x (k 0) (k (log2 c))))
(alt      div_const_mul (Div x (k c)) :when (range_bounded x)
     :let (m (magic c (range_hi x))) (sh (magic_shift c (range_hi x)))
     => (SRA (Mult (as x (k m))) (k sh)) :expect (synth rhs))
  • {proven} carries a contract (pairwise-exclusive controls). rulegen emits an extra obligation for it: And(s, And(Not s, t)) == 0 under the guards.
  • NONZERO lets admits pick any nonzero value the literal allows. For a two-arm Mux any nonzero value selects t (cprop.cpp:964-965), so 0ub1? admits 2.

Tags, priorities and limits

  • simplify (Cranelift's subsume): the first match in priority order replaces the class representative.
    • Until H the lint is structural: the right-hand side must have no more nodes than the left, and no right-hand-side op may be priced above the left-hand root by any profile table.
    • From H the lint uses each profile's cost; a failure says "make it alt".
    • This is the Cranelift lesson: its eager-rewrite misses came from rules that went against cost.
  • alt: every match up to matches_limit is unioned with the original, and extraction picks one.
  • choice: an alt whose members carry (literal, value), under the tagged-union rules above.
    • rulegen adds an implicit (admits ?q C) guard to each arm.
    • A right-hand side may not contain a fresh ?, because that would split a literal.
    • Merging literals is never a rule.
    • Expression assignments (?q := x) are allowed (Q4), under the three constraints in the Rulings.
  • Priorities: an integer, default 0, higher tried first. Ties break by file order, then by group order fixed in BUILD, so the order is deterministic by construction. rulegen warns when two rules at the same priority overlap.

Analyses (C++, each declared with a spec)

;; rules/analyses.decl
(analysis bool01 (x) :type bool  :spec (and (<= 0 x) (<= x 1)))
(analysis range  (x) :type Range :spec (and (<= LO x) (<= x HI)))
(analysis known  (x) :type Known_bits :spec (= (And x MASK) VAL))
(pred known_zero_outside (x m) :uses known :spec (= (And x (Not m)) 0))
(pred nonneg        (x) :uses range :spec (<= 0 x))
(pred range_bounded (x) :uses range :spec (and (<= LO x) (<= x HI)))   ; finite LO/HI
(simplify ror_bool01    (Ror (as x)) :when (bool01 x) => x)
(simplify and_redundant (And (as x (k m))) :when (known_zero_outside x m) => x)
  • Implementations live in pass/aegraph/analyses/<name>.cpp and register with AEG_ANALYSIS(name, Fact, make, meet).
  • Merging the facts of a class's untagged members is a meet: ranges intersect and known bits union.
  • The :spec serves two uses: it is the hypothesis of each proof obligation, and it is the fuzz sampler. bool01 samples {0, 1}; range samples inside [LO, HI].
  • A generated soundness fuzz checks, on evaluated values, that an implementation returning true implies its spec.

Native rules

Region producers such as low_lane, mux_share and opshare cannot be written as patterns. They use AEG_NATIVE_RULE(name, tag, root_op, prio) { ... } on the same runtime. They get counters, knobs, the union-invariant fuzz and the example test, but no automatic proof obligation, so they need an equiv fixture.

Cost profiles

;; cost/synth.cost                        ;; cost/sim.cost
(cost synth (SHL _ (k _)) 0)              (cost sim (SHL _ (k _)) 1)
(cost synth (Get_mask _ _ _) 0)           (cost sim (Mult _ _) 3)
(cost synth (Mult _ (k _)) (* 2 W))       (cost sim (Hotmux ..) (* 2 (arity)))
(cost synth (Mult _ _) (* W W))
(cost synth (Mux _ ..a) (* W (arity)))

Profiles use the same pattern compiler. The only link from a rule to cost is its :expect assertion. If someone retunes a table and silently undoes a rule's purpose, that assertion's generated test fails.

What one rule produces with no hand edits

  • Registry. A Rule_id enum and rule_table[] (name, tag, priority, file:line, proof tier), plus dispatch per root Ntype_op. The dispatch is a switch now; a decision trie comes later if profiling asks for one.
  • Knobs. rules_disable, rules_only, max_rewrites and explain accept the rule name with no list edit.
  • Counters per rule. tried, matched, applied, subsumed, limit-rejected and extracted. "Extracted" comes from the creating-rule id and is the key QoR signal for alt rules. Counters live in thread-local arrays and are merged after the workers join.
  • Census.
    • With lhd.stats=true, an aegraph-rule diag record per rule (the sweep script parses it the way prplib parses bitfuzz-summary).
    • A generated pass/aegraph/RULES.md table, kept fresh by the rulegen staleness test.
    • lhd describe renders only static labels, so it does not show the census.
  • aeg_fuzz_<group>_smoke (about 1000 samples per rule, fixed seed, AEG_FUZZ_SEED to override, the seed echoed on failure, well under 1 s in -c opt):
    • LHS/RHS equality under the Dlop evaluator on unbounded values up to about 130 bits, negatives included;
    • ? refinement mode: every known LHS bit is kept, and no new ? appears;
    • choice arms with their assignment substituted;
    • "example fires";
    • the :expect checks (from H).
  • Union-invariant fuzz (aeg_union_smoke, independent of the rules, so it also covers native rules). Random depth-3 trees are inserted with one rule enabled, and every member of a class must evaluate equal (a tagged member only under its assumption set). A companion check compares Dlop with the LEC encoder on random single ops, so the two semantics cannot drift.
  • aeg_prove_<group>_smoke, the bv tier.
    • The LHS and RHS become micro-LGraphs proven equal by lec::prove_equal.
    • Inputs at width w ∈ {1, 2, 3, 8, 16, 65}, sign- or zero-extended. Both sides are evaluated and compared at width ≥ 2w + max_const_bits + 2 with no truncation. Otherwise a rule that only holds modulo 2^w would pass.
    • Guards are miter assumptions.
    • At most 8 instances per rule by default, picked by a fixed per-rule hash; AEG_PROVE_FULL=1 runs the full sweep.
    • Per-group wall time is asserted under 25 s in opt.
    • Per the test ruling, timeout or Unknown is PASS, and Refuted is FAIL with the counterexample printed as a rule instance.
    • This is bounded evidence, not a proof for all widths. The census records bv-swept, int-proven (N) or native-unverified.
  • Lints that fail generation:
    • an unknown op or pin;
    • an unbound right-hand-side variable;
    • a simplify that does not reduce cost;
    • a fresh ?, or a create_const of a ?-bearing value;
    • a choice arm without an assignment;
    • an unregistered {attr} contract;
    • an unknown profile.

How to add a rule (checklist)

  1. Add one entry to pass/aegraph/rules/<family>.rules with an :example. Add :expect if it is an alt meant for one target.
  2. For an analysis-guarded or native rule, also add an equiv fixture to the existing prp-lec-* tier:
    • a base file inou/prp/tests/equiv/aeg_<rule>.prp;
    • a header-only variant aeg_<rule>_1.prp with :set: compile.cprop.engine=aegraph and :compile_grep: aegraph-rule <rule> .*extracted=[1-9].
    No BUILD edit is needed, because the group is wired by file name. For a plain pattern rule the fixture is optional; the in-process example test already shows it fires.
  3. Run bazel test -c opt //pass/aegraph/.... That covers the lints, the example test, the fuzz, the union invariant, the bv tier and any fixture.
  4. If the rule needs a fact that no analysis provides, add an analysis with a :spec rather than writing C++ inside the rule.
  5. Never edit enums, knob lists or documentation tables by hand.

  • A vs B: lhd lec --impl lg:B --ref lg:A --top T --set formal.effort=proof --set formal.timeout=60 --set lhd.incremental=false. Exit codes: 0 means proven, tested or timeout; 10 means REFUTED with a counterexample; 7 means REFUTED otherwise.

Risks and mitigations

  • False unions turn local inconsistencies into global miscompiles. Classic's two out-of-range Mux rules were the live example, until the Q1 ruling turned both into one choice.
    • Q1–Q3 are decided. Encode Q1 as a choice and never as two simplify rules. Fix P2, P4 and P10 in classic first.
    • The union-invariant fuzz, the dbg check=unions mode, and deterministic max_rewrites bisection catch the rest.
  • A tagged member leaks into facts or a subsume. That would be a silent ? split. Mitigation: tagged members feed no facts and allow no subsume, assumption sets propagate, and the fuzz checks equality under each member's assumption set.
  • The cost model undoes narrowing. A node-count extractor would discard low_lane, mux_share and opshare. Mitigation: width- and sharing-aware cost from I on, pred_aig in G2, extraction counters per rule, and G3.
  • LEC cannot check ? consistency by default. It smears unknowns and masks them to 0. Mitigation: gold_x=symbolic plus the certificate (K), and the refinement-mode fuzz from E.
  • Interning merges same-spelling literals, and classic and bitwidth split literals (P7). The merge is legal (Q5), but it loses freedom. The split is a bug. Mitigation: P7 in A, the no-create_const-of-? lint, and the optional interning exemption measured in J.
  • Elaboration churn. New nids break specialize's assumptions, and atomic contention in create_node slows parallel workers. Mitigation: diff-elaboration that reuses original nodes, and the create count in the profile record.
  • Compile time and memory at XS scale. XSCore has 17.6M nodes summed over definitions, and the 190 MB Verilog compile peaks at 19.6 GB. Mitigation:
    • Cranelift limits and a per-graph edge budget;
    • 32–48 B e-nodes, with the e-graph freed after each graph;
    • large designs already in G1, not first seen at G4.
  • Nondeterminism. Mitigation: ordered class ids, a total tie-break order, no output-affecting iteration over hash containers, and the determinism check in every sweep.
  • LEC difficulty changes even when outputs are equivalent, for example when a false cycle is removed. Mitigation: classic stays in the LEC preflight until O, and per the test ruling a timeout or Unknown at a small budget counts as PASS.
  • Test time. Mitigation: fuzz and proof tests split per rule group, a bounded instance count, prove-on-mismatch in the differential fuzzer, and high iteration counts behind environment variables. Targets: under 30 s in opt (goal under 4 s) and 90 s in dbg.
  • Knob spelling traps. Nested flags would be silently dropped by merge_sets. Mitigation: flat labels only, one spelling per flag, and eager value checks.

Non-goals

  • No upass/LNAST pass in this plan. A simplify-only LNAST pre-pass is a later follow-up, and only if the A/B numbers justify it.
  • Classic cprop is not deleted or replaced as the default here. Even at parity, Bitwidth_rewrite, enableopt and satopt keep using its internals.
  • No full equality saturation, no rebuild, and no cyclic e-graph through state. Bitwidth's fixed point stays in bitwidth.
  • muxctx, loop_hoist, enableopt and flop-enable folding are not reimplemented inside the e-graph.
  • No consistency guarantee for ? in emitted Verilog.
