2r-route Legroute as the final routability step of placement

Plan written 2026-11-15 from a question-and-answer session, after the URoute calibration (3u-uroute) and the SAT legroute work (2l-legroute) both failed their goals. This page replaces those two pages; their history is in git. Step A is done (2026-11-15: DREAMPlace ea2d85a, LiveHD 3c45f532c, scorer kept as tools/routetruth); steps B to E are not implemented yet. Names of options, targets and directories are proposals. Updated 2026-11-15 with the first routed-cohort snapshot (Cohort evidence). The items C1–C5 come from that snapshot and were accepted as written on 2026-11-15.

Remove the learned congestion model (URoute) and the SAT-based legroute stages, and rebuild legroute as one step at the end of placement: a FastRoute-style global router and a per-grid-cell legality check alternate until the placement is as dense as it can be while still routable. The router proves that routes fit the track capacity of every grid cell. The legality check proves that the standard cells the router assumed inside a grid cell can really be placed there, which a global router never checks. Both proofs hold back a reserve for the buffers that CTS and hold repair add after placement (C1). The name legroute stays: it is still route-aware legalization.

Why

  • URoute did not earn its place. Trained on 121 routed HighTide layouts (10 designs, asap7 / nangate45 / sky130hd, macros, high-density and aspect-ratio stress cases), the best recipe reached parity with RSMT on map correlation for unseen designs (Pearson 0.662 vs 0.659) and stayed below it on Spearman, SSIM and hotspot overlap. RSMT ordered placements of the same design by routed congestion far better (pairwise concordance 0.90–0.98 vs 0.4–0.7). In the placement loop URoute left lfsr, minimax and liteeth bit-identical to RSMT while costing 6–59× the wall time (about 15 s per call on x86).
  • RSMT cannot trigger. The same study measured RSMT's utilization at about 0.33 of the routed value, so its util > 1 overflow test almost never fires. Placement needs a signal with a real capacity scale. A global router on the track grid has one.
  • SAT legroute did not close. Local and Final never produced complete native routing on SHA3, minimax or ternip (2l-legroute). The code is about 20k lines plus about 11k lines of tests, and Plan carries more machinery (cell boxes, partitions, admission) that only exists to feed it.
  • LiveHD placements are hard to route. With OpenROAD routing LiveHD's placement and the platform's global-route derating applied, nangate45 minimax, nangate45 liteeth and sky130hd liteeth all fail global routing on congestion (Step B results). These are the cases the new step must fix. The first measurements (112K and 38K detailed-route violations at iteration 3) came from a handoff recipe that skipped that derating; see B1.
  • Most of the router exists. Legroute Plan already routes the converged placement with a negotiated A* router with rip-up, has an opt-in port of FastRoute's L/Z patterns (fast_route_pattern.cpp), a window fit check (assess_window_fit) and can feed its congestion back into DREAMPlace (plan.route_model=coarse).

Decisions from the cohort (accepted 2026-11-15)

#TopicDecision
C1Repair reserveThe site capacity of a grid cell is its free sites minus a reserve for CTS and hold buffers. The edge capacity keeps the clock charge and adds a share for the nets of those buffers. A grid cell is frozen only if it fits under the reduced capacity, so the "dense" of R8 means dense under the reserve. The first version uses one fraction for the design, set from measurement (gemmini needed 8% more cell area; NVDLA/partition_a took 4.6% and was still short). It becomes a per-grid-cell value, for example by flop count, only if step B shows that the buffers cluster.
C2Per-layer testThe grid already keeps capacity and demand per layer (Grid::layers, layer_dem). The freeze test and the final report use the per-layer numbers, at least for the low layers that pins escape through, not only the 2D sum.
C3Measure after repairThe gate reads the backend's congestion report both before and after timing repair, and the legalization result after each insertion phase. A step that passes before repair and fails after it has not passed.
C4Outcome by stageEvery gate run records the first stage that fails: load, placement, handoff, floorplan, CTS, global routing, detailed routing or timeout. For detailed routing it keeps the marker type, layer, box and net of each saved report. This replaces a bare violation count as the first thing compared.
C5Cohort in the gateThe cohort designs join the gate as a second table. With the step on, no design may stop at an earlier stage than without it, and no completed design may be lost.

The step

Input: the converged global placement (not legal), the fixed die, core, rows, macros and power grid, and the routing tracks. Output: a legal placement and a global route that fits it, or a report of the grid cells that could not be closed.

  1. Grid. Build the routing grid with a grid cell of N tracks per side. Edge capacity comes from the real tracks minus power, macros, blockages and the clock charge, kept per layer (C2). Cell capacity is the free row sites inside the grid cell minus the repair reserve (C1).
  2. Assign. Every movable cell belongs to the grid cell that holds it. A grid cell over its site capacity sends its excess to nearby grid cells that have both site room and routing slack (minimum displacement, timing-critical cells last).
  3. Route. Route all nets on the grid: pattern seeds where enabled, then negotiated rip-up and reroute. Later rounds reroute only the nets whose pins moved.
  4. Prove and freeze. A grid cell whose cells fit under the reserve and whose edges have slack on every layer is legalized with greedy plus Abacus restricted to that grid cell. If the legalizer succeeds, the cells are frozen there and the routes through it are kept.
  5. Repair. For grid cells that fail (sites or routing), free only the cells in and around them, inflate where routing is short, and resume global placement with everything else fixed. Then go back to step 2 for the freed cells.
  6. Finish. Stop when every grid cell is frozen or the round budget is spent. Legalize what is left with the regular legalizer, route once on the legal pins and report overflow per layer, unfrozen grid cells, the reserve left in each grid cell, displacement, HPWL and timing.

Decisions that stay open inside the step, to be settled by measurement: the grid-cell size, how much routing slack a grid cell needs before it is frozen, the size and shape of the repair reserve (C1), how far the repair region extends around a failing grid cell, and whether a frozen grid cell may be reopened when a neighbour cannot close.

Direction after this

Once the step is on by default, the next goal is PCRS: native distribution of power, clock, reset and scan chains, as an alternative to OpenROAD CTS and a stepping stone to native routing in LiveHD. The SAT attempt at native routing failed, so that needs a new plan. It is recorded here only to keep the direction clear; it is not part of this task. The cohort supports the order: on asap7, two of the three LiveHD failures that reached the backend stopped in OpenROAD's CTS and hold repair, before any signal was routed.
