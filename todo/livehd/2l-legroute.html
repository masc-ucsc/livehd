2l-legroute Route-aware legalization with a SAT solver

legroute is route-aware legalization: it replaces the LiveHD legalizer (opt-in, synth.place.legroute=true). Cells are grouped into cell-boxes; a SAT solver places the boxes of each region on a tile grid so that the box-to-box routes fit the tracks by construction. Routability is guaranteed instead of guessed from density targets, so the design can be packed denser and the routes stay simple (straight and L). Quality order, given a routable design: WNS, then TNS, then wire length and vias.

Redesign 2026-10-30: SAT placement replaces the min-cost snap, the bin spill and PathFinder The guide lists current decisions only. Partition vocabulary (grid, region, bin) follows 3g-partitions.

Building blocks

NameDefinitionMain properties
cell-box A small group of directly connected cells treated as one custom cell (2 × the widest flop, two rows).
  • A cell never leaves its box. Nets with all pins inside a box are internal: legroute.final routes them, and they lower the pins the solver deals with.
  • Built by connectivity (agglomeration c1_T20_pa_cd4). Fill is high but not 100%: the leftover space is for later sizing.
tile One box-sized location of a grid aligned to the rows and sites. Holds at most one box. Fixed cells lower its free width; macros (with halo) block whole tiles and snap to the tile grid unless they have a custom location.
track capacity Per tile and per direction: the horizontal tracks of all H layers summed, and the vertical tracks of all V layers summed.
  • Layers are not in the SAT model. After placement the track assignment gives each trace its layer and track by criticality, inside the space the SAT guaranteed.
  • Power stripes and the clock spine take their tracks first (reservations; both get nicer constraints later). One track per layer stays free for pin access.
  • Each box lowers the capacity over its tile by an approximate in-box demand derived from its internal nets (not their plain count: in-box nets can be H or V and discontinuous).
region A busorder region. It is the unit of the SAT model. The region size comes from the region creation parameter (max cells), not from legroute. It is tuned so that legroute.local on sha3 takes about 5 minutes on one thread (soft target; larger regions trade time for freedom).
virtual IO pin Where a cross-region net crosses the region boundary. Fixed while a region is solved; a route from a box to it ends on its tile.

Passes

PassWhat it does
legroute.local
  1. Boxes. Build the cell-boxes and their in-box demand.
  2. Routability (baseline SAT). Per region, any box may take any free tile. The only constraints are placement (one tile per box, one box per tile) and track capacity per tile and direction for the chosen route shapes. Any answer is routable.
  3. Timing tiers (incremental SAT). Constraints that help timing are added one at a time under assumptions and kept only when the solver stays SAT and the timing proxy improves (see below). Starting points may change for speed, but every answer keeps the baseline's guarantee.
  4. Traces. The chosen shapes become metal with vias: straight and L legs on real tracks (layer by criticality), bends and the vias between the legs included.
legroute.final Each cell-box alone (independent, so parallel): pick the cell positions inside the box so it routes (a small SAT or search), then connect its pins to the lanes that reach or cross the box, one trace at a time, adding metal and vias inside the box only. Boxes that fail are placed for real and legroute.local runs again with their actual in-box demand.
legroute.global (global_route, built 2026-11-01) Every region-crossing net routed over the tile grid ahead of the regions: a tree of tile edges by shortest paths on the edges with room, most critical nets first. Its boundary crossings become the regions' hops, its runs through the regions holding none of the net's pins become fixed pieces; the regions holding its pins route it themselves. See legroute.global.

All or nothing: unless every net closes, no signal wire is written and an external router starts from the legal placement.

SAT model (legroute.local)

  • Solver: Sat_solver with CaDiCaL (fastest in the 2026-10-29 benchmark), incremental, assumptions for the tiers. No flow or CP solver.
  • Placement: a box's column and row in order encoding; a tile literal per (box, free tile); exactly one tile per box, at most one box per tile.
  • Connections: a 1-1 net is one connection; a 1-n net is the set of connections from its driver to each sink (a Steiner tree approximated by L-shapes). A virtual IO pin or fixed cell is an endpoint at a fixed tile.
  • Shapes: each connection picks H-first or V-first (one bit); a straight connection is both. Each leg covers every tile from end to end in its row or column, the boxes' own tiles included (an external pin always needs a trace in its box).
  • Overlap is free within a net: per tile and direction one literal per net, implied by any of its connections' legs covering the tile.
  • Capacity per tile edge: a cardinality constraint per edge between two neighbor tiles over the nets whose covers hold on both (the nets crossing it), against the physical tracks of the narrower tile less the edges the routes fixed ahead cross. The in-box wires stay off the edge counters (the boxing pin limit and legroute.final's job). The bounds are lazy: an edge's constraint is added only when an answer overflows it, and the model is solved again incrementally (capacity_lazy).
  • Seeded search: the fallback placement (boxes on their nearest fitting tiles) is tried first as assumptions (sat_seed); an UNSAT there drops them and the free model continues with what the solver learned.
  • Later, as tiers or exceptions: trivial Steiner shapes for 1-n nets (all sinks on one row or column, or two crossing axes); adjacent 1-1 nets cost no track (in-box metal).

Pre-placement and policies (options, off by default)

  • Fixed ahead of the SAT: the top critical boxes on their timing tiles; 1-n trees, highest fanout first, skipping a net that would leave a tile with less than half of its tracks free, until the region's model estimate is under 10M clauses; IO pre-routes (pin to pin, and pin to one box to pin with the box held under the track). Fixed demand over a tile's tracks drops trees, then unpins critical boxes.
  • Two policies per region, in parallel: the options and a second set (sat_alt) that fails differently (more fixed: fast UNSAT; fewer fixed: timeouts). A region keeps a routable answer, the shorter weighted routes when both are.

Routability probe (GOALPlace-style inflation, off by default)

  1. After GP converges, before bus order: small probe regions (about 1,200 cells, no virtual pins) run legroute.local with a short SAT limit; regions at or under 50% box fill are taken as routable. With probe.real the probe is the real legroute's twin instead: the real region mesh with bus-ordered virtual pins, refined at each round's placement, every region solved.
  2. Cells of the regions without a routable answer widen in x by r = box fill / target, at most 1.5. The target is the median fill of the probe regions that solved, at most 0.6. The top 10% of cells by timing weight keep their size (GOALPlace's 10-quantile guard).
  3. GP resumes from the converged placement (its density weight, fillers in the whitespace) for a bounded number of iterations; the widths come back with the cells' centres kept.
  4. No room for the inflation at the GP target density: a generated core grows (place.allow_increase_area, on by default) and lhd synth runs again at the utilization that fits, the GP target density lowered with it (utilization + 0.05), at most twice. A fixed core (die, core or floorplan given) keeps its size and the inflation scales down.
  5. Then the real run: bus order and pinalign, critical boxes and 1-n trees pinned (the trees avoid a DCGP-style congestion potential: a Poisson-smoothed map of per-tile routing demand over tracks), two policies per region in parallel, the better answer kept.
  6. If that run still leaves a region without a routable answer, placement stops: not routable.

Measured so far: 1-n nets are typical (61–88% of boxes sit on a net with 16 or more sinks in every design tried). Routability follows box fill: regions under about 65% fill solve, at 75% or more most fail. Box fill follows the GP target density (fill is about 1.15 × density), not the utilization.

Density push-back (core growth from an impossible start)

With place.allow_increase_area (on by default) and a generated core, placement never stops as not routable while it can grow the core. Three signals push the utilization down and placement runs again: the legalizer cannot close the global placement (DREAMPlace: insufficient row space; the timing seed and the final placement retry in place, utilization times 0.8); legroute's boxes do not fit their tiles (times 0.8); a region has no routable answer (the densest failing region's box fill pushed toward 0.7, at most a 0.92 step). The GP target density follows the utilization (utilization + 0.05). At most eight restarts. A fixed core (die, core or floorplan given) still stops as not routable.

legroute.global: the crossing nets routed ahead (2026-11-01)

What partition_c showed (328k cells, 632 regions, 274 regions without an answer): the regions that fail are pass-through regions, thin slivers between macros that hold two boxes and carry two thousand nets of other regions. The hop chain chooses a boundary tile with hop room for every crossing, but room on a boundary tile says nothing about the tracks across the region behind it, so a 6-tile channel was asked to carry what only a 60-tile one could; the IO pre-route ran out of room (263k feedthroughs skipped) and the SAT got the rest. The fix is a global router for the crossing nets (pass/place/legroute_global.cpp, synth.place.legroute.global_route=true):

  • Graph: the tile grid; an edge between two neighbor tiles carries at most global_share (0.5) of the narrower tile's tracks in its direction, so the regions keep the other half for their own routes. The edges inside a region holding one of the net's own pins cost length only and take no room: that region's SAT routes the net there, nothing is reserved. A boundary crossing costs one extra tile, so a path along an irregular boundary does not hop back and forth.
  • Nets: every net with endpoints in two or more regions (its boxes' placement tiles, its fixed pins, the hop tiles of its busorder virtual pins, which it crosses for free). Most critical first, then the short nets (few ways to go) before the long ones (they detour). Each net is a tree grown by multi-source Dijkstra from the driver's tile to the nearest pending endpoint, within the bounding box plus global_margin (8) tiles, grown four times and then to the die when no path fits; the cost of an edge is 1 + 2·use/limit. A net without a path keeps the hop chain (counted as global_unrouted).
  • Hand-off: a tree edge between two regions is a hop (both tiles are endpoints of their regions, as before); the tree's edges inside a region without any of the net's pins are split at hop and branch tiles into straight runs, two runs make an L piece (Global_piece, reserved on its edges by edge_reserve, emitted as a fixed connection, the region's model skips the net).
  • Cost: 0.3 s for sha3's 3,951 crossing nets; seconds on partition_c.

Results: sha3 @0.70 samples in the option sweep table (rows global_route). partition_c (328k cells, 632 regions, 8 threads): 163 regions without an answer against 274 with the hop chain, 437M clauses against 1.05G, 147k cells unroutable against 191k, the IO pre-route skipped 58k feedthroughs against 263k; the router itself took 1,092 s on the first binary (a whole-die retry per unroutable net; bounded since) and left 3,420 nets to the chain, which is where the remaining failures sit: macro-channel regions with 0 to 30 boxes and hundreds of chained pass-through nets.

Regions most at risk first, and cuts by net cut (2026-11-01)

  • Risk order and fail-fast. The regions solve in order of risk (box fill plus the share of the region's tracks that the routes fixed ahead took: hops, pieces, trees, IO pre-routes), so the long solves start first and the threads end together; with fail_fast (set when the caller restarts the placement on any failure: the probe and the strict push-back) the first region without an answer ends the solve and the density push-back iterates at once instead of after every region.
  • synth.place.region.cut=mincut. The macro mesh forced a cut line at every macro edge and IO bus span: partition_c started from 1,959 rectangles, merged back to 670, 300 of them under 50 cells, and the thin channel regions between macros are exactly the ones the SAT cannot route, since a boundary along a macro's channel leaves the channel's routes to two regions and hops. With mincut no line is forced; each split takes the position in the middle half of the longest side that the fewest nets cross (nets with cells on both sides, weighted), so a macro sits inside the region whose cuts surround it and its channel is one region's problem. The boundary pins already keep off macro interiors. partition_c with it: pending.

The cut bound, regions as chips (results), domino cells (2026-11-01, later)

Regions as chips: the dump had a bug, now fixed. Nine of sha3's twelve region DEFs aborted in OpenROAD's detailed router ("Maze Route cannot find path") on a boundary net: the hop pin sat within half a pin width of a die corner and its rectangle left the die, so it had no access point. The dump now treats a track whose pin would leave the die (two pitches clear of a corner) like a taken track and moves the pin inward. With that, every sha3 region routes as a chip of its own (pin access + global route 5 s, detailed route 3 to 11 minutes per region, a few end-of-line violations at the edge pins); the chip-level detailed route of the same placement is 285 s for all twelve, so the region-as-chip time is inflated by the fixed edge pins and is not the number to quote.

The cut bound: unsat before any solving. With a window of two tiles, a net whose endpoints are held on both sides of a tile-column (row) boundary must cover one of that boundary's edges, so the nets forced across a boundary cannot exceed the summed tracks of its edges (the model's own k per edge; a 2-pin net between boxes that may sit side by side across the boundary takes no track and does not count). solve_region checks every boundary after the windows are final and returns unsat with the boundary named; the same bound from the placement windows is the leading term of the risk order (cut ratio, then box fill, then the fixed routes' share). On the crossbar it rejects the three unsat stripes in 0.01–0.02 s instead of 2.5–8.3 s of solving ("359 nets forced across column boundary 10, 153 tracks"); the three timeouts stay timeouts (their demand is just over the tracks and the windows leave the bound unprovable). Unit test CutBoundIsUnsatBeforeSolving; report field sat_cut_regions. scratchpad/cpsat/region_cut.py prints the same demand per cut for any dumped run.

Why the crossbar fails: region shape, not the model. syn_xbar32x8 cut into full-width horizontal stripes (20×12 tiles) puts every input bus across each stripe: the worst vertical cut of stripe 2 is crossed by 542 nets against 12 rows × 19 H tracks (M4+M6; 34 with M2), stripes 0 and 4 need 31–33 per row-tile edge, the three unknown stripes 22–23. sha3's regions sit at 10–20 (all SAT). The min-cut region cut does not help here (7 of 11 regions fail against 6 of 14), because a bus crosses every cut equally; the next criterion is the cut ratio (forced nets over the tracks across the cut) when choosing where and in which direction to split, so a bus becomes pass-through traffic for narrow regions instead of internal traffic for wide ones.

partition_c with min-cut cuts: 343 regions (670 with the macro cuts), 212 SAT / 57 unsat / 74 unknown (131 failures, 38%, against 163 of 632, 26%), L3 5,060 s against 2,951 s, 179k unroutable cells against 147k. Fewer, larger regions in the macro channels time out more. Cuts stay macro by default; the ratio criterion above is the follow-up.

Domino cells through legroute. The provisional domino profile (pass.usyn.physical=domino_provisional) emits two-row masters (0.54 µm tall, 1.0–1.1 µm wide on asap7: half a tile). legroute now takes a two-row movable cell as a tile-tall box: it takes the same sites in both rows of its tile (tall_layout in legroute_boxes.cpp: widest first into the leftmost span free in both rows, the single-row cells of the box pack beside it), lands on the tile's bottom row with that row's orientation (the rail-twin sync at the end of placement picks the master its rows need), and single-row flops alone set the tile width. The placement no longer refuses busorder regions or anchors the two-row cells ahead when legroute is on (place.cpp, the kernel's usage check); satlegalize still refuses them, so these runs use synth.place.satlegalize=false. Unit test TwoRowCellsPlaceAsTileTallBoxes. Two macro-design fixes came with it: the generated core flipped rows (N/FS) only when every cell was X-symmetric, and a memory block (minimax's register file, one block under USYN) is not, so its rows were all N and no two-row cell could match shared rails (block macros no longer decide the flip); and the final stage's frame import now accepts a two-row cell's rail twin as the same cell (the macro stage had already switched it). Larger designs with macros (cnn, partition_c)

The next targets are HighTide's cnn (69 FakeRAM hard macros, four of them 0.3 mm² each) and NVDLA partition_c (two FakeRAM macro types through the NVDLA RAM wrappers, the other RAM sizes as flops), both as slang blackbox interfaces with their LEF and Liberty (scratchpad/cpsat/run_design.py). The plan as they are iterated, in the order the data will likely ask for it:

  1. Standard flow and legroute at the same cores, routed by OpenROAD: where the standard flow sits and where the bisection lands, with macros as exclusion areas on the tile grid.
  2. legroute.global: the critical paths that cross regions get their long traces reserved on the high metal (M6/M7) ahead of the regions, so the per-region SAT sees them as fixed endpoints and the routes do not meander around macros.
  3. Routing speed: the SAT already runs regions in parallel; the next costs are the hop chaining and the fixed-tree pre-placement on designs with thousands of regions, and legroute.final's in-box router.
  4. Post-route WNS: the tiers judged by routed timing, the coupling tier for long critical traces, and the first and last cell of a critical path at its pins.
  5. Region boundaries: pinalign run with busorder as one pass, combined with the SAT alignment of boundary cells (satalign), and busorder's pin pitch matched to a tile edge's track capacity, which is what limited sha3.
  6. Region size: larger regions (or a region grown around a failing one) for the cases a cut cannot fix, now that the model is small enough.

Region cuts (busorder regions)

  • No net dips into a region through one side with zero or one cell inside: a cell whose nets all leave through one side moves across it, and a pinless RSMT piece with all its crossings on one boundary line is dropped (place.region.cleancuts).

Rules validated against a routed witness (2026-10-31)

The method: route a legroute placement with OpenROAD (same core, M2–M7), count its wires in the tile grid (scratchpad/cal/route_census.py), apply our counting rules to those wires (rules_check.py), and solve the SAT with every box frozen on that placement (legroute.freeze=true). A rule that rejects a routing that exists is wrong; simplifying a wrong model is pointless. On sha3 at utilization 0.52 OpenROAD routes legroute's own fallback placement with zero overflow and zero DRCs; the old model said 0 of 8 regions, UNSAT in under a second even frozen. Three rules were wrong, each about 2×:

  1. Count per tile edge, not per tile. 73% of the real H runs stay inside one tile (pin stubs); per-tile distinct nets are 2.3× the per-edge count. (Now the only counting; the per-tile model is gone.)
  2. The in-box charge stays off the edge counters. The wires that stay inside a tile (mean 14.5 nets, max 42) fit on their own; adding our in-box charge (mean 6, max 14) to the edge count rejected 1,275 of 3,094 edges of the real routing. The in-box budget is a boxing rule (pin limit) and legroute.final's job. (The option to charge them on the edges is gone.)
  3. Fixed routes reserve the edges they cross, not whole tiles. A hop, a fixed tree or an IO pre-route took one track from every tile of its path, i.e. two or three edges per crossing; at the boundary tiles the hops cluster (up to 15 per tile) and left 0–5 tracks for the region. The UNSAT cores of the frozen model were all region-edge tiles with a fixed endpoint. Now a hop takes its boundary edge only and a trunk the edges along it.

A fourth, in the hop chaining: a region-crossing net joined a region to the net through the first adjacent region already joined, in region-id order, so chip-spanning nets (minimax's 17-pin buffer trees) were pulled through regions holding none of their pins, stacking 30+ hop-to-hop straight runs on one row (minimax region 7: UNSAT in 0.5 s while OpenROAD used 8–12 nets there). The join now prefers regions holding the net's own endpoints, nearest first.

Search: the fallback placement (boxes on their nearest fitting tiles) is tried first as assumptions (sat_seed); the dense sha3 regions that timed out at 180 s are SAT on it in 5–13 s, and an UNSAT costs little while the solver keeps what it learned. OpenROAD on the resulting sha3 placement: zero overflow, zero DRCs, WNS −392.7 ps / TNS −470k against the satlegalize baseline's −394.5 / −466k on the same core (10 GHz SDC): timing competitive before any timing tier.

With the fixes the frozen witness placement is SAT in every sha3 region (1–13 s each), the unfrozen SAT routes 8 of 8 sha3 regions and 11 of 11 minimax regions (every crossing modeled), and lazy bounds (capacity_lazy=2: a tile edge's bound is added only when an answer overflows it, re-solving incrementally) cut the model from 74M to 13M clauses. Every region-boundary crossing is modeled (hop_share 1): a pass-through net's chained hops avoid the rows and columns earlier pass-throughs loaded (hop_spread, either L orientation), which is what let the last dense sha3 region solve. Still open: the in-box routing is no longer checked by the SAT; shapes (Z, trunks) made no difference once the counting was right.

Robust by construction (user, 2026-11-01): whatever the mesh deals a region, the algorithm must adjust: push cells out of a region that does not route, or reshape it; a pathological placement that puts everything in one region should still end in a routed design, only a worse one. Landed as legroute.spill_rounds (off by default): after the regions solve, each region without a routable answer (a model UNSAT, no answer in time, or more boxes than fitting tiles) pushes about spill_fraction (0.2) of its boxes into the regions around it, the crossings are redone and only the regions whose boxes, fixed boxes, hops, global pieces, trees or IO pre-routes changed solve again (a per-region signature keeps the others' answers), up to spill_rounds times. Which boxes leave and where they go is a min cut, as the user asked (2026-11-01, "a very fast approximate that runs only inside the region"), with the refinement that the region and its neighbours form one graph so the cut also decides the neighbour: the region's movable boxes are the nodes, each receiving neighbour is contracted to a terminal Tj, and a terminal S stands for what stays. A net is a star of edges from its first box to its other boxes (weight 100 × the net's timing weight over its endpoint count less one, so a net costs about the same however many boxes it joins); a net reaching neighbour j pulls its boxes to Tj, a net with a fixed endpoint in the region or an endpoint past the neighbours pulls them to S. Each neighbour's side is its isolating s-t min cut (VieCut's push-relabel, third_party/viecut, Tj against S and the other terminals), so the netlist decides which boxes leave and to which neighbour, connected clusters together. A uniform pull of every box to the neighbours is bisected to the smallest value that moves at least the wanted count; a box two cuts claim goes to the neighbour its nets reach most, and a neighbour takes at most its room: the fill that evens it with the failing region (never past nine tenths of its tiles), the next ring of regions joining when the neighbours are short of room. A failing neighbour receives too: the two sides of a hot boundary fail together (the hops are the same on both sides), and a box crossing it toward its nets takes its hops with it. Each moved box lands on the nearest free tile it fits in its new region (the SAT then places it within its window). The data behind the mechanism: abc and usyn CMOS sha3 have the same fan-out mix and net reach, usyn is only 20% larger and its mesh has 25% more regions and 46% more hops; what fails is region-scale (cut bounds, boundary tiles), never the cell-boxes. Alternative placements for a failing region (plan, 2026-11-01)

Found while measuring (2026-11-01, late): the final legroute runs without bus-order virtual pins. The kernel runs the region refinement (bus order, boundary pins, pin alignment) as its own placement call (refine_regions: optimization pending, region_refine) and the terminal placement that runs legroute is a later call where boundarypins.enabled is off, so refine_final_boundaries never runs there, boundary_pins is empty and legroute's regions.pins is empty ("2 regions, 0 virtual pins" on lfsr with busorder on; the sha3 runs show the same path). Bus order therefore shapes the placement (cells settle toward ordered pins during the refinement) but legroute's crossings are plain hops chained through the regions, and probe.real fell back to the small pinless probe in every run so far (the "real probe" results above are really the old probe with the real-run budget). Fix, before any pin-dependent conclusion: carry the refinement's mesh and pins to the terminal stage (the refinement writes regions/region_mesh.json and regions/boundarypins/pins.tsv; the terminal placement reads them into final_regions and regions.pins, and the probe's real mode uses them too), with the hash covering the pin set.

The user's plan (2026-11-01, latest): per failing region, several ways to make an alternative placement, tried while the SAT works on the region, measured on average across designs, then ordered. Three families plus the density lever:

  1. Noise: every movable box's home moves by a uniform random offset of up to window_tiles tiles inside the region (a new seed per round). Cheap, no model of why it failed.
  2. Shuffle with anchors: a few boxes of the region's densest 4×4 patch move to the centre of its sparsest patch and keep a one-tile window (anchors), so the SAT must build around them; at the GP level the same with the cells anchored for a resume (probe.shuffle, landed).
  3. Topology-shape-metrics stretch: the region's box graph loses edges (never those of nets with a fixed endpoint, a hop or an IO pin) until every node has four or fewer, highest degree first; the reduced graph is drawn orthogonally on the region's free tiles (breadth first from the node of highest degree, each node on the free tile nearest the centroid of its placed neighbours: ports north, east, south and west, so connected boxes sit adjacent and 1-1 nets go free), and the drawing's tiles are the new homes. If the SAT still fails: the drawing seeds a region-local GP resume (the other cells boxed in place) for a few iterations and the result is tried; if it fails again, the removed edges return and GP iterates once more before the last try.
  4. Checkerboard relaxation (user, 2026-11-01, latest): a relaxed model of the failing region first: the edges a checkerboard of tiles owns carry no capacity bound (half the capacity clauses gone, the metal over or into those boxes untracked) and the boxes homed on those tiles may move checker_window tiles (8), the others twice the window, so the solver explores far positions without the clauses blowing up. An UNSAT relaxed model says the region needs less density, not another placement. A SAT one seeds the full model: its tiles become the homes, every box gets a window of four, and capacity counts on every tile. Landed as the checker family; in the unit test it moves four of six boxes next to the boundary, past the windows of two, and routes a region the cut bound rejected.
  5. Density: the GOALPlace inflation brings a region from about 0.8 toward 0.6 fill step by step (probe.real once the pins are carried over), and at each step a failing region tries the families above before the next step down.

Orchestration. In legroute's round loop a region without a routable answer generates its candidates (box homes, per-box windows) and solves each with the round's fixed hops (no hop choice, so the neighbours' answers stay consistent), in parallel threads within the round's budget, the first routable candidate in priority order being adopted (its homes replace the boxes' homes, its answer stands); only then the spill or the shift. Later, speculatively: the candidates start when the region's own solve starts, so a failure costs no extra wall time. Options: legroute.alternatives=noise,anchor,tsm (the order is the priority), alt_ms (budget per candidate, default the region budget); report fields per family: tried, rescued, seconds.

Timing tiers (built 2026-10-31)

After a region's routable answer, each tier's constraints are tried in batches of 8, most critical first (a net is critical for the tiers when its slack rank is under tier_fraction, 5%), with every box held on its tile by assumptions, so the answer moves only where a constraint needs it. An UNSAT core loosens the held boxes in it (first to within one tile, then free inside their windows); a core with no box drops the constraints in it; a batch out of rounds (tier_rounds, 8) is dropped. Kept constraints become units for the batches and tiers after, the lazy capacity bounds keep every answer routable, and all tiers share tier_ms (30 s) per region, so a dense region keeps its base answer. The tiers (place.legroute.tiers, in order; the first three are the default):

  1. adjacent: a critical 1-1 net's two boxes are neighbors (in-box metal, no lane).
  2. pins: a critical net's box on or beside the net's fixed tile (virtual pin, hop, fixed cell).
  3. home: a critical box within one tile of its placement tile.
  4. near (off): a critical connection's two boxes within one tile of each other.

Measured on sha3 (OpenROAD detailed route of the placement, zero DRCs in every case; the satlegalize baseline on the same core is −394.5 ps / −466k): no tiers WNS −395.0 ps / TNS −473.8k; adjacent + pins + home with the boxes freed outright on a core and no budget (446 of 675 kept, the densest region spending its whole 180 s) −388.0 / −472.0k; the same three tiers with the two-stage loosening and the 30 s budget (518 of 675 kept, 67 s wall) −383.6 / −469.7k, the default; with near added (1,193 more candidates, 197 kept, the batches starving the tiers after it) −415.1 / −478.8k. The proxy (the tier nets' length, −1.1% with near, flat without) did not predict any of this: the boxes a batch loosens drift inside their windows, and what they lengthen is not in the proxy. So a tier earns its place by the routed timing, not the proxy.
