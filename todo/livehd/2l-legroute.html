2l-legroute Native routing closure in two steps

Plan for offline review — implementation pending

Goal. Make LiveHD legalize and route without requiring OpenROAD. Joint regional SAT placement and routing should reach at least the density OpenROAD can route, with substantially lower total runtime and comparable routed timing: WNS within about 4%, then TNS, wire length and vias. OpenROAD supplies comparison results and known routable references. The production flow remains native.

Immediate objective. Make legroute.final close ordinary connections through a consistent plan → local → final handoff. Build cellboxes, predict their individual routing reservations, find a feasible coarse placement and route, materialize routes to box edges, then move cells inside each box to finish its connections. This page defines the proposed work; historical run logs and superseded experiments belong in the history and feedback.md.

Two steps to closure

StepRouting scopeCompletion condition
1. Ordinary connectionsConnect every required internal cellbox net, external cell pin and die IO except the explicitly deferred clock/reset connections.Every participating net is connected in the emitted native geometry; placement, metal, vias and PG interactions pass physical checks. Report this as ordinary-net closure, with the deferred set visible.
2. Clock and resetAdd clock/reset distribution and all deferred pins using the same reservation and access contract, including their actual layer and spacing requirements.Recheck every ordinary, clock and reset connection together. Only this complete result qualifies for the final all-net density, routed WNS and runtime comparison.

One scope manifest for every pass. Identify deferred terminals and distribution nets from declared clock/reset roots, timing constraints and connectivity through the distribution cells. Record exact names and reasons; a broad name regex must not silently exclude functional logic. Mixed-use nets retain their non-exempt terminal obligations. Preserve the netlist and cells. In step 1, deferred routing is omitted consistently from plan demand, local allocation and Final closure accounting. Any retained clock/reset metal in a control remains a physical obstacle and is reported explicitly. PG, fixed obstacles and technology rules remain active in both steps.

Reset stays false-pathed for timing. This timing exception does not remove its physical connection requirement in step 2. Step-1 timing comparisons use the same declared clock model and reset exceptions on both flows; they are provisional until clocks are physically routed.

Current L5 layer/track assignment belongs to local's physical handoff: coarse directional capacity alone is insufficient to declare local complete. Final must receive metal and access resources that agree with the solution admitted by plan and local.

A different reservation for each cellbox

Build the box first, then estimate its demand. Start from small connectivity groups that fit the legal rows/sites and retain room for cell movement. Use internal-net topology, internal and external pin counts, external pin multiplicity, cell types and pin shapes, possible cell arrangements, box fill, and candidate arrival layers to predict routing demand. A universal M2+M3 allowance is only a calibration control.

The reservation starts as per-layer capacity budgets plus candidate routing and access domains. Plan uses their capacity summary; local and Final refine the domains into compatible physical intervals. Local commits crossing metal and edge ports while preserving choices for Final's exact interior allocation. The budget protects space for internal wiring and for external pins to reach box-edge ports, including via cuts, landing widths and intermediate-layer links. Upper-metal arrivals may need staggered vias and short lower-metal wires. Demand follows the actual arrival layer and pin access; a clock or reset label alone does not determine the layer chain.

Use relative to the requesting connectionAccounting
Own destination metalReuse already connected own-net geometry and charge its union once. A disconnected piece of the same net still needs a connection.
Other local destinationsPreserve their access windows; their metal blocks conflicting connections just as other foreign metal does.
Foreign transitAccount for the occupied intervals and physical clearances while leaving compatible access alternatives for the box's pins.
Mixed transit and destination branchesTrack the role of each piece; serving a local pin does not make every segment of that net free to use.

Share positions when intervals permit. Different nets can use different lengths of the same track subject to spacing rules. Protect the required landing/link footprints rather than dedicating a whole track to every destination. Candidate access windows must be mutually compatible across the box's connections, not just individually reachable. Deduct PG and fixed obstacles once; preserve layer direction, width, spacing, cut rules and any active nondefault routing rules in every pass.

Shared contract. Store stable box membership/version, legal placement domain, participating pins/nets, per-layer reservation, candidate edge-port/access domains and selected geometry. Plan, local/L5 and Final consume the same resolved contract and occupancy accounting. Moving a box re-evaluates it against the destination tile; changing membership, cell types, port choices or arrival layers invalidates affected demand and access estimates. Internal nets consume real resources even though they do not cross a box boundary.

Calibration target. Seek at least 99% first-attempt cellbox completion on held-out samples covering different designs, pin/topology classes and densities, with small reservations. Measure failure tails by class as well as the aggregate. This is a predictor target to validate, not an achieved guarantee: every remaining failure still needs repair before a design passes. Recorded OpenROAD track usage is a successful reference footprint, not the minimum number of extra tracks Final needs.

Plan: placement, cellboxes and FastRoute iterate together

  1. Prepare the physical frame. Load the scope manifest, rows/sites, macros, PG, pin geometry and technology constraints. Keep the DREAMPlace/DREAMGoal session alive for timing-aware recovery.
  2. Build cellboxes and reservations. Group the current cells, check box fit, and estimate internal/external routing and access demand. Provide plausible edge-port domains rather than committing early to an arbitrary pin position.
  3. Route the current candidate. Use native FastRoute-derived routing on the current box pose and port domains, with the per-box reservations reflected in directional, layer-aware capacity. Return connected per-net corridors, branches, attachments, arrival layers, edge loads and localized failures. FastRoute guides placement; the placer or a bounded cell-movement heuristic changes the pose.
  4. Repair and repeat. Localized pressure triggers targeted cell movement, spacing or inflation. Broad pressure triggers wider spreading or permitted core-area growth. Run at least 20–50 meaningful global-placement steps with timing feedback per recovery slice; use 50 as the initial evaluation setting. Release region IO constraints for broad redistribution, and retain only compatible ones for localized repair. Rebuild affected cellboxes when regrouping is beneficial, recompute reservations, and rerun FastRoute after placement, membership, area or resource changes. Area growth beyond a configured threshold also reruns macro_plan; smaller changes still require macro-fit checks.
  5. Admit and partition. Require every participating global route to be resolved, every edge within capacity, and cell/routing density at or below the default 0.90 ceiling. Screen box fit, access support and region size before SAT. Any failing global edge blocks admission; an average cannot hide it. The ceiling is an admission policy, not a routing proof or a second deduction of the same reserve. Only then cut connected regions along the routed trees and seed their interface domains.

Regions may be nonrectangular connected meshes. Penalize critical crossings while allowing necessary cuts; size regions for short useful searches. Preserve the route witness through refinement while allowing bounded alternatives inside its corridors. A cellbox edge port terminates a connection at a box; a region virtual IO joins two regional solves. Both carry stable net identity, layer/geometry or candidate interval, access support and dependency versions.

Local: movable boxes, real routes and supported edge ports

  1. Solve the hardest regions first. SAT jointly moves cellboxes among their admitted tiles and selects connected routes inside plan's corridors. Preserve the freedom to arrange member cells later inside their boxes; regrouping belongs to plan.
  2. Respect the reservation while choosing metal. Materialize and check physical track intervals, layer transitions and landing footprints. Foreign transit may cross a box only through allowed space. A destination branch stops at a cellbox edge port with a compatible reserved path to its pins. A mixed branch may both cross and serve the box. Layer assignment cannot consume access space promised to Final.
  3. Repair movable interfaces transactionally. First solve with the current region pins; if needed, jointly move eligible pins and boxes. Send new interfaces to unsolved neighbors and invalidate all affected solved neighbors. Re-solve the dependent neighborhood and restore poses, pins, metal and reservations together if the repair fails. Box-edge port changes also invalidate their access contracts.
  4. Validate the handoff. Independently check decoded connectivity, capacities, physical occupancy and mutual access support before committing regional routes. Return named blockers to plan when bounded local repair cannot resolve them.

Keep iterative regional feasibility attempts, including model construction and retries, below 60 seconds; start with a roughly 10-second probe. About 10 million clauses is a soft complexity goal, not a hard cutoff. Record SAT, UNSAT, UNKNOWN and model errors separately. A timeout does not establish unroutability and does not, by itself, justify area growth.

Final: move cells to complete the box

  1. Search placement and access together. Within the box's legal rows and sites, try cell shifts, reordering and legal orientations to bring pins near their assigned edge ports and usable tracks. Use a small bounded joint search; exhaustive arrangements are useful for tiny boxes and calibration. Optimize successful connection first, then timing and wire cost.
  2. Complete internal and external wiring. Extend the arriving destination metal toward the chosen pin positions, reuse connected own-net pieces, and add the needed short wires and vias, including staggered layer transitions. Route every participating internal net under the same reservation. Keep foreign crossings and committed boundary geometry valid while moving cells.
  3. Validate and commit. Check cell legality, PG/obstruction clearance, full metal/via rules, same-net physical unions and complete connectivity before committing a box's contribution. Revalidate the assembled net across all boxes and interfaces. Pending taps that later roll back are not successful connections.
  4. Return precise repair information. Report the pin or join, layer, candidate and blocker ownership, rule, attempted placement/access alternatives and any measured resource shortfall. Distinguish budget exhaustion, exhaustion of the represented candidate graph, malformed geometry and physical conflicts.

Final first retries in-box placement/access choices within its budget. Needing a different edge port, crossing route or larger reservation sends a request to local and invalidates affected neighbors. Needing a different membership, more placement room or a new global corridor returns to plan for regrouping or placement recovery and another FastRoute check. Reuse unaffected results only while their versions remain valid. A whole-net maze fallback remains a diagnostic escape path; routinely losing the planned tree must be repaired at the handoff rather than hidden by increasing global search budgets.

MilestoneDeliverable and check

2. Cellbox demand and calibrationPer-box/per-layer reservations and access alternatives after grouping; bounded in-box search on representative boxes. Compare a uniform control against adaptive demand, measure first-attempt completion and reservation cost, and retain failing examples.
3. Plan and local share the contractFastRoute consumes the same resources local/L5 materialize. Exercise placement recovery, regrouping, port movement and neighbor invalidation. Require valid global routing before regional SAT and a physically supported handoff before Final.
4. Step-1 Final closureJoint in-box placement/routing plus structured repair closes every ordinary connection. Check emitted geometry independently; report first-attempt and repaired box success, remaining failures, area and time per stage. An aggregate 99% box rate is insufficient for design acceptance.
5. Step-2 full closure and QoREnable clock/reset, recalibrate affected reservations, and rerun plan/local/Final as needed. Recheck all connections and measure complete routed WNS/TNS, density and total runtime against matched OpenROAD runs.

Use LFSR for fast iteration, then SHA3 and minimax, followed by partition_m, partition_c/o/p, CNN and other HighTide designs below 60k cells. Compare identical netlists, physical constraints, PG, timing policy and thread counts. Push requested density upward and measure where automatic recovery settles; the eventual full-route point must be at least as dense as the matched OpenROAD reference. Include every placement/reboxing/recovery iteration in elapsed time. Final emits explicit scope and closure counts so partial runs cannot be mistaken for complete routed designs.

Evidence motivating this plan

  • Scope reduction alone is insufficient. Recent SHA3/minimax diagnostics that skipped only clock/reset in Final did not close the remaining signals. Many failures lost the planned tree and fell back to expensive whole-net search. The proposed step 1 applies its scope and resource contract throughout the flow.
  • Reserve intervals and access, not just whole tracks. On fully routed OpenROAD SHA3, 2,628 of 2,880 occupied analysis tiles share M3 track positions between destination and foreign metal. A sampled tile uses 22 destination + 39 foreign − 9 shared = 52 of 60 raw positions, with disjoint occupied intervals.
  • Upper arrivals need connected lower-layer access. Twelve random SHA3 tiles contain 36 observed pin/upper-layer connection pairs with complete via footprints inside the tile: 30 from M4, five from M5 and one from M6. All selected paths stagger via coordinates. These are successful-route examples, not minimum reserves; two reach pins of cells whose centers lie in neighboring tiles.

The SHA3 study used native-sized analysis tiles over unchanged OpenROAD final geometry, preserving clock NDR and PG. It did not demonstrate native plan/local/Final closure.
