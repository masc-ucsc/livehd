2l-legroute Native routing closure in two steps

Plan and local pass. Final does not close cellboxes. Current work: per-class cellbox reservations (milestone 2), iterated by agents.

Goal. Make LiveHD legalize and route without requiring OpenROAD. Joint regional SAT placement and routing should reach at least the density OpenROAD can route, with substantially lower total runtime and comparable routed timing: WNS within about 4%, then TNS, wire length and vias. OpenROAD supplies comparison results and known routable references. The production flow remains native.

Immediate objective. Make legroute.final close ordinary connections through a consistent plan → local → final handoff. Build cellboxes, predict their individual routing reservations, find a feasible coarse placement and route, materialize routes to box edges, then move cells inside each box to finish its connections. This page defines the proposed work and the iteration loop; run logs and superseded experiments belong in the git history of this page and of pass/place/README.md.

Where the flow stands and what blocks it

Working. legroute.plan admits a placement with a connected global route witness; legroute.local moves cellboxes among their admitted tiles, picks regional routes, and L5 assigns layers and tracks. Both pass their checks and the committed metal reaches the cellbox tiles.

Failing. legroute.final cannot find legal in-box routes for the box's connections, internal and external alike, even though every incident net has metal at the tile. The box's own metal supply is exhausted: its internal nets and the stubs from its pins to the arriving metal need more tracks than were held back for it.

Root cause. The reservation is one uniform allowance for every box. box_reserve (default M2:1,M3:1) subtracts the same track count from every tile of each layer and records it as the tile's cellbox_budget; build_boxes only caps a box's signal pins against the horizontal tracks over the tile (its pin_limit). Nothing in that budget depends on how many internal nets the box has, how its pins are arranged, or how its cells can move. One count is too small for most boxes that have internal nets; raising it for everyone takes tracks from the global route and breaks plan admission. Both failure modes were observed; neither is a usable setting.

Position. There is no solution in hand. The path is known: size the reservation by cellbox class so a box routes on the first attempt almost always, keep each reservation as small as that allows, shape boxes at construction time so cheap reservations suffice, and repair the rare failure by moving cells rather than by over-reserving everywhere. Agents iterate on this loop; the sections below define it.

Reservation by cellbox class

Three objectives, in this priority order. They are the acceptance criteria for every iteration.

  1. Rare failure. Find the smallest set of constraints under which a box fails its first Final attempt rarely. The target is about 99% first-attempt completion per class on held-out boxes, not 100%: a reservation that never fails is too conservative and its cost shows up as global overflow.
  2. Small reservations. Among constraint sets reaching the target, take the one that holds back the fewest tracks. Measure the cost as reserved tracks over usable tracks per layer, and as the change in plan admission (overflow edges, density) on the same design.
  3. Easy shapes. Constrain cellbox construction so the cheap reservation is the common case. A box whose internal nets each live on a single row of the tile needs one horizontal track per internal net, shareable along the row when intervals are disjoint. Prefer building such boxes over predicting demand for awkward ones.

Class features. Classify a box after grouping, before any reservation, from data the builder already has: member count and total sites, one- or two-row members, number of internal nets and the rows their pins occupy, external pin count and multiplicity per net, pin shapes and their accessible layers, box fill, and the arrival layers and sides local commits for its external nets. The class key should be coarse (a handful of buckets per feature) so a table of per-class reservations can be learned from a few hundred boxes and read by a person.

Constraint form. A reservation is per box and per layer: a track count on the box layer and on each routed layer over the tile, plus the orientation rule below. It is derived from the class, not from a global constant. The uniform box_reserve remains only as the calibration control. The demand model stays simple: internal nets on one row cost one horizontal track each unless their intervals can share; internal nets spanning both rows cost a horizontal track per row plus a vertical link; each external pin costs a stub from its arrival to the pin, whose layer chain follows the actual arrival layer and pin access (staggered vias for upper arrivals). Add a small per-class margin and nothing else.

Share positions when intervals permit. Different nets can use different lengths of the same track subject to spacing rules. Protect the required landing/link footprints rather than dedicating a whole track to every destination. Candidate access windows must be mutually compatible across the box's connections, not just individually reachable. Deduct PG and fixed obstacles once; preserve layer direction, width, spacing, cut rules and any active nondefault routing rules in every pass.

Shared contract. Store stable box membership/version, class, legal placement domain, participating pins/nets, per-layer reservation, candidate edge-port/access domains and selected geometry. Plan, local/L5 and Final consume the same resolved contract and occupancy accounting. Moving a box re-evaluates it against the destination tile; changing membership, cell types, port choices or arrival layers invalidates affected demand and access estimates. Internal nets consume real resources even though they do not cross a box boundary. Recorded OpenROAD track usage is a successful reference footprint, not the minimum number of extra tracks Final needs.

Option: vertical arrivals, one horizontal track inside

A candidate rule to evaluate early, because it changes both the reservation and the box builder. External nets should arrive at the box on the vertical layer and land on a horizontal track inside the box; cells then move horizontally along the row to meet that track.

Why. Cells inside a box move only along the row. A horizontal track inside the box runs the whole box width, so a pin can meet it from any horizontal position: the placement freedom and the track are aligned. One horizontal track can therefore serve several cells of the same net, and several nets on disjoint intervals, with the arrival landing anywhere along it. The opposite arrangement, a horizontal arrival that turns vertical inside the box to reach a pin, pins the cell to the few vertical track positions that cross a cell (a cell a few sites wide is crossed by only about two vertical tracks) and consumes one of the scarce horizontal positions across the entire row width for a single connection.

What to measure. Per technology, count the horizontal track positions over a tile row after PG rails and pin shapes and the vertical positions across typical cell and box widths; confirm the asymmetry before relying on it. Then compare, on the same boxes, first-attempt completion and reserved tracks with and without the rule. If it holds, plan and local prefer vertical destination branches into boxes, the per-class reservation counts one horizontal internal track per net (shared when intervals permit) plus vertical landings, and the builder prefers box widths that leave enough vertical landing positions.

Constrain cellbox construction so reservations stay cheap

  • Single-row internals. Prefer groupings where each internal net's pins fit in one row of the tile. A net that must span both rows is allowed but counts as the expensive class; the builder should not create it when a single-row alternative exists.
  • Bound internal nets per box by the horizontal tracks a row can supply after PG, pin shapes and the arrivals; today's pin_limit bounds signal pins only, which is the wrong quantity.
  • Leave room to move. Keep box_fill low enough that cells can shift and reorder along the row; a full row removes the freedom the horizontal track relies on.
  • Width and landing positions. Choose box widths that admit at least as many vertical landing positions as the box has distinct external nets, counting interval sharing from above and below.
  • Keep two-row cells rare per box and place them first; they block vertical positions in both rows.
  • Report the class mix after every build: boxes per class, expected reservation per class, and the boxes the builder could not shape into a cheap class with the reason.

Repair ladder when a box still fails

Failures are expected at about the 1% level by design. The ladder keeps them local and never answers a local failure with a global reservation increase.

  1. In-box retry. Different cell order, shifts and legal orientations; different access window or landing position within the box's own reservation. Bounded budget per box.
  2. Move one cell to a neighbor box. Allowed when the neighbor's tile has room, its reservation absorbs the extra pins (re-evaluate its class), and every affected net's committed metal still reaches a supported port of both boxes. This changes box membership, so both contracts are re-versioned and their Final results are discarded.
  3. Evict and rerun local. Move the cells that cannot be served out of the box to a free tile or a neighbor and rerun legroute.local for the affected region with the updated boxes and reservations. Neighbors whose interfaces change are invalidated transactionally, as in local's own repair.
  4. Return to plan only when the region cannot absorb the change: regroup, recover placement, rerun FastRoute. A whole-net maze fallback stays a diagnostic escape; routinely losing the planned tree is a handoff bug, not something to hide behind larger global search budgets.

Every rung records the box, class, the failing pin or join, layer, blocker ownership and rule, the alternatives tried and the measured shortfall. Distinguish budget exhaustion, exhaustion of the represented candidate graph, malformed geometry and physical conflicts. These records feed the next calibration round.

The iteration loop for agents

Each round is self-contained and ends with a measurement table that is committed with the change. Do not move to the next round on an unmeasured hunch.

  1. Collect failing and passing boxes. Run step-1 Final with final_box_dump on LFSR and SHA3; keep the per-box report (members, budgets per layer, phases, failed nets) and the dumped boxes.
  2. Find each box's minimum. Drive tools/legroute_box_probe over each dumped box with a shrinking track menu per layer to find the smallest menu under which it still routes, with the orientation rule on and off. This is the ground truth for the class table.
  3. Fit the class table. Bucket by the class features, take the per-class quantile that meets the first-attempt target, and record the margin. Report the cost of that table versus the uniform control.
  4. Replace the uniform budget. Make the builder emit the per-box reservation, make the grid charge it per tile from the box that sits there (instead of the constant in box_reserve), and make FastRoute, local/L5 and Final read the same numbers. Keep box_reserve as the control.
  5. Rerun plan → local → Final on the same designs. Report: boxes per class, first-attempt and repaired completion per class, reserved-track cost per layer, plan admission (overflow edges, density), L5 allowance shortfall, Final failure reasons, and elapsed time per pass.
  6. Adjust one thing. Change the class features, the builder constraints or the orientation rule, not several at once, and repeat. Retain failing boxes as fixtures for the next round.

Calibration target. At least 99% first-attempt cellbox completion on held-out samples covering different designs, pin/topology classes and densities, with small reservations. Measure failure tails by class as well as the aggregate. This is a predictor target to validate, not an achieved guarantee: every remaining failure still needs repair before a design passes. An aggregate 99% box rate is insufficient for design acceptance.

Plan: placement, cellboxes and FastRoute iterate together

  1. Prepare the physical frame. Load the scope manifest, rows/sites, macros, PG, pin geometry and technology constraints. Keep the DREAMPlace/DREAMGoal session alive for timing-aware recovery.
  2. Build cellboxes and reservations. Group the current cells under the construction constraints above, check box fit, classify, and look up the per-class reservation. Provide plausible edge-port domains rather than committing early to an arbitrary pin position.
  3. Route the current candidate. Use native FastRoute-derived routing on the current box pose and port domains, with the per-box reservations reflected in directional, layer-aware capacity. Return connected per-net corridors, branches, attachments, arrival layers, edge loads and localized failures. FastRoute guides placement; the placer or a bounded cell-movement heuristic changes the pose.
  4. Repair and repeat. Localized pressure triggers targeted cell movement, spacing or inflation. Broad pressure triggers wider spreading or permitted core-area growth. Run at least 20–50 meaningful global-placement steps with timing feedback per recovery slice; use 50 as the initial evaluation setting. Release region IO constraints for broad redistribution, and retain only compatible ones for localized repair. Rebuild affected cellboxes when regrouping is beneficial, recompute reservations, and rerun FastRoute after placement, membership, area or resource changes. Area growth beyond a configured threshold also reruns macro_plan; smaller changes still require macro-fit checks.
  5. Admit and partition. Require every participating global route to be resolved, every edge within capacity, and cell/routing density at or below the default 0.90 ceiling. Screen box fit, access support and region size before SAT. Any failing global edge blocks admission; an average cannot hide it. The ceiling is an admission policy, not a routing proof or a second deduction of the same reserve. Only then cut connected regions along the routed trees and seed their interface domains.

Regions may be nonrectangular connected meshes. Penalize critical crossings while allowing necessary cuts; size regions for short useful searches. Preserve the route witness through refinement while allowing bounded alternatives inside its corridors. A cellbox edge port terminates a connection at a box; a region virtual IO joins two regional solves. Both carry stable net identity, layer/geometry or candidate interval, access support and dependency versions.

Local: movable boxes, real routes and supported edge ports

  1. Solve the hardest regions first. SAT jointly moves cellboxes among their admitted tiles and selects connected routes inside plan's corridors. Preserve the freedom to arrange member cells later inside their boxes; regrouping belongs to plan.
  2. Respect the reservation while choosing metal. Materialize and check physical track intervals, layer transitions and landing footprints. Foreign transit may cross a box only through allowed space. A destination branch stops at a cellbox edge port with a compatible reserved path to its pins, on the preferred arrival layer when the orientation rule is active. A mixed branch may both cross and serve the box. Layer assignment cannot consume access space promised to Final.
  3. Repair movable interfaces transactionally. First solve with the current region pins; if needed, jointly move eligible pins and boxes. Send new interfaces to unsolved neighbors and invalidate all affected solved neighbors. Re-solve the dependent neighborhood and restore poses, pins, metal and reservations together if the repair fails. Box-edge port changes also invalidate their access contracts.
  4. Validate the handoff. Independently check decoded connectivity, capacities, physical occupancy and mutual access support before committing regional routes. Return named blockers to plan when bounded local repair cannot resolve them.

Keep iterative regional feasibility attempts, including model construction and retries, below 60 seconds; start with a roughly 10-second probe. About 10 million clauses is a soft complexity goal, not a hard cutoff. Record SAT, UNSAT, UNKNOWN and model errors separately. A timeout does not establish unroutability and does not, by itself, justify area growth.

Final: move cells to complete the box

  1. Search placement and access together. Within the box's legal rows and sites, try cell shifts, reordering and legal orientations to bring pins near their assigned edge ports and usable tracks. Use a small bounded joint search; exhaustive arrangements are useful for tiny boxes and calibration. Optimize successful connection first, then timing and wire cost.
  2. Complete internal and external wiring. Extend the arriving destination metal toward the chosen pin positions, reuse connected own-net pieces, and add the needed short wires and vias, including staggered layer transitions. Route every participating internal net under the same reservation. Keep foreign crossings and committed boundary geometry valid while moving cells.
  3. Validate and commit. Check cell legality, PG/obstruction clearance, full metal/via rules, same-net physical unions and complete connectivity before committing a box's contribution. Revalidate the assembled net across all boxes and interfaces. Pending taps that later roll back are not successful connections.
  4. Fail through the repair ladder with the precise record defined above.

Evidence motivating this plan

  • Uniform reservations do not close boxes. With plan and local passing, step-1 Final fails boxes whose internal nets and pin stubs exceed the one-track-per-layer allowance, while a larger uniform allowance removes global capacity and fails plan admission. The reservation has to depend on the box.
  • Scope reduction alone is insufficient. Earlier SHA3/minimax diagnostics that skipped only clock/reset in Final did not close the remaining signals. Many failures lost the planned tree and fell back to expensive whole-net search. Step 1 applies its scope and resource contract throughout the flow.
  • Reserve intervals and access, not just whole tracks. On fully routed OpenROAD SHA3, 2,628 of 2,880 occupied analysis tiles share M3 track positions between destination and foreign metal. A sampled tile uses 22 destination + 39 foreign − 9 shared = 52 of 60 raw positions, with disjoint occupied intervals.
  • Upper arrivals need connected lower-layer access. Twelve random SHA3 tiles contain 36 observed pin/upper-layer connection pairs with complete via footprints inside the tile: 30 from M4, five from M5 and one from M6. All selected paths stagger via coordinates. These are successful-route examples, not minimum reserves; two reach pins of cells whose centers lie in neighboring tiles.

The SHA3 study used native-sized analysis tiles over unchanged OpenROAD final geometry, preserving clock NDR and PG. It did not demonstrate native plan/local/Final closure. Its artifacts are tagged sha3_final_cellbox_roles_20261104. The next action is round 1 of the iteration loop.
