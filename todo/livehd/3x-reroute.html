3x-reroute Aligned pre-CTS setup and partial pre-routing before the OpenROAD handoff

Move the physical preparation that OpenROAD performs between the LiveHD DEF import and CTS into LiveHD, so the handoff DEF is already a complete 3_place-equivalent: cut rows, tap and endcap cells, routing tracks, tie cells, and the power grid itself, generated from a built-in per-technology profile (pg_asap7, pg_sky130, …) rather than a configurable script. On that aligned frame, LiveHD then routes a bounded class of nets itself and hands them over as FIXED wiring. OpenROAD keeps everything that needs a propagated clock or real wires: CTS, post-CTS repair, global/detailed routing of the remaining nets, power-grid checking and signoff.

Plan ruled — not implemented; all rulings Q1–Q11 decided 2026-10-23 Drafted 2026-10-23. Ruling 2026-10-23: LiveHD builds the power grid from a hardcoded generator plus per-technology profiles (see Power grid profiles). Everything here is opt-in; default handoff behavior does not change until the LiteEth evaluation (X6) passes.

Depends on

Legal final placement from 3p-placement and the final legalization of 3s-terminal-legalize (nothing moves after pre-routing). The track-capacity model of 3r-route is reused for the pre-route occupancy grid. The OpenROAD inspection issues in 2p-plan (local ties, IO/clock pins) must not be regressed. The measurement harness is the frozen repros/route_compare_20261019 flow (handoff.tcl, run.py, ORFS cts.tcl → final_report.tcl).

Why

  • The handoff re-legalizes. Today handoff.tcl runs make_tracks, tapcell and pdngen on the imported DEF, then calls detailed_placement because taps and row cuts overlap otherwise legal LiveHD cells. handoff_cleanup.json records that displacement separately, but it still perturbs the placement LiveHD closed timing on, and it makes any LiveHD wire impossible: a moved cell disconnects from its wire. Alignment is the precondition for pre-routing, and it is worth having on its own (zero handoff displacement).
  • LiveHD decisions do not survive the router. Steiner repair topologies, bus ordering and pin alignment are placement-side hints; FastRoute and TritonRoute are free to ignore them. A wire LiveHD owns keeps the decision.
  • Routing runtime. On the LiteEth pair the native arm spends 1,050 s in final routing versus 730 s for the OpenROAD arm. Most nets in these designs are short two- or three-pin nets. Pre-routing them removes work from global and detailed routing and turns their RC from an estimate into a fact for LiveHD's own timing.
  • OpenROAD already supports the contract. A DEF net may carry + FIXED wiring; the global router skips any net that already has wires and applies the wire shapes as obstructions (findNetsObstructions); TritonRoute never rips up a FIXED net and uses its shapes as fixed obstacles; the resizer honors dont_touch on nets and instances; detailed placement skips FIRM/LOCKED instances. No OpenROAD change is required for phase 1.

Facts to confirm first (X0)

  • Tap pattern. Reproduce OpenROAD's tapcell -distance 25 with the asap7 tap/endcap master and MACRO_ROWS_HALO row cuts bit-identically: same row segments, same site indices, same orientations. Verify by running tapcell on a LiveHD DEF without taps and diffing the component list against LiveHD's own insertion on three designs (LFSR, LiteEth, CNN). Any mismatch blocks X1; do not approximate.
  • Which router paths key on wire type. FastRoute skips a net with any wire (!net->hasWires()) and obstructs on any wire; but isDetailedRouted() requires type ROUTED, and the CUGR demand adoption and updateEdgesUsage use that predicate. Confirm the ORFS default (FastRoute, no CUGR) treats FIXED wires correctly in global_route, -start/-end_incremental and repair_antennas. If any path misbehaves, the fallback is to emit ROUTED wires plus dont_touch and accept that TritonRoute may rip them up only when it has guides, which it will not have.
  • Fixed-versus-fixed checking. TritonRoute's DRC skips pairs where both shapes are fixed, and the power grid is special-net wiring. A short between a LiveHD wire and a grid stripe would therefore be silent downstream. Since both now come from LiveHD, the X2 oracle checks them against each other; still confirm whether check_drc reports fixed-vs-special violations so X5 knows what the independent check covers.
  • Grid import. Confirm that read_def of routed SPECIALNETS with DEF VIAS (via arrays from VIARULE GENERATE) reproduces pdngen's shapes on LFSR, that the handoff's existing_pdn branch then connects every cell and macro PG pin (check_power_grid clean), and that analyze_power_grid matches the pdngen arm within noise.
  • Connectivity of fixed nets. TritonRoute does not verify that a FIXED net actually touches all its pins. Confirm what design_is_routed and check_antennas do with such a net, and whether OpenRCX extracts it normally (it should: extraction walks dbWire).
  • Parasitics before extraction. estimate_parasitics -global_routing uses guides. Confirm what RC a wired, guide-less net gets in the CTS and GRT repair stages (placement estimate, wire-based, or zero), because post-CTS repair_timing ranks paths through those nets with it.
  • Vias in DEF. + FIXED paths name vias. Confirm whether the asap7 technology LEF provides fixed VIA definitions usable from DEF, or whether LiveHD must emit a DEF VIAS section generated from VIARULE GENERATE (TritonRoute generates its own via masters; the handoff must not depend on their names).
  • Handoff legalization and locked cells. Confirm that detailed_placement in handoff.tcl, cts.tcl and global_route.tcl never moves a FIRM instance, and that check_placement accepts a FIRM standard cell on a cut row.

Rulings (all decided 2026-10-23)

#QuestionProposed default
Q1Where does the power-grid geometry come from before pre-routing? Ruled 2026-10-23: LiveHD builds the grid. One hardcoded generator (followpin rails, straight stripes, via arrays at intersections, macro avoidance with halo, macro PG connection) driven by a built-in per-technology profile; no Tcl, no reservation parser. Anything outside that topology (rings, power switches, multiple voltage domains, sroute) is rejected explicitly and disables the grid and pre-routing for that platform. Details under Power grid profiles.
Q2Does LiveHD insert real tap/endcap components or only reserve their sites? Ruled 2026-10-23: insert real FIXED components with the platform tap master; the handoff skips TAPCELL_TCL when instances of that master already exist. Reserving sites was rejected: it still requires the exact pattern and leaves the re-legalization step in place.
Q3Wire type of pre-routed nets. Ruled 2026-10-23: FIXED only in phase 1. LiveHD owns correctness of those nets end to end. A ROUTED-plus-guides mode (OpenROAD may repair them) stays phase 2.
Q4Which nets are eligible? Ruled 2026-10-23: every signal net whose pins are all on standard cells is eligible, with no pin-count or length limit. Excluded: nets with a macro pin or an IO terminal, clock, reset, tie nets, and nets inside a dont_touch cone. Eligibility is not a promise to route: the pre-router tries nets shortest-first and any net it cannot route within its budget, its rule oracle, the Q7 headroom or the Q5 margins stays with OpenROAD. preroute.max_rows/max_sites remain as optional caps, default unlimited. Multi-pin nets use a FLUTE Steiner topology; long nets get LiveHD's own repeaters (existing Steiner repair) locked with them. Channel nets (Q11) remain phase 2 only because they need the channel layers.
Q5Timing margin for eligibility, since a dont_touch net can never receive a post-CTS hold or setup fix. Ruled 2026-10-23: LiveHD guarantees the timing of every pre-routed net; OpenROAD must never add buffers to or resize along one. Exclude a net unless LiveHD's pre-CTS placement-RC timing shows setup slack ≥ preroute.setup_margin (default 20 ps) and hold slack ≥ preroute.hold_margin (default: two HOLD_BUF_CELL delays at nominal load, read from Liberty) on every path through it. Clock skew after CTS is unknown pre-CTS; the margins are the price of locking, and a pre-routed net that later fails timing is a LiveHD mistake to be fixed in LiveHD, not patched downstream. The LiteEth evaluation (X6) may tune the defaults.
Q6Layers. Ruled 2026-10-23: any routing layer the technology allows for signals is fair game for a dont_touch pre-route, within the platform's signal layer range (asap7: M2–M7). No LiveHD-side layer cap. The pre-router picks layers by cost: lower layers for short nets, upper layers for long nets where the RC pays for the vias, and the Q7 headroom bound applies per layer and gcell so clock layers (MIN_CLK_ROUTING_LAYER and up) and IO pin layers keep room for CTS and OpenROAD's router. preroute.layers=M2-M7 remains as an optional restriction for experiments, default the full signal range.
Q7Capacity headroom left for OpenROAD. Ruled 2026-10-23: leaving enough room for TritonRoute is LiveHD's responsibility and LiveHD's fault when it fails; there is no downstream safety net and no contract that OpenROAD must succeed regardless. LiveHD must therefore be conservative: pre-routes may occupy at most preroute.capacity (default 0.30) of the usable tracks of any gcell on each layer, counted after grid and macro obstructions, and the RUDY/RSMT demand of the remaining nets in that gcell must stay below the layer capacity minus the pre-routed share. Exceeding either bound rejects the net, never reroutes a neighbour. A routed-flow failure (overflow, unrouted nets, DRC) in the pre-route arm is reported as a LiveHD defect and tightens the default, not as an OpenROAD limitation.
Q8Lock policy. Ruled 2026-10-23: the DEF is self-describing; there is no sidecar. Every instance touched by a pre-routed net is written as a FIXED component (imports as FIRM, which detailed placement never moves). The handoff derives the rest: every net carrying FIXED wiring is set_dont_touch before any repair step, and the handoff fails if such a net has an instance that is not FIRM. The routers need no marks (FastRoute skips wired nets and obstructs on them; TritonRoute never rips up FIXED wiring); the derived dont_touch exists solely for the resizer, which is otherwise blind to wires and would split or resize the net silently. No other instance changes status.
Q9Defaults and naming. Ruled 2026-10-23: synth.place.handoff.taps=false|true, synth.place.pg=off|auto|asap7|sky130|… (off disables the grid and therefore pre-routing; auto picks the profile from the technology LEF and errors when none matches), flat synth.place.pg.* overrides, synth.place.preroute=false|true and the synth.place.preroute.* knobs above. All off by default. Placement cache identity includes every one of them; a pre-route is cached with the final placement it belongs to.
Q11Signal channels through power-grid metal. Ruled 2026-10-23 (intent): the generator can give up part of the upper-layer power metal to long communication signals. Two patch styles, both profile data: slot skip (a stripe position left empty over an extent, its via stacks dropped) and gap (a stripe cut over a short length with legal end spacing and minimum area). A patch may never isolate a stripe island (check_power_grid clean) and its IR-drop cost against the unpatched grid is reported and bounded by pg.channel.max_ir_drop. The concept is technology-independent; the legal geometry (layers, rules) comes from the profile and the LEF. Reference profiles ship with channels off so Q10 parity holds.
Q10Must a profile reproduce the ORFS reference grid exactly? Ruled 2026-10-23: yes for every technology that has an ORFS reference arm. Each built-in profile is transcribed once from the platform's pdn file with the ORFS commit recorded, and X5 diffs its shapes against pdngen output on one design. Otherwise a LiveHD-versus-OpenROAD comparison measures a grid difference.

Power grid profiles

The generator is one algorithm; technologies differ only in data. A profile is a C++ struct registered by name (pg_asap7, pg_sky130), selected by synth.place.pg or auto-detected from the technology LEF. Whatever the LEF already states is derived, not configured: followpin layer and width from the standard cell PG pin geometry, rail pitch from the site height, via arrays from VIARULE GENERATE, macro PG pins from the macro LEF. The profile holds only what cannot be derived.

  • Ruled 2026-10-23: flat overrides use the same field names (synth.place.pg.stripe=M5:0.12:0.072:5.4:0.300, repeatable) so a pitch can be tried without a rebuild. Plain options only; no scripting layer and no conditional or computed configuration.
  • Ruled 2026-10-23: grid validation stays in OpenROAD. check_power_grid (connectivity) and analyze_power_grid (IR drop) are the acceptance checks; LiveHD does not implement its own IR solver or connectivity checker beyond the unit tests of the generator.
  • A profile may declare unsupported features it knows the reference platform uses (rings, switches); selecting it then errors with the reason instead of generating a different grid.
  • Stripe placement avoids macros and memory proxies by the halo, extends to the core boundary, and snaps to the layer's track grid; followpins are cut at row cuts. Via arrays are sized by the intersection and the VIARULE limits, like pdngen's default.
  • Grid shapes enter the same occupancy grid the pre-router uses (X3), so a signal/grid short is a LiveHD-side failure caught by the X2 oracle.
  • Channels (Q11) are generated with the grid, so their freed tracks are visible to the pre-router, to the RUDY capacity model and, if LiveHD routes nothing there, to OpenROAD's router. A channel is therefore measurable on its own: IR-drop cost versus routed timing of the long nets that end up in it.

Subtasks

  • X0 — Facts and baselines.
    • Resolve every item in Facts to confirm first with a reproducible script under repros/; record the OpenROAD commit used.
    • Baseline the three designs through the frozen harness unchanged: handoff displacement, GRT overflow, DRT runtime and DRC count, routed wire and via count per layer, extracted setup/hold WNS/TNS. These are the numbers X6 compares against.
    • Net census per design: all-standard-cell signal nets by pin count and HPWL bucket, and the fraction remaining after the Q5 timing margins. This sets the expected pre-routed share and tells which buckets matter for the router budget.
  • X1 — Aligned pre-CTS setup in LiveHD.
    • Row cuts: cut rows around fixed macros and memory proxies with the platform halo, producing the same row segments OpenROAD's tapcell would. Emit cut rows in the DEF instead of full rows.
    • Taps and endcaps: insert FIXED components per the X0-verified pattern after final legalization; they participate in density, overlap and site checks like any fixed object. Name them so write_verilog -remove_cells [find_physical_only_masters] drops them as today.
    • Tracks: audit that LiveHD's TRACKS records equal OpenROAD's make_tracks output on the same die (origin rounding, M2 multi-offset records). Fix the importer if not.
    • Power grid (Q1): pass/place/power_grid.cpp with the generator, the profile registry (pg_asap7 first, pg_sky130 when a sky130 arm exists) and auto-detection; emits routed SPECIALNETS plus DEF VIAS, records synth/place/power_grid.json (per-layer length, via counts, macros avoided, unconnected PG pins = must be zero). Unit tests: rail count equals row count plus one, stripe positions against the profile arithmetic, via arrays within VIARULE limits, macro halo respected, and a shape diff against a saved pdngen DEF for LFSR.
    • Channels (Q11): implement skip first, gap second; both reject any configuration that would leave a stripe segment below minimum area or disconnect a stripe. Record removed stripe length, dropped via stacks and the freed track count per layer in power_grid.json. Evaluate channels alone before any channel pre-route: same placement, channels off versus on, OpenROAD routes everything; report IR drop, long-net slack and routed wire length.
    • Handoff script: skip TAPCELL_TCL when the tap master is already instantiated; mark every net with FIXED wiring dont_touch and fail if one of its instances is not FIRM; the existing existing_pdn branch already skips pdngen when special wires are present, so only add check_power_grid after global_connect; keep the detailed_placement fallback but fail the run if it moves anything when taps were supplied. Acceptance: handoff_cleanup.json reports zero moved instances on all three designs, check_placement and check_power_grid pass.
  • X2 — Pin access and rule oracle.
    • Extend the LEF reader (inou/lefdef/physical.cpp, Layer) with the rule subset the pre-router needs: spacing (table and EOL), minimum area, minimum step, via cut size/spacing/enclosure, and VIARULE GENERATE. Anything the oracle cannot model makes the net ineligible.
    • Pin access: for each master pin on M1, enumerate on-track M2 crossings whose V1 enclosure and spacing to neighbouring pin shapes are satisfied without any assumption about the neighbouring cell. Pins with no such point are ineligible. Compare the resulting access points against OpenROAD pin_access on the same instances and report the agreement rate; LiveHD's set must be a subset.
    • Unit tests on synthetic two-cell cases per rule, plus a fuzz that routes random eligible pairs and asserts the oracle against a KLayout or check_drc run (whichever X0 shows reports fixed shapes).
  • X3 — Pre-router.
    • Occupancy grid per layer at track granularity, seeded with macro obstructions, power-grid shapes (X1), tap cells and pin shapes; gcell capacity accounting shared with the 3r-route RUDY model.
    • Candidate ordering: shortest first, then by slack margin descending, so the easy nets go in before contention. Each net gets a bounded search: L and Z patterns on M2/M3 with on-track vias for two-pin nets; a FLUTE Steiner topology routed edge by edge for multi-pin nets; then a bounded maze within the net's expanded box. Long nets are first repeated by the existing Steiner repair using the pre-route layer RC, and the repeaters are placed on free legal sites and locked. Failure at any step leaves the whole net to OpenROAD and removes its repeaters; nothing already routed is ever ripped up.
    • Per-net search budget (preroute.budget_ms) and a whole-stage deadline, so the pre-router cannot dominate placement runtime on large designs; the census in X0 and preroute.json report the share routed versus attempted by pin count and length bucket.
    • Every accepted route is checked by the X2 oracle against all existing shapes, the Q7 headroom bounds, and the Q5 margins recomputed with the wire's actual RC through the existing physical RC and OpenTimer session.
    • Outputs: synth/place/preroute.json (counts, per-layer length, vias, rejections by reason, runtime) and the lock list.
  • X4 — Emission.
    • DEF writer: + FIXED paths per net, a VIAS section if X0 requires one, FIRM status on locked components, cut rows and tap components; DEF re-read round-trips bit-identically through read_def.
    • No sidecar (Q8): the DEF carries FIXED components and FIXED wiring; the handoff derives dont_touch from the wiring and verifies the component status.
    • Placement QoR gains a preroute member; the cache stores the pre-route with the placement and replays it on a hit.
  • X5 — Independent validation.
    • OpenROAD Tcl check run right after import: every pre-routed net's wire touches every one of its pins (open check), and no wire shape intersects a special-net shape or another net's wire within spacing (short check), since TritonRoute will not report either. Zero tolerance.
    • Grid acceptance: check_power_grid clean for every PG net; analyze_power_grid worst IR drop within noise of the pdngen arm on the same placement; one-time shape diff of the LiveHD grid against pdngen for each built-in profile (Q10), repeated whenever the profile or the ORFS reference commit changes.
    • Post-route: check_drc plus the ORFS DRC report must be zero, all nets routed, and the extracted netlist must carry the pre-routed wires unchanged (hash the FIXED paths before and after).
  • X6 — Evaluation and acceptance.
    • LiteEth first (routes cleanly today), then CNN, then Eyeriss, through the frozen harness with identical netlist, frame, SDC and downstream policy. Arms: baseline handoff; aligned handoff without pre-routing; aligned handoff with pre-routing.
    • Accept alignment alone when handoff displacement is zero and all other metrics are within noise. Accept pre-routing when: zero DRC, all nets routed, extracted setup and hold WNS/TNS not worse than the aligned arm, and GRT+DRT wall time or total routed wire length improves. Report pre-routed share, per-layer usage, GRT overflow and runtime of the pre-router itself. A timing loss on any design rejects the default-on proposal, not the opt-in feature.
  • X7 — Phase 2 (not scheduled).
    • Critical and bus nets: pre-route the Steiner-repaired and bus-ordered nets with M4 escapes, with an explicit measurement of what locking them costs in post-CTS repair.
    • Channel nets: select long communication nets (length above preroute.channel.min_length, typically macro-to-macro or block-to-block links), size and repeat them with the existing Steiner repair on the channel layer's RC, lock the repeaters, and route them as FIXED wide wires inside Q11 channels with escapes down to the pins. Acceptance adds: channel-net slack versus the OpenROAD-routed arm, and IR drop within the Q11 bound.
    • ROUTED wires plus LiveHD-written guides, so OpenROAD may incrementally repair a pre-routed net instead of being forbidden to.
    • Clock pre-routing requires a LiveHD CTS and is out of scope for this task.

Design notes

  • Order inside LiveHD is fixed: final legalization → pre-CTS repair → tap insertion → power grid → pre-routing → DEF. Nothing may move a cell after pre-routing; any later ECO invalidates the pre-route and must drop it (the cache identity makes this automatic).
  • A pre-routed net is a contract, not a hint. The handoff must make it impossible for OpenROAD to half-honor it: locked cells, derived dont_touch nets, and a validation step that fails the run instead of warning. The information lives only in the DEF, so nothing can drift out of sync.
  • The grid is deliberately not configurable beyond its profile fields. pdngen is general because it serves every ORFS platform; this generator serves one flow with one topology. A platform that needs more disables the grid and pre-routing rather than getting an approximation.
  • Grid shapes and signal pre-routes share one occupancy grid and one rule oracle, which is what makes the fixed-versus-fixed blind spot of the downstream DRC acceptable.
  • Pre-routing is the last step, so its wires are the only ones whose RC LiveHD knows exactly. Feeding that RC back into the final timing snapshot improves the pre-CTS/routed correlation measured in 3r-route even when no QoR is gained.
  • Channels trade IR drop for wide, low-resistance signal metal. The grid is a mesh, so a single skipped slot or gap is a local resistance increase, not a connectivity loss; the budget is design-dependent, which is why the measurement is analyze_power_grid on the actual placement rather than a rule.
  • Keep clock and reset nets, IO nets and macro-pin nets out of phase 1: clocks do not exist as trees yet, IO pins sit on M4/M5, and macro pins have their own access rules.
